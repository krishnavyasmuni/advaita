Viṣṇu Purāṇa

Book 2, Chapter 1

Descendants of Priyavrata, the eldest son of Swáyambhuva Manu: his ten sons: three adopt a religious life; the others become kings of the seven Dwípas, or isles, of the earth. Agnídhra, king of Jambu-dwípa, divides it into nine portions, which he distributes amongst his sons. Nábhi, king of the south, succeeded by Rishabha; and he by Bharata: India named after him Bhárata: his descendants reign during the Swáyambhuva Manwantara.

VP 2.1.43

एष स्वायम्भुवः सर्गो येनेदं पूरितं जगत् ।
वाराहे तु मुने कल्पे पूर्व-मन्वन्तराधिपः ॥४३॥

Under these princes, Bhárata-varsha (India) was divided into nine portions (to be hereafter particularized); and their descendants successively held possession of the country for seventy-one periods of the aggregate of the four ages (or for the reign of a Manu). This was the creation of Swáyambhuva Manu, by which the earth was peopled, when he presided over the first Manwantara, in the Kalpa of Varáha.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

A question arises: earlier you also described Uttānapāda and the others as universal sovereigns; now Priyavrata and the others are also called universal sovereigns, and they cannot have ruled at the same time. Therefore the sequence must be stated. The answer begins “of them”: the earth Bhāratī was first enjoyed by those born in the lineage of Priyavrata, and later by the descendants of Uttānapāda. The period called one Manvantara is named through seventy-one cycles of the four ages, beginning with Kṛta and Tretā; that is the meaning. The reading “with the creation called Kṛta, Tretā, and so on” is construed in the same way; the reading “seventy-one” makes the meaning clear. “This” makes it explicit: in this Varāha Kalpa, when Svāyambhuva Manu was lord of the first Manvantara, this was the creation of the kings in Priyavrata’s lineage; thereafter, beginning with Svārociṣa, came the descendants of Uttānapāda. Hence the birth of the Ādityas in the lineage of Uttānapāda from the daughter of Dakṣa Prajāpati is taught in the Vaivasvata Manvantara. With the locative reading, the meaning is that while Svāyambhuva, lord of the first Manvantara, was reigning, this creation of Priyavrata and the rest first arose from that Manu, who was a portion of Svāyambhuva. Or, “this” concludes the whole creation described earlier.
