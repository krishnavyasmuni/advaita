VP 2.11.1

VP 2.11.4

यदि सप्तगणो वारि हिममुष्णं च वर्षति । तत्किमत्र रवेर्येन वृष्टिः सूर्यादितीर्यते

If the sevenfold group produces water, snow, and heat, what is there about the Sun by which rain is said to arise from the Sun?

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

yadi saptagaṇo vāri himam uṣṇaṃ ca varṣati — if the sevenfold group produces water, snow, and heat; tat kim atra raver yena vṛṣṭiḥ sūryād itīryate — what is there about the Sun by which rain is said to arise from the Sun?

Śrīdhara

nanu ādityasya api vṛṣṭi-karma uktam eva — but it is also said that the Āditya performs the work of raining; vivasvān aṣṭabhiḥ māsaiḥ iti-ādinā — in the statement beginning “Vivasvān for eight months”; ataḥ āha yadi — therefore he says “if”; saḥ ayam sapta-gaṇaḥ — this sevenfold group; hima-uṣṇa-vāri-vṛṣṭīnām hetuḥ — is the cause of snow, heat, water, and rain; sādhāraṇyasya uktatvāt — because it is stated to be common

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

yadi saptagaṇo vāri himamuṣṇaṃ ca varṣati | tatkimatra raveryena vṛṣṭiḥ sūryāditīryate ||4||

nanv ādityasyāpi vṛṣṭikarmoktam eva vivasvān aṣṭabhir māsair ityādinā ata āha—yadi iti || so 'yaṃ saptagaṇo himoṣṇavārivṛṣṭīnāṃ hetur iti sādhāraṇyasyoktatvād ity arthaḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

नन्वादित्यस्यापि वृष्टिकर्मोक्तम् एव विवस्वानष्टभिर्मासैरित्यादिना अत आह—यदि इति ॥ सोऽयं सप्तगणो हिमोष्णवारिवृष्टीनां हेतुरिति साधारण्यस्योक्तत्वादित्यर्थः

Śrīdhara commentary.

Is it not also said that the Āditya performs the work of raining, in the statement beginning “Vivasvān for eight months”? Therefore he says “if”: the sevenfold group is stated to be the common cause of snow, heat, water, and rain.

VP 2.11.7

सर्वशक्तिः परा विष्णो ऋग्यजुःसामसंज्ञिता । सैषात्रयी तपत्यंहो जगतश्च हिनस्ति या

The entire and mighty energy of Vishnu, which is called the three Vedas, or Rich, Yajush, and Saman, is that which enlightens the world, and destroys its iniquity.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

sarvaśaktiḥ parā viṣṇo ṛgyajuḥsāmasaṃjñitā — The entire and mighty energy of Vishnu, which is called the three Vedas, or Rich; saiṣātrayī tapatyaṃho jagataśca hinasti yā — Yajush, and Saman, is that which enlightens the world, and destroys its iniquity.

Śrīdhara

yad yasmāt yā parā — because it is that supreme power; sarvārtha-prakāśikā — illuminating all objects; viṣṇoḥ trayī-rūpā śaktiḥ — Viṣṇu's power in the form of the three Vedas; sā eva ravi-rūpeṇa tapati — it burns in the form of the Sun; ahaṃ ca pāpam upāsitā satī hinasti — and, when approached, destroys sin; ataḥ prādhānyena adhikaḥ raviḥ — therefore the Sun is superior by primacy; sarvā śaktiḥ samagrā śaktiḥ — in the reading sarvā śaktiḥ, it means complete power

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

sarvaśaktiḥ parā viṣṇo ṛgyajuḥsāmasaṃjñitā | saiṣātrayī tapatyaṃho jagataśca hinasti yā ||7||

yad yasmād yā parā sarvārtha-prakāśikā viṣṇos trayī-rūpā śaktiḥ saivaiṣā ravi-rūpeṇa tapati ahaṃś ca pāpam upāsitā satī hinasti, ataḥ prādhānyenādhiko raviḥ, saiṣā trayeva vidyā tapati iti śruteḥ | sarvā śaktir iti pāṭhe sarvā śaktiḥ samagrā śaktir ity arthaḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

यद् यस्माद् या परा सर्वार्थप्रकाशिका विष्णोस्त्रयीरूपा शक्तिः सैवैषा रविरूपेण तपति अहंश्च पापमुपासिता सती हिनस्ति, अतः प्राधान्येनाधिको रविः, सैषा त्रयेव विद्या तपति इति श्रुतेः । सर्वा शक्तिरिति पाठे सर्वा शक्तिः समग्रा शक्तिरित्यर्थः

Śrīdhara commentary.

Because that supreme power of Viṣṇu, which illuminates all things and has the form of the three Vedas, itself burns as the Sun and destroys sin when approached, the Sun is superior by primacy. “This burns as the threefold knowledge” is the Vedic statement. In the reading sarvā śaktiḥ, sarvā means “the complete power.”

VP 2.11.8

VP 2.11.10

VP 2.11.26
