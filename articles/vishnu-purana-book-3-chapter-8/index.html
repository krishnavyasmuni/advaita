VP 3.8.1

भगवन्भगवान्देवः संसारविजिगीषुभिः । समाख्याहि जगन्नाथो विष्णुराराध्यते यथा
॥१॥

O Lord, O divine Lord, tell me how Lord Viṣṇu, the master of the universe, is worshipped by those who seek to overcome saṃsāra.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

bhagavanbhagavāndevaḥ saṃsāravijigīṣubhiḥ । samākhyāhi jagannātho viṣṇurārādhyate yathā ॥१॥ — bhagavan — O Lord; bhagavān devaḥ — the divine Lord; saṃsāra-vijigīṣubhiḥ — by those seeking to overcome saṃsāra; samākhyāhi — explain; jagannāthaḥ — the Lord of the universe; viṣṇuḥ ārādhyate — Viṣṇu is worshipped; yathā — how.

Śrīdhara

viṣṇorārādhanopāyo nijadharmaprasaṅgataḥ । sagaraurvīyasaṃvādaṃ navādhyāyairnirūpyate । yathā yatprakāreṇa dharmeṇa — The means of worshipping Viṣṇu through one’s own dharma will be explained over nine chapters through the dialogue between Sagara and Aurva, showing how He is worshipped through each form of dharma.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

bhagavanbhagavāndevaḥ saṃsāravijigīṣubhiḥ । samākhyāhi jagannātho viṣṇurārādhyate yathā ॥१॥

Śrīdhara

viṣṇorārādhanopāyo nijadharmaprasaṅgataḥ । sagaraurvīyasaṃvādaṃ navādhyāyairnirūpyate । yathā yatprakāreṇa dharmeṇa

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

विष्णोराराधनोपायो निजधर्मप्रसङ्गतः । सगरौर्वीयसंवादं नवाध्यायैर्निरूप्यते । यथा यत्प्रकारेण धर्मेण

Śrīdhara commentary (source range 1).

Śrīdhara explains that the means of worshipping Viṣṇu in relation to one’s own dharma is taught through the dialogue of Sagara and Aurva over nine chapters, showing the manner in which He is worshipped through dharma.

VP 3.8.9

वर्णाश्रमाचारवता पुरुषेणपरः पुमान् । विष्णुराराध्यते पन्था नान्यस्तत्तोषकारकः
॥९॥

The Supreme Person, Viṣṇu, is worshipped by a person who lives by the duties of his varṇa and āśrama; there is no other path that pleases Him.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

varṇāśramācāravatā puruṣeṇaparaḥ pumān । viṣṇurārādhyate panthā nānyastattoṣakārakaḥ ॥९॥ — varṇa-āśrama-ācāravatā puruṣeṇa — by a person observing varṇa and āśrama conduct; paraḥ pumān viṣṇuḥ — the Supreme Person Viṣṇu; ārādhyate — is worshipped; panthāḥ na anyaḥ — there is no other path; tat-toṣa-kārakaḥ — that brings His satisfaction.

Śrīdhara

varṇāśramācāravatety-adhikāri-viśeṣaṇād vedokta-tad-aviruddha-purāṇāgamādy-uktācāravān eva tatrādhikārī, na vigītācāraḥ । anyaḥ śruty-ukta-dharma-parityāgena tad-vrata-dhāraṇa-śravaṇa-kīrtanādi-rūpaḥ panthā na bhavati — Śrīdhara explains the qualification expressed by “one who follows varṇa and āśrama conduct”: the eligible person follows conduct taught by the Veda and by Purāṇic and Āgamic texts that do not contradict it, not censured conduct. Abandoning scriptural duties while merely adopting vows or devotional practices is not an alternative path.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

varṇāśramācāravatā puruṣeṇaparaḥ pumān । viṣṇurārādhyate panthā nānyastattoṣakārakaḥ ॥९॥

Śrīdhara

varṇāśramācāravatety-adhikāri-viśeṣaṇād vedokta-tad-aviruddha-purāṇāgamādy-uktācāravān eva tatrādhikārī, na vigītācāraḥ । anyaḥ śruty-ukta-dharma-parityāgena tad-vrata-dhāraṇa-śravaṇa-kīrtanādi-rūpaḥ panthā na bhavati

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

वर्णाश्रमाचारवतेत्य्-अधिकारि-विशेषणाद् वेदोक्त-तद्-अविरुद्ध-पुराणागमाद्य्-उक्ताचारवान् एव तत्राधिकारी, न विगीताचारः । अन्यः श्रुत्य्-उक्त-धर्म-परित्यागेन तद्-व्रत-धारण-श्रवण-कीर्तनादि-रूपः पन्था न भवति

Śrīdhara commentary (source range 9).

Śrīdhara explains that the qualified worshipper follows conduct taught by the Veda and by Purāṇic and Āgamic texts consistent with it, and is not one of censured conduct. No alternative path exists through abandoning scriptural duties and merely taking up vows or devotional practices.

VP 3.8.10

यजन्यज्ञान्यजत्येनं जपत्येनं जपन्नृप । निघ्नन्नन्यान्हिनस्त्येनं सर्वभूतो यतो हरिः
॥१०॥

One worships Him through sacrifice, through study, and through recitation; because Hari is the Self in every being, one also worships Him—though wrongly—when one harms another.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

yajann yajñān yajaty enaṃ japaty enaṃ japannṛpa । nighnann anyān hinasty enaṃ sarva-bhūto yato hariḥ ॥१०॥ — yajann yajñān — offering sacrifices; yajati enam — worships Him; japati enam — worships Him through recitation; japann — while reciting; nighnann anyān — striking down others; hinasti enam — harms Him; sarva-bhūtaḥ — the Self in every being; yataḥ hariḥ — because Hari is [that].

Śrīdhara

yajñān yaṣṭavyān indrādīn yajan viṣṇuṃ yajati, yaṃ kañcana japan viṣṇumeva japati, anyaṃ ghnan viṣṇumeva hanti — Varna aashrama iti | Aachaaravata iti atra aachaaragrahanam yajnyaadeh karmano api upalakshanam | Yajnyaas and other karmaas are also included in this. These are all Vishnu Aaraadhana only. Everything told in the Poorva Kaanda of Vedaas is said to be aaraadhana of Bhagavaan. Yajnyaas and other karmaas are also included in this. These are all Vishnu Aaraadhana only. Everything told in the Poorva Kaanda of Vedaas is said to be aaraadhana of Bhagavaan. Sri Vishnu Puraana, Amsha 3, Chapter 8, Shloka 10: Yajan yajnyaan yajanti enam japati enam japan nrupa | Nighnan anyaan hinasti enam sarvabhooto yato harih || Why is everything Vishnu's aaraadhana only? Whoever performs yajnya, yajnya is " yaja devapoojaayaam"; whichever god he worships, he will be worshipping Vishnu only, because " Sarvabhooto yato harih". Hari is only everything, means that he is present as antaryaami of everything; everything is His shareera; He is shareeri. Everything is Vishnu aatmaka, Bhagavadaatmaka. Whoever worships any god ultimately worships Bhagavaan only, who is antaryaami. Any japa one does, he will be doing japa of Vishnu only. If one is killing others, he will be injuring Vishnu only. Therefore, one should not cause injury to others. Everything is Vishnu only. If he is worshipping through yajnyaas, he will only be worshipping Sri Mahaa Vishnu. Any japa he is doing, ultimately, he will be only doing japa of Sri Mahaa Vishnu. If he hurts someone also, he knows that he is hurting Mahaa Vishnu only. Because Bhagavaan is there everywhere, in every being, in every devataa. He is antaryaami of everyone. Because of this idea, a mumukshu is performing all the karmaas. This is explained in the commentary. Varna aashrama dharma is to do with devataas. How can it be bhagavat aaraadhana? Sarva bhootah sarva aatma bhootah \ ye yajanti pitruun devaan braahmanaan sa hutaashanaan | Sarva bhoota antaraatmaanam vishnum eva yajanti te | He is inner self of everything. Whoever does poojaa, yajnya to devaas, pitruus, or anybody, they all will be worshipping Paramaatman only, He is antaraatman to all beings. \ Ye api anyadevataa bhaktaah yajante shraddhayaa anvitaah | Te api maam Sri Vishnu Puraana, Amsha 3

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

yajann yajñān yajaty enaṃ japaty enaṃ japannṛpa । nighnann anyān hinasty enaṃ sarva-bhūto yato hariḥ ॥१०॥

Śrīdhara

yajñān yaṣṭavyān indrādīn yajan viṣṇuṃ yajati, yaṃ kañcana japan viṣṇumeva japati, anyaṃ ghnan viṣṇumeva hanti

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

यज्ञान् यष्टव्यान् इन्द्रादीन् यजन् विष्णुं यजति, यं कञ्चन जपन् विष्णुमेव जपति, अन्यं घ्नन् विष्णुमेव हन्ति

Śrīdhara commentary (source range 10).

Śrīdhara asks how duties directed toward deities can be worship of Bhagavān. He answers that Hari is the Self of every being. The rites performed for ancestors, gods, Brāhmaṇas, and the sacred fire therefore reach Viṣṇu, the inner Self of all; even worship offered with faith to other deities is worship of Him, though performed without the proper understanding. The sacrificial and other duties included in varṇa-āśrama conduct are thus forms of Viṣṇu’s worship.

Śrīdhara now explains the rules for earning wealth. “For livelihood” means performing sacrifices for others and teaching others; “receiving gifts” means accepting appropriate gifts such as cattle. Śrīdhara explains “stone” as a stone and “another’s jewel” as a jewel belonging to someone else; the verse says that the Brāhmaṇa should regard them with equal-mindedness. Union with one’s wife during her fertile period is permitted and praised by scripture, but the statement is not a restrictive command that forbids union at other times. He then distinguishes three kinds of Vedic injunction: a vidhi creates an act not otherwise available; a niyama restricts a choice when the act is available in some ways but not others; and a parisaṃkhyā excludes alternatives when both would otherwise be possible. He illustrates these with the commands to perform Jyotiṣṭoma, to husk rice, and to eat five-clawed animals, as well as the injunction to approach one’s wife in season. In the last case, ordinary desire already supplies the possibility of union, so the scriptural statement serves to exclude other times rather than to create the act. The same logic explains why a mantra about taking the horse’s bridle is applied to the horse and not to a donkey: the contextual sign and the mantra together make the horse-application available, while the other is excluded. Thus the three defects—giving up the direct meaning, inventing another meaning, and failing to block the alternative—do not arise. Śrīdhara concludes that “approach one’s wife in season” is a parisaṃkhyā, since union is otherwise possible in both seasonal and non-seasonal periods. The warning that one who does not approach his wife after she has bathed incurs the sin of foeticide is not contradictory: it applies when desire is present but he abstains through aversion or another fault. The general point is that sexual intercourse, meat, and liquor are objects of ordinary human desire, not acts made obligatory by a command.

VP 3.8.39

क्षात्रं कर्म द्विजस्योक्तं वैश्यकर्म तथाऽऽपदि । राजन्यस्य च वैश्योक्तं शूद्रकर्म न चैतयोः
॥३९॥

In an emergency, a Brāhmaṇa may undertake Kṣatriya work, a Kṣatriya may undertake Vaiśya work, and a Vaiśya may undertake Śūdra work; these alternatives are not prescribed for the other two.

Śrīdhara

uktarītyā vaiśya-śūdra-karma-prāptau | tat-niṣedham āha | etayoḥ kṣatriya-vaiśyayoḥ | brāhmaṇasyāpi upalakṣaṇam — In an emergency, the rule permits the Vaiśya and Śūdra occupations in the stated order; the restriction concerning the two lower occupations also indicates the corresponding exclusion for the Brāhmaṇa.

Śrīdhara

uktarītyā vaiśyaśūdrakarmaprāptau taniṣedhamāha etayoḥ kṣatriyavaiśyayoḥ, brāhmaṇasyāpyupalakṣaṇam

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

उक्तरीत्या वैश्यशूद्रकर्मप्राप्तौ तनिषेधमाह एतयोः क्षत्रियवैश्ययोः, ब्राह्मणस्याप्युपलक्षणम्

Śrīdhara commentary (source range 39).

Śrīdhara explains that the verse turns to emergency duties: a Brāhmaṇa may take up Kṣatriya work, a Kṣatriya may take up Vaiśya work, and a Vaiśya may take up Śūdra work. The prohibition concerning the two higher groups marks the limits of these alternatives.

VP 3.8.40

सामर्थ्ये सति तत्त्याज्यमुभाभ्यामपि पार्थिव । तदेवापदि कर्तव्यं न कुर्यात्कर्मसंकरम्
॥४०॥

When one has the capacity, the alternative duty must be set aside by both. In adversity one should undertake that alternative, but one must not create a confusion of occupations.

Śrīdhara commentary (source range 40).

Śrīdhara explains that the primary duty is binding on a capable Brāhmaṇa, Kṣatriya, or Vaiśya, while an incapable person may resort to the alternative. Mixing of works means confusion of occupations.

VP 3.8.41

इत्येते कथिता राजन्वर्णधर्मा मया तव । धर्मानाश्रमिणां सम्यग्ब्रुवतो मे निशामय
॥४१॥

Thus I have told you the duties of the varṇas, O king. Now listen as I properly explain the duties of those who follow the āśramas.

Viṣṇu Purāṇa

uktānuvādapūrvakam āśramadharmān vaktuṃ pratijānīte iti iti

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

Śrīdhara commentary (source range 41).

Śrīdhara explains that the final verse restates the completed discussion of varṇa duties and announces the explanation of āśrama duties that follows. The emergency-duty alternatives are to be used only when necessary; using the wrong rule for one’s capacity produces a confusion of occupations.
