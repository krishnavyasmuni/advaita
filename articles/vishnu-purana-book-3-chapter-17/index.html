VP 3.17.1

इत्य् आह भगवान् और्व सगराय महात्मने।
सदाचारान् पुरा सम्यङ् मैत्रेय परिपृच्छते

Thus the blessed Aurva spoke to the great Sagara, who had formerly asked properly about right conduct, O Maitreya.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
ityāha bhagavanaurvassagarāya mahātmane — thus the blessed Aurva spoke to the great Sagara;
sadācāraṃ purā samyaṅ maitreya paripṛcchate — O Maitreya, [to him] who had formerly asked properly about right conduct
Śrīdhara
evṃ nvbhirdhyāyaiḥ tryīdhrmāḥ prpñcitāḥ — thus the duties of the three Vedas have been elaborated through nine chapters;
— but through their violation, attainment of harm is set forth here in two chapters;
— there in the seventeenth, praised by the gods, Viṣṇu produced Māyāmoha;
— through which the Daityas became deluded and naked
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

ityāha bhagavanaurvassagarāya mahātmane | sadācāraṃ purā samyaṅ maitreya paripṛcchate ||1||

evaṃ navabhiradhyāyaistrayīdharmāḥ prapañcitāḥ | vyatireke tvanarthāptirdvayeneha nirūpyate | tatra saptadaśe devaiḥ stuto viṣṇarajījanat | māyāmohaṃ yato magnā datyā nagnāśca jajñire

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

एवं नवभिरध्यायैस्त्रयीधर्माः प्रपञ्चिताः । व्यतिरेके त्वनर्थाप्तिर्द्वयेनेह निरूप्यते । तत्र सप्तदशे देवैः स्तुतो विष्णरजीजनत् । मायामोहं यतो मग्ना दत्या नग्नाश्च जज्ञिरे

Śrīdhara commentary.

Through nine chapters the duties of the threefold Veda have been elaborated. Their opposite, and the attainment of misfortune that follows it, is explained here in two [sets of] verses. In the seventeenth chapter Viṣṇu, praised by the gods, generated Māyā-moha; through him the Daityas became deluded and were born as nagna.

VP 3.17.6

त्रयी समस्त-वर्णानां द्विज संवरणं यतः।
नग्नो भवत्य् उज्जितायाम् अतस् तस्यां न संशयः

Since the threefold Veda is the covering and protection of all classes, one becomes nagna when it is abandoned; of this there is no doubt.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
trayī samastavarṇānāṃ dvija saṃvaraṇaṃ yataḥ — since the threefold Veda [is] the covering of all classes, O twice-born;
nagno bhavatyujjhitāyā matastasyāṃ na saṃśayaḥ — when it is abandoned, he becomes naked;
of this there is no doubt
Śrīdhara
kiñc — moreover;
— because that itself is also a covering;
— an outer covering;
— that is, an upper garment;
— therefore, on abandoning it;
— as if deprived of clothing and an upper garment;
— becomes naked, this is the meaning
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

trayī samastavarṇānāṃ dvija saṃvaraṇaṃ yataḥ | nagno bhavatyujjhitāyā matastasyāṃ na saṃśayaḥ ||6||

kiñca yataḥ saiva saṃvaraṇamapi prāvaraṇam uttarīyam iti yāvat, atastatparityāge paridhānottarīyahīna iva nagnī bhavatītyarthaḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

किञ्च यतः सैव संवरणमपि प्रावरणम् उत्तरीयम् इति यावत्, अतस्तत्परित्यागे परिधानोत्तरीयहीन इव नग्नी भवतीत्यर्थः

Śrīdhara commentary.

The threefold Veda is not merely a covering like cloth; it is saṃvaraṇa, that which removes all suffering. Therefore one who abandons it is called nagna; this does not mean merely a digambara.

VP 3.17.14

You are the earth, water, fire, air, and ether; you are the entire inner organ, primordial matter, and the transcendent Person.

Word-for-word

samastamantaḥkaraṇaṃ pradhānaṃ tatparaḥ pumān — the entire inner organ, primordial matter, and the supreme Person

tatra prathamaṃ viśvarūpeṇa saguṇaṃ stuvanti — there first they praise the qualified form as the universal form;
tvam urvi iti ādinā tasmai sarva-ātmanē namaḥ ity-antena — from “you are the earth” through “obeisance to that Self of all”;
samasta-mano-buddhi-ahaṅkāra-citta-ākhyam antaḥkaraṇam — the inner instrument called mind, intellect, ego, and memory;

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

tvamurvī salilaṃ vahnirvāyurākāśameva ca | samastamantaḥkaraṇaṃ pradhānaṃ tatparaḥ pumān ||14||

tatra prathamaṃ viśvarūpeṇa saguṇaṃ stuvanti—tvamurvi ityādinā tasmai sarvātmane namaḥ ityantena | samastamanobuddhyahaṅkāracittākhyamantaḥkaraṇam | tasyāḥ prakṛteḥ paraḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तत्र प्रथमं विश्वरूपेण सगुणं स्तुवन्ति—त्वमुर्वि इत्यादिना तस्मै सर्वात्मने नमः इत्यन्तेन । समस्तमनोबुद्ध्यहङ्कारचित्ताख्यमन्तःकरणम् । तस्याः प्रकृतेः परः

Śrīdhara commentary.

Here, beginning with “you are the earth,” they praise the Lord in his qualified universal form, up to “homage to that Universal Self.” The entire inner organ consists of mind, intellect, ego, and consciousness; “pradhāna” is that which is beyond primordial matter.

VP 3.17.45

इत्य् उक्ताः प्रणिपत्यैनं ययुर् देवा यथागतम्।
माया-मोहोऽपि तैः सार्धं ययौ यत्र महासुराः

Thus addressed, the gods prostrated to him and went as they had come; Māyā-moha too went with them to where the great Asuras were.
