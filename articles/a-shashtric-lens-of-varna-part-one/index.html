1. Preface

1.1 Introduction

One of the most misunderstood and propagandised topics in Hinduism is the varṇa system, often incorrectly called the “caste system” in English. Because it is a politically charged subject, people hold many different views and cite pragmatic concerns, tradition, history, and numerous other sources to support their claims.

Lord Kṛṣṇa, however, states in the Gītā:

Show Sanskrit

यः शास्त्रविधिमुत्सृज्य वर्तते कामकारतः। न स सिद्धिमवाप्नोति न सुखं न परां गतिम्॥ २३॥
तस्माच्छास्त्रं प्रमाणं ते कार्याकार्यव्यवस्थितौ। ज्ञात्वा शास्त्रविधानोक्तं कर्म कर्तुमिहार्हसि॥ २४॥

The Supreme Lord said: “He who discards scriptural procedure and acts according to his own wishes attains neither success, happiness, nor the highest goal. Therefore, scripture is your basis for determining what is and is not to be done. Having understood the scriptural injunction, it befits you to perform actions in this world.”

Bhagavad Gītā 16.23–24

Varṇa concerns what is and is not to be done (kāryākāryavyavasthiti). Therefore, scripture (śāstra) alone is the pramāṇa, or authoritative basis; nothing else can be an independent authority.

Two major sides nevertheless dominate the debate: varṇa by karma (action) and varṇa by janma (birth). Both appear to possess extensive scriptural support. To an outsider, the Hindu scriptures may therefore seem contradictory, but they are not.

The problem is that both sides tend to use śāstra as an ad hoc justification for a predetermined view instead of attempting to understand the full scriptural position. Each side ignores the pramāṇas presented by the other rather than undertaking samanvaya, or synthesis. Samanvaya seeks an understanding that does not discard any scriptural injunction. This article therefore synthesises the scriptural references on varṇa and attempts to establish the orthodox Hindu position.

1.2 On pramāṇa

Because this is a matter of scripturally enjoined dharma, śāstra alone is the pramāṇa. This is stated in the second Mīmāṃsā Sūtra:

Show Sanskrit

चोदनालक्षणोऽर्थो धर्मः॥ १.१.२॥

“That which is enjoined by a statement of scripture is dharma.”

Mīmāṃsā Sūtra 1.1.2

Śāstra includes the Vedas, whose four divisions are the Saṃhitā, Brāhmaṇa, Āraṇyaka, and Upaniṣad. The Vedas are interpreted with the aid of the Vedāṅgas, particularly grammar, prosody, and etymology. Although the Vedas are the highest scriptural authority, the itihāsas—the Rāmāyaṇa and Mahābhārata—and the eighteen Purāṇas assist in their proper interpretation. Mīmāṃsā, the science of hermeneutics, supplies principles for interpreting scripture.

The interpretations of bona fide ācāryas of the past will also be used as supporting evidence, but an ācārya’s words are not an independent pramāṇa. The Yājñavalkya Śikṣā, a Vedāṅga text, states:

Show Sanskrit

युक्तियुक्तं वचो ग्राह्यं न ग्राह्यं गुरुगौरवात्॥

“One should accept a guru’s statement when it is reasonable or established by scripture; one should not accept it merely because it was spoken by a guru.”

Yājñavalkya Śikṣā

The Yoga Vāsiṣṭha, attributed to Vālmīki, likewise states:

Show Sanskrit

युक्तियुक्तमुपादेयं वचनं बालकादपि। अन्यत्तृणमिव त्याज्यमप्युक्तं पद्मजन्मना॥ ३॥

“A reasonable statement should be accepted even when spoken by a child. An unreasonable statement should be discarded like a piece of straw, even if it was spoken by lotus-born Brahmā himself.”

Yoga Vāsiṣṭha 2.18.3

The Taittirīya Āraṇyaka further states:

Show Sanskrit

यान्यनवद्यानि कर्माणि तानि सेवितव्यानि। नो इतराणि॥ यान्यस्माकं सुचरितानि तानि त्वयोपास्यानि। नो इतराणि॥

“Only those actions of ours that are unobjectionable should be followed, not the others. Only those good deeds of ours should be emulated, not the others.”

Taittirīya Āraṇyaka

Thus, even the statements and conduct of ācāryas remain subject to scripture and must be rejected when they conflict with it. Scripture alone is an authority in its own right. In the final section, which concerns the practical implementation of varṇa, pratyakṣa, or empirical evidence, will also be considered.

1.3 Summary view

The conclusion reached through a synthesis of all the scriptures, and demonstrated in this article, is as follows:

  1. There are two forms of varṇa: jāti, or aupādhika varṇa, acquired through birth into a particular family; and guṇa-based, or svābhāvika varṇa, arising from a person’s qualities.
  2. A person who belongs to one varṇa by birth can enter another on the basis of qualities or the absence of them. Thus, a śūdra may become a brāhmaṇa, a brāhmaṇa may fall to the status of a śūdra, and other changes are likewise possible.
  3. For these reasons, varṇa is predominantly based on guṇa, while birth-based jāti-varṇa is secondary.

2. Varṇa by janma (jāti)

One error made by advocates of varṇa exclusively by qualities and actions is their denial that varṇa has any relation to jāti. Although the predominant scriptural position supports varṇa by qualities, a system based solely on qualities cannot account for the numerous scriptural passages that speak of varṇa by birth. These must be considered first.

2.1 Indirect pramāṇa

There are first several indirect difficulties. Passages such as Bhagavad Gītā 1.41 and portions of the Mahābhārata and Manusmṛti Chapter 10 describe varṇasaṅkaras, or mixed descendants of different varṇas, as highly discouraged and outside ordinary varṇāśrama. An exclusively karma-based theory cannot explain this. Some organisations therefore mistranslate varṇasaṅkara in Gītā 1.41 as “unwanted progeny,” avoiding the implications of the actual expression, “admixture of varṇas.”

Other passages, such as Manusmṛti 2.36, prescribe different ages of initiation for different varṇas. Such rules are likewise inexplicable if birth-based varṇa is rejected altogether.

2.2 Direct pramāṇa

There are also explicit references. The Manusmṛti states:

Show Sanskrit

ब्राह्मणः क्षत्रियो वैश्यस्त्रयो वर्णा द्विजातयः। चतुर्थ एकजातिस्तु शूद्रो नास्ति तु पञ्चमः॥ ४॥
सर्ववर्णेषु तुल्यासु पत्नीष्वक्षतयोनिषु। आनुलोम्येन सम्भूता जात्या ज्ञेयास्त एव ते॥ ५॥

“The brāhmaṇa, kṣatriya, and vaiśya are the three twice-born varṇas; the fourth, the śūdra, is once-born, and there is no fifth. Among all varṇas, those born in the natural order from wives of equal status are to be regarded as belonging to the same jāti as their fathers.”

Manusmṛti 10.4–5

The Padma Purāṇa similarly states:

Show Sanskrit

जन्मना ब्राह्मणो ज्ञेयः संस्कारैर्द्विज उच्यते॥ १२९॥

“By birth he is known as a brāhmaṇa, and through the initiatory rites he is called a dvija, or twice-born.”

Padma Purāṇa 7.47.129

Finally, the Chāndogya Upaniṣad, which is śruti, states:

Show Sanskrit

तद्य इह रमणीयचरणा अभ्याशो ह यत्ते रमणीयां योनिमापद्येरन् ब्राह्मणयोनिं वा क्षत्रिययोनिं वा वैश्ययोनिं वा। अथ य इह कपूयचरणा अभ्याशो ह यत्ते कपूयां योनिमापद्येरन् श्वयोनिं वा सूकरयोनिं वा चण्डालयोनिं वा॥

“Those whose conduct in this world was good quickly attain a good birth: birth as a brāhmaṇa, kṣatriya, or vaiśya. Those whose conduct was evil quickly attain an evil birth: birth as a dog, a pig, or a caṇḍāla.”

Chāndogya Upaniṣad 5.10.7

The word used is yoni. Brāhmaṇa, kṣatriya, vaiśya, and caṇḍāla are all called yonis. The word generally refers to a class determined by birth, comparable here to the animal species named alongside them.

Many advocates of varṇa by birth stop at this point. Having shown that “brāhmaṇa” and “śūdra” can name a yoni acquired at birth, they treat the question as settled. The matter, however, is not so simple. Even one born in a particular yoni may acquire another varṇa through qualities and actions, as the next section will demonstrate.

3. Change in varṇa

The primary aim of this treatise is to establish varṇa by qualities on the basis of scriptural evidence. We shall therefore begin with passages showing that the varṇa acquired at birth may change according to a person’s qualities.

3.1 Introductory pramāṇas

Show Sanskrit

शूद्रयोनौ हि जातस्य सद्गुणानुपतिष्ठतः। वैश्यत्वं लभते ब्रह्मन् क्षत्रियत्वं तथैव च॥ आर्जवे वर्तमानस्य ब्राह्मण्यमभिजायते॥

“O brāhmaṇa, one born in a śūdra yoni who possesses good qualities may attain vaiśyahood or, likewise, kṣatriyahood; one who lives in rectitude may attain even brāhmaṇahood.” The expression is explicitly śūdrayonau, “in a śūdra yoni.” Birth in the yoni of one varṇa therefore does not prevent the acquisition of another through qualities.

Mahābhārata 3.203.7

Show Sanskrit

ब्राह्मणः पतनीयेषु वर्तमानो विकर्मसु। दाम्भिको दुष्कृतप्रायः शूद्रेण सदृशो भवेत्॥ ११॥
यस्तु शूद्रो दमे सत्ये धर्मे च सततोत्थितः। तं ब्राह्मणमहं मन्ये वृत्तेन हि भवेद्द्विजः॥ १२॥

“A brāhmaṇa who engages in degrading and improper actions, who is hypocritical and habitually commits evil deeds, becomes like a śūdra. By contrast, a śūdra who remains established in self-restraint, truth, and dharma I regard as a brāhmaṇa, for one becomes a dvija through conduct.” Both elevation and degradation are thus connected to qualities.

Mahābhārata 3.215.11–12

3.2 Varṇa according to Śiva

In a conversation on varṇa found identically in the Mahābhārata and the Brahma Purāṇa, Śiva tells Pārvatī:

Show Sanskrit

वैश्यकर्म च यो विप्रो लोभमोहव्यपाश्रयः। ब्राह्मण्यं दुर्लभं प्राप्य करोत्यल्पमतिः सदा॥ १६॥
स द्विजो वैश्यतामेति वैश्यो वा शूद्रतामियात्। स्वधर्मात्प्रच्युतो विप्रस्ततः शूद्रत्वमाप्नुयात्॥ १७॥

“A brāhmaṇa who, overcome by greed or delusion, continually performs the work of a vaiśya after attaining the rare state of brāhmaṇahood—that foolish dvija becomes a vaiśya. A vaiśya may likewise become a śūdra, and a brāhmaṇa who falls from his own dharma may attain śūdrahood.”

Brahma Purāṇa

Show Sanskrit

न योनिर्नापि संस्कारो न श्रुतिर्न च सन्ततिः। कारणानि द्विजत्वस्य वृत्तमेव तु कारणम्॥ ५६॥
सर्वोऽयं ब्राह्मणो लोके वृत्तेन तु विधीयते। वृत्ते स्थितस्तु शूद्रोऽपि ब्राह्मणत्वं च गच्छति॥ ५७॥

“Neither yoni, initiatory rites, learning, nor lineage is the cause of dvijahood; conduct alone is its cause. Brāhmaṇa status in this world is determined by conduct. Even a śūdra who abides in such conduct attains brāhmaṇahood.” Yoni is therefore not the sole cause: a śūdra may rise through conduct, while a brāhmaṇa may fall by abandoning his duties.

3.3 Varṇa according to Nārada

When Yudhiṣṭhira asks Nārada about varṇāśrama in Śrīmad Bhāgavatam 7.11, Nārada presents the teaching not as his own opinion, but as what he heard from Nārāyaṇa:

Show Sanskrit

नत्वा भगवतेऽजाय लोकानां धर्मसेतवे। वक्ष्ये सनातनं धर्मं नारायणमुखाच्छ्रुतम्॥ ५॥

“Having bowed to the unborn Lord, the guardian of dharma for all worlds, I shall explain sanātana-dharma as heard from the mouth of Nārāyaṇa.”

Śrīmad Bhāgavatam

The chapter then defines the characteristics of each varṇa:

Show Sanskrit

शमो दमस्तपः शौचं सन्तोषः क्षान्तिरार्जवम्। ज्ञानं दयाच्युतात्मत्वं सत्यं च ब्रह्मलक्षणम्॥ २१॥
शौर्यं वीर्यं धृतिस्तेजस्त्यागश्चात्मजयः क्षमा। ब्रह्मण्यता प्रसादश्च सत्यं च क्षत्रलक्षणम्॥ २२॥
देवगुर्वच्युते भक्तिस्त्रिवर्गपरिपोषणम्। आस्तिक्यमुद्यमो नित्यं नैपुण्यं वैश्यलक्षणम्॥ २३॥
शूद्रस्य सन्नतिः शौचं सेवा स्वामिन्यमायया। अमन्त्रयज्ञो ह्यस्तेयं सत्यं गोविप्ररक्षणम्॥ २४॥

The characteristics of a brāhmaṇa are control of mind and senses, austerity, purity, contentment, patience, rectitude, knowledge, compassion, devotion to Acyuta, and truthfulness. Those of a kṣatriya are heroism, strength, fortitude, splendour, generosity, self-mastery, forgiveness, devotion to brāhmaṇas, graciousness, and truth. Those of a vaiśya are devotion to the gods, guru, and Acyuta; support of the three aims of life; faith; enterprise; and skill. Those of a śūdra are humility, purity, sincere service of the master, sacrifice without Vedic mantras, non-stealing, truthfulness, and protection of cows and brāhmaṇas.

At the end of the chapter, Nārada gives the rule by which these characteristics are to be applied:

Show Sanskrit

यस्य यल्लक्षणं प्रोक्तं पुंसो वर्णाभिव्यञ्जकम्। यदन्यत्रापि दृश्येत तत्तेनैव विनिर्दिशेत्॥ ३५॥

“Whatever characteristic has been described as indicating a man’s varṇa, if it is seen elsewhere, he should be designated according to that very characteristic.”

Śrīdharācārya comments:

Show Sanskrit

शमादिभिरेव ब्राह्मणादिव्यवहारो मुख्यो न जातिमात्रादित्याह—यस्येति। यद्यदन्यत्र वर्णान्तरेऽपि दृश्येत तेनैव लक्षणनिमित्तेनैव वर्णेन विनिर्दिशेत्। न तु जातिनिमित्तेनेत्यर्थः॥

“The principal basis for designating someone a brāhmaṇa and so forth is the qualities beginning with śama, not birth alone. If a characteristic is found elsewhere, in another varṇa, that person should be designated by the varṇa of which that characteristic is the cause, not on the basis of jāti.” The operative cause is thus lakṣaṇa, not birth.

3.4 Degradation in varṇa

Show Sanskrit

यस्य कायगतं ब्रह्म मद्येनाप्लाव्यते सकृत्। तस्य व्यपैति ब्राह्मण्यं शूद्रत्वं च स गच्छति॥ ९७॥

“When the Brahman residing in a man’s body is once flooded with intoxicating liquor, his brāhmaṇahood departs and he becomes a śūdra.”

Manusmṛti 11.97

Show Sanskrit

सद्यः पतति मांसेन लाक्षया लवणेन च। त्र्यहेण शूद्रो भवति ब्राह्मणः क्षीरविक्रयात्॥ ९२॥

“By selling meat, lac, or salt, a brāhmaṇa becomes an outcaste at once; by selling milk, a brāhmaṇa becomes a śūdra in three days.”

Manusmṛti 10.92

Show Sanskrit

सन्ध्यां नोपासते यस्तु ब्राह्मणो हि विशेषतः। स जीवन्नेव शूद्रः स्यान्मृतः श्वा चैव जायते॥ ७८॥

“One who does not perform the daily sandhyā—especially if he is a brāhmaṇa—becomes a śūdra while living and is born as a dog after death.”

Dakṣa Smṛti 2.78

A member of the three upper varṇas who is not initiated at the prescribed time is described as fallen, a vrātya or dvijabandhu. Śrīdhara defines the latter:

Show Sanskrit

द्विजबन्धवो यथोचितकालसंस्कारादिरहितास्त्रैवर्णिका भासाः॥

“Dvijabandhus are semblances of members of the three higher varṇas who lack the proper saṃskāras at the prescribed time.” Grammatically, dvijabandhu is a tatpuruṣa compound meaning “a relative of the dvijas,” implying one who is merely connected with them rather than truly belonging among them.

One might object that statements about losing varṇa are non-literal arthavāda, meaning only that a person becomes “like a śūdra.” Even if that reading were granted, it would still show that qualities and conduct take precedence over birth. Moreover, the following passage cannot be reduced in the same way:

Show Sanskrit

शूद्रो राजन् भवति ब्रह्मबन्धुर्दुश्चरित्रो यश्च धर्मादपेतः॥

“O king, a brahmabandhu who is of wicked conduct and has departed from dharma becomes a śūdra.” If brahmabandhu already meant merely “like a śūdra,” then to read this as another mere likeness would render it pointless. A person born a brāhmaṇa may first become a fallen brahmabandhu through lack of initiation and then become a śūdra through reprehensible conduct.

Mahābhārata 12.63.4

3.5 A pramāṇa concerning the right to the Vedas

A further objection is that when someone born in one jāti acquires another varṇa through qualities or action, the acquisition is only figurative: he does not receive the actual rights of that varṇa, just as a powerful king called a lion does not literally become one. Śrīmad Bhāgavatam answers this directly:

Show Sanskrit

यन्नामधेयश्रवणानुकीर्तनाद्यत्प्रह्वणाद्यत्स्मरणादपि क्वचित्। श्वादोऽपि सद्यः सवनाय कल्पते कुतः पुनस्ते भगवन्नु दर्शनात्॥ ६॥

“By hearing or chanting whose name, bowing to whom, or even once remembering whom, a dog-eater immediately becomes eligible for the Soma rite—what, then, can be said of directly seeing You, O Lord?”

Śrīmad Bhāgavatam 3.33.6

Vallabhācārya explains:

Show Sanskrit

श्वादोऽपि चाण्डालोऽपि सद्यः सवनाय सोमाभिषवयोग्यत्वाय सद्यस्तस्मिन्नेव जन्मनि अवकल्पते योग्यो भवति॥

“Even a dog-eater—that is, a caṇḍāla—becomes eligible for the Soma pressing immediately, in that very birth.”

A word-by-word (dalakṛtya) analysis removes the possible ambiguities:

  • śvādo’pi, “even a dog-eating caṇḍāla,” includes the lowest jāti and therefore necessarily includes those above it. Vallabha writes: श्वादश्चाण्डालो नामाधमः। उत्कृष्टस्योत्तमत्वे कः सन्देह इत्यपि शब्दार्थः—“The dog-eating caṇḍāla is the lowest; what doubt, then, can there be concerning those who are higher?”
  • sadyaḥ, “immediately,” rules out the claim that the change occurs only in a future birth. Vallabha glosses it as तस्मिन्नेव जन्मनि, “in that very birth.”
  • savanāya refers specifically to the Vedic Soma rite, not merely to a Purāṇic or Āgamic sacrifice that might also be called a yajña.
  • kalpate, “becomes eligible,” establishes a genuine entitlement. Soma rites require upanayana, and upanayana belongs to the three upper varṇas. Eligibility for Soma therefore entails eligibility for initiation and acquisition of the corresponding higher varṇa.

The objection that such a result is impossible and must therefore be mere praise of the divine name is also answered by Vallabha:

Show Sanskrit

अधर्मसंस्कृतभूतविशेषैर्हि चाण्डालादिशरीरमारभ्यते; सर्वोत्कृष्टधर्मसंस्कृतभूतविशेषैस्तु ब्राह्मणशरीरम्। भगवन्नाम्ना दीक्षया प्राप्तेन वा सर्वान् दोषानौत्पत्तिकान् परिहृत्य, तानि भूतानि उत्कृष्टसंस्कारेण संस्कृतानि क्रियन्त इति नाम्नो युक्तमेव माहात्म्यम्। अनेन सर्वदोषनाशनपूर्वकसर्वगुणोपपादकत्वं नाम्न उक्तम्॥

A caṇḍāla body results from deeply adharmic impressions, while a brāhmaṇa body results from highly dharmic ones. The Lord’s name, received through initiation, destroys the former faults and establishes excellent saṃskāras. This is therefore a fitting glory of the name: it removes all faults and produces all virtues. Jīva Gosvāmī and other ācāryas likewise accept that this verse grants brāhmaṇical eligibility to one born even as a caṇḍāla.

Vallabha then cites Vālmīki as an example:

Show Sanskrit

स्मरणस्य तु वाल्मीकेः। स हि चाण्डाल एव मार्गघातकः, ऋषेरुपदेशात्पूर्वं महर्षिभावनां कृत्वा पश्चाद्रामस्मरणेन तस्मिन्नेव जन्मनि वाल्मीकिर्जात इति॥

“Regarding remembrance, consider Vālmīki. He was a caṇḍāla highway robber, but through the instruction of a ṛṣi and remembrance of Rāma he became Maharṣi Vālmīki in that very birth.”

4. Varṇa by qualities

It might still be argued that changes of varṇa are exceptions and that the varṇa system remains primarily birth-based. The following scriptural conversations address the ideal basis of varṇa directly.

4.1 Bhṛgu and Bharadvāja

In Mahābhārata, Vana Parva 188–189 in the Critical Edition, Bharadvāja asks Bhṛgu about varṇa. The well-known commentary of Nīlakaṇṭha is used here.

The difficulty with jāti. Bharadvāja asks:

Show Sanskrit

चातुर्वर्ण्यस्य वर्णेन यदि वर्णो विभज्यते। सर्वेषां खलु वर्णानां दृश्यते वर्णसङ्करः॥
कामः क्रोधो भयं लोभः शोकश्चिन्ता क्षुधा श्रमः। सर्वेषां नः प्रभवति कस्माद्वर्णो विभज्यते॥

“If the four varṇas are divided according to jāti-varṇa, admixture is nevertheless seen among all of them. Desire, anger, fear, greed, grief, anxiety, hunger, and fatigue arise in everyone. How, then, is varṇa divided?” Bodily characteristics are likewise common to all. Nīlakaṇṭha summarises the difficulty: मनुष्येष्वपि न जातिभेदनिश्चय इत्यर्थः—“Among human beings there is no certain determination of distinctions of jāti.” Jāti is therefore an insufficient basis, especially where extensive intermixture has occurred.

The origin of the varṇas. Bhṛgu replies:

Show Sanskrit

न विशेषोऽस्ति वर्णानां सर्वं ब्राह्ममिदं जगत्। ब्राह्मणाः पूर्वसृष्टा हि कर्मभिर्वर्णतां गताः॥
कामभोगप्रियास्तीक्ष्णाः क्रोधनाः प्रियसाहसाः। त्यक्तस्वधर्मा रक्ताङ्गास्ते द्विजाः क्षत्रतां गताः॥
गोषु वृत्तिं समाधाय पीताः कृष्युपजीविनः। स्वधर्मान्नानुतिष्ठन्ति ते द्विजा वैश्यतां गताः॥
हिंसानृतप्रिया लुब्धाः सर्वकर्मोपजीविनः। कृष्णाः शौचपरिभ्रष्टास्ते द्विजाः शूद्रतां गताः॥
इत्येतैः कर्मभिर्व्यस्ता द्विजा वर्णान्तरं गताः। धर्मो यज्ञक्रिया चैषां नित्यं न प्रतिषिध्यते॥

“Originally there was no distinction among the varṇas; this whole world was brāhmaṇa. The brāhmaṇas created at the beginning attained different varṇas through their actions. Those fond of sensual pleasure, fierce and irascible, who delighted in valour and abandoned their own dharma, became kṣatriyas. Those who adopted cattle-rearing and agriculture became vaiśyas. Those fond of violence and falsehood, greedy, living by every kind of labour, and fallen from purity became śūdras. In this way, through their actions, the dvijas entered other varṇas; Vedic dharma and sacrifice were not invariably forbidden to them.”

The colours in this account signify guṇas: sattva for brāhmaṇas, rajas for kṣatriyas, rajas mixed with tamas for vaiśyas, and tamas for śūdras. The passage explains why no physical difference decisively separates human jāti groups: the distinction is one of qualities and actions. The Puruṣa Sūkta’s description of the varṇas as the mouth, arms, thighs, and feet of the cosmic Person is not a competing biological account. Medhātithi interprets the corresponding passage in Manusmṛti as commendatory language expressing relative functions and status. Bhṛgu’s answer therefore establishes guṇa and karma as the original and ideal basis of varṇa.

Qualities as the basis of varṇa. In the next chapter, Bharadvāja asks:

Show Sanskrit

ब्राह्मणः केन भवति क्षत्रियो वा द्विजोत्तम। वैश्यः शूद्रश्च विप्रर्षे तद्ब्रूहि वदतां वर॥

“By what does one become a brāhmaṇa or a kṣatriya, O best of dvijas? By what does one become a vaiśya or śūdra? Tell me, O best of speakers.”

Bhṛgu answers that one becomes a brāhmaṇa through the prescribed saṃskāras, purity, Vedic study, the six duties, proper conduct, devotion to the guru, vows, truth, charity, non-hatred, compassion, forgiveness, and austerity. One who protects society, worships the gods, studies, and delights in charity is called a kṣatriya. One devoted to agriculture, cattle-rearing, trade, purity, and Vedic study is called a vaiśya. One who abandons the Veda, lacks purity and proper conduct, and lives by indiscriminate labour is called a śūdra. He concludes:

Show Sanskrit

शूद्रे चैतद्भवेल्लक्ष्यं द्विजे तच्च न विद्यते। न वै शूद्रो भवेच्छूद्रो ब्राह्मणो न च ब्राह्मणः॥

“If these characteristics are found in a śūdra, and if they are absent in a dvija, then that śūdra is not a śūdra, nor is that brāhmaṇa a brāhmaṇa.”

Nīlakaṇṭha comments:

Show Sanskrit

एतत्सत्यादिसप्तकं द्विजे त्रैवर्णिके धर्म एव वर्णविभागे कारणं न जातिरित्यर्थः। तमेव ब्राह्मण्यहेतुं धर्ममाह॥

“The seven qualities beginning with truth, when present in a member of the three upper varṇas, are the cause of the division of varṇa—not jāti. This dharma alone is stated to be the cause of brāhmaṇahood.”

4.2 Yudhiṣṭhira on varṇa

In Mahābhārata, Vana Parva 180, a serpent asks Yudhiṣṭhira who becomes a brāhmaṇa. Yudhiṣṭhira answers:

Show Sanskrit

सत्यं दानं क्षमा शीलमानृशंस्यं दमो घृणा। दृश्यन्ते यत्र नागेन्द्र स ब्राह्मण इति स्मृतः॥

“Where truthfulness, charity, forgiveness, good conduct, compassion, self-control, and mercy are found, O king of serpents, that person is remembered as a brāhmaṇa.”

Mahābhārata

The serpent objects that truth, charity, freedom from anger, nonviolence, and compassion may also occur in a śūdra. Nīlakaṇṭha explains that the serpent is treating “brāhmaṇa” as birth alone; in fact, if a śūdra possesses these qualities, he too is a brāhmaṇa. Yudhiṣṭhira replies with the same decisive verse used by Bhṛgu:

Show Sanskrit

शूद्रे चैतद्भवेल्लक्ष्यं द्विजे तच्च न विद्यते। न वै शूद्रो भवेच्छूद्रो ब्राह्मणो न च ब्राह्मणः॥
यत्रैतल्लक्ष्यते सर्प वृत्तं स ब्राह्मणः स्मृतः। यत्रैतन्न भवेत्सर्प तं शूद्रमिति निर्दिशेत्॥

“If these characteristics occur in a śūdra and are absent in a dvija, then that śūdra is not a śūdra, nor is that brāhmaṇa a brāhmaṇa. Where this conduct is seen, O serpent, that person is regarded as a brāhmaṇa; where it is absent, he should be designated a śūdra.” Nīlakaṇṭha is unequivocal: a śūdra endowed with śama and the other virtues is a brāhmaṇa, while a brāhmaṇa endowed with lust and similar defects is a śūdra.

The serpent understands the implication: “If a brāhmaṇa is determined by conduct, birth is useless until the requisite conduct is seen.” Yudhiṣṭhira does not deny this. Instead, he explains why birth is an unreliable test:

Show Sanskrit

जातिरत्र महासर्प मनुष्यत्वे महामते। सङ्करात्सर्ववर्णानां दुष्परीक्ष्येति मे मतिः॥
सर्वे सर्वास्वपत्यानि जनयन्ति यदा नराः। वाङ्मैथुनमथो जन्म मरणं च समं नृणाम्॥
इदमार्षं प्रमाणं च ये यजामह इत्यपि। तस्माच्छीलं प्रधानेष्टं विदुर्ये तत्त्वदर्शिनः॥

“Among human beings, jāti is difficult to examine because all varṇas have intermixed. Men produce offspring with women of every group; speech, intercourse, birth, and death are common to all. The Vedic expression ‘we who sacrifice’ is also authority for this. Therefore, those who see truth know conduct to be the principal criterion.” The account of Satyakāma Jābāli in the Chāndogya Upaniṣad illustrates the same principle: although the boy did not know his paternal lineage, a sage accepted him as a brāhmaṇa solely because of his truthfulness.

Nīlakaṇṭha explains the cited Vedic evidence:

Show Sanskrit

तथा च श्रुतिः—“न चैतद्विद्मो ब्राह्मणाः स्मो वयमब्राह्मणा वा” इति ब्राह्मण्ये संशयमुपन्यस्यति। अत्र “ये यजामहे” इत्यनेन च ये वयं स्मो ब्राह्मणा वा अब्राह्मणा वा ते वयं यजामहे इति ब्राह्मण्येऽनवधारणं दर्शितम्॥

“Śruti states, ‘We do not know whether we are brāhmaṇas or non-brāhmaṇas,’ presenting doubt concerning brāhmaṇahood. ‘We who sacrifice’ likewise means, ‘Whatever we may be—brāhmaṇas or non-brāhmaṇas—we sacrifice,’ showing that brāhmaṇahood is not conclusively ascertained.”

4.3 Śukrācārya on varṇa

In Bhagavad Gītā 10.37, Kṛṣṇa identifies Himself with Uśanas, or Śukrācārya, among the wise. Śukrācārya’s Śukranīti states:

Show Sanskrit

कर्मैव कारणं चात्र सुगतिं दुर्गतिं प्रति। न जात्या ब्राह्मणश्चात्र क्षत्रियो वैश्य एव न।
न शूद्रो न च वै म्लेच्छो भेदिता गुणकर्मभिः॥ ३८॥

“Karma alone is the cause of good and bad destinations. A person is not a brāhmaṇa, kṣatriya, vaiśya, śūdra, or mleccha by birth; these are distinguished by qualities and actions.”

Śukranīti

Show Sanskrit

ब्रह्मणस्तु समुत्पन्नाः सर्वे ते किं न ब्राह्मणाः। न वर्णतो न जनकाद् ब्राह्मतेजः प्रपद्यते॥ ३९॥
ज्ञानकर्मोपासनाभिर्देवताराधने रतः। शान्तो दान्तो दयालुश्च ब्राह्मणश्च गुणैः कृतः॥ ४०॥

“Since all have arisen from Brahmā, why are all not brāhmaṇas? The splendour of brāhmaṇahood is obtained neither from colour nor from one’s father. One devoted to worship through knowledge, ritual, and contemplation—peaceful, self-controlled, and compassionate—is made a brāhmaṇa by these qualities.” Śukrācārya then defines the other varṇas by their characteristic work: protection and restraint of evil for the kṣatriya; trade, animal husbandry, and agriculture for the vaiśya; and service and manual labour for the śūdra.

4.4 The Gītā on varṇa

Show Sanskrit

चातुर्वर्ण्यं मया सृष्टं गुणकर्मविभागशः। तस्य कर्तारमपि मां विद्ध्यकर्तारमव्ययम्॥ १३॥

“The fourfold order was created by Me according to the divisions of qualities and actions. Although I am its creator, know Me as the immutable non-doer.”

Bhagavad Gītā 4.13

Madhvācārya explains:

Show Sanskrit

सात्त्विको हि ब्राह्मणः सात्त्विकराजसः क्षत्रियः। राजसतामसो वैश्यः। तामसः शूद्र इति गुणविभागः। कर्मविभागस्तु शमो दम इत्यादिना वक्ष्यते॥
स्वाभाविको ब्राह्मणादिः शमाद्यैरेव भिद्यते। योनिभेदकृतो भेदो ज्ञेय औपाधिकस्त्वयम्॥

“The brāhmaṇa is sāttvika, the kṣatriya sāttvika-rājasa, the vaiśya rājasa-tāmasa, and the śūdra tāmasa: this is division by guṇa. Division by karma is taught through śama, dama, and the other duties. The natural, or svābhāvika, brāhmaṇa and other varṇas are distinguished only by śama and the other qualities. The distinction produced by yoni is to be understood as merely aupādhika, or circumstantial.” Madhva thus recognises two forms of varṇa: svābhāvika, based on qualities and actions, and aupādhika, based on birth.

Neither Rāmānuja nor Śaṅkara interprets this verse as making birth the basis. Rāmānuja says that the universe is divided according to sattva and the other guṇas, and according to corresponding actions such as self-control. Śaṅkara likewise assigns the four varṇas according to combinations of sattva, rajas, and tamas and to their characteristic duties. Their commentaries therefore agree that the verse itself describes guṇa and karma.

Show Sanskrit

स्वे स्वे कर्मण्यभिरतः संसिद्धिं लभते नरः। स्वकर्मनिरतः सिद्धिं यथा विन्दति तच्छृणु॥ ४५॥

“A person devoted to his own respective work attains perfection. Hear how one devoted to his own work reaches perfection.” Madhvācārya clarifies that “one’s own” refers to qualities rather than birth:

Bhagavad Gītā 18.45

Show Sanskrit

अधिकास्तु गुणाः शूद्रे ब्राह्मणादिः स उच्यते। ब्राह्मणोऽप्यल्पगुणकः शूद्र एवेति कीर्तितः॥

“If higher qualities are present in a śūdra, he is called a brāhmaṇa or another higher varṇa. A brāhmaṇa who possesses inferior qualities is declared a śūdra.”

4.5 Vajrasūcī Upaniṣad

The Vajrasūcī Upaniṣad asks: को वा ब्राह्मणो नाम किं जीवः किं देहः किं जातिः किं ज्ञानं किं कर्म किं धार्मिक इति—“Who is called a brāhmaṇa? Is it the jīva, body, birth, knowledge, action, or religiosity?” It rejects each insufficient criterion in turn. On birth, it states:

Show Sanskrit

तर्हि जातिर्ब्राह्मण इति चेत् तन्न। तत्र जात्यन्तरजन्तुष्वनेकजातिसम्भवात् महर्षयो बहवः सन्ति। ऋष्यशृङ्गो मृग्याः, कौशिकः कुशात्, जाम्बूको जाम्बूकात्, वाल्मीको वाल्मीकात्, व्यासः कैवर्तकन्यकायाम्, शशपृष्ठात् गौतमः, वसिष्ठ उर्वश्याम्, अगस्त्यः कलशे जात इति श्रुतत्वात्। एतेषां जात्या विनाप्यग्रे ज्ञानप्रतिपादिता ऋषयो बहवः सन्ति। तस्मान्न जातिर्ब्राह्मण इति॥

“If it is said that birth makes a brāhmaṇa, that is not so. Many great ṛṣis arose from other classes of beings: Ṛṣyaśṛṅga from a doe, Kauśika from kuśa grass, Jāmbūka from a jackal, Vālmīki from an anthill, Vyāsa from a fisherwoman’s daughter, Gautama from the back of a hare, Vasiṣṭha from Urvaśī, and Agastya from a jar. Although lacking brāhmaṇa birth, these many became ṛṣis through supreme knowledge. Therefore, birth does not make a brāhmaṇa.” Vālmīki and Vyāsa, authors of the two itihāsas, are especially significant human examples.

Vajrasūcī Upaniṣad

After rejecting the proposed alternatives, the Upaniṣad gives its conclusion:

Show Sanskrit

शमदमादिसम्पन्नो भावमात्सर्यतृष्णाशामोहादिरहितो दम्भाहङ्कारादिभिरसंस्पृष्टचेताः समस्तलक्षणो यः स एव ब्राह्मण इति श्रुतिस्मृतीतिहासपुराणानामभिप्रायः। अन्यथा हि ब्राह्मणत्वसिद्धिर्नास्त्येव॥

“One endowed with śama, dama, and the other virtues, free from attachment, jealousy, craving, delusion, and the like, and whose mind is untouched by hypocrisy and ego—he alone is a brāhmaṇa. This is the purport of śruti, smṛti, itihāsa, and Purāṇa. Brāhmaṇahood is certainly not established otherwise.”

5. Examples of change in varṇa

The preceding sections established what the scriptures prescribe. We now turn to what they describe: examples of how varṇa operated in scriptural narratives. Vyāsa and Vālmīki have already been mentioned; the following cases provide additional evidence.

5.1 Pṛṣadhra

Śrīmad Bhāgavatam 9.2 and Viṣṇu Purāṇa 4.1 describe Manu’s lineage in similar terms. The Viṣṇu Purāṇa traces it from Brahmā through Dakṣa, Aditi, Vivasvān, and Manu. Although Dakṣa is accepted as a brāhmaṇa, Manu’s descendants include kṣatriya kings such as Ikṣvāku and Karūṣa. The lineage itself thus illustrates a transition from brāhmaṇa to kṣatriya, consistent with Bhṛgu’s account.

Show Sanskrit

पृषध्रस्तु मनुपुत्रो गुरुगोवधाच्छूद्रत्वमगमत्॥

“Pṛṣadhra, the son of Manu, became a śūdra because he killed his guru’s cow.” The Bhāgavatam explains that Pṛṣadhra was guarding the cows when a tiger attacked at night. In darkness, he mistook a cow for the tiger and severed its head. His guru cursed him:

Show Sanskrit

न क्षत्रबन्धुः शूद्रस्त्वं कर्मणा भवितामुना॥

“You shall not even remain a kṣatrabandhu; by this action you shall become a śūdra.” The phrase “not even a fallen kṣatriya” removes the possibility that “śūdra” is only a comparison.

Bhāgavatam

5.2 Nābhāga

Show Sanskrit

दिष्टपुत्रस्तु नाभागो वैश्यतामगमत्॥
नाभागो दिष्टपुत्रोऽन्यः कर्मणा वैश्यतां गतः॥

The Viṣṇu Purāṇa says, “Nābhāga, son of Diṣṭa, attained vaiśyahood,” while the Bhāgavata Purāṇa makes the mechanism explicit: “Another son of Diṣṭa was Nābhāga, who became a vaiśya through his karma.”

5.3 The lineages of Dhṛṣṭa and Agniveśya

Show Sanskrit

धृष्टाद्धार्ष्टमभूत्क्षत्रं ब्रह्मभूयं गतं क्षितौ॥
ततो ब्रह्मकुलं जातमाग्निवेश्यायनं नृप॥

“From Manu’s son Dhṛṣṭa arose the Dhārṣṭa kṣatriyas, who attained brāhmaṇahood on earth.” Śrīdharācārya glosses brahmabhūyam as brāhmaṇatvam. Likewise, “From Agniveśya, a grandson of Manu, arose the brāhmaṇa lineage called Āgniveśyāyana.” Descendants of Manu’s kṣatriya line thus became brāhmaṇas, demonstrating flexibility not only for individuals but for lineages.

5.4 Viśvāmitra

The Rāmāyaṇa and other scriptures describe Viśvāmitra’s birth as a kṣatriya king and his ascent to brāhmaṇahood through austerity. Brahmā himself confirms it:

Show Sanskrit

विश्वामित्रोऽपि धर्मात्मा लब्ध्वा ब्राह्मण्यमुत्तमम्। पूजयामास ब्रह्मर्षिं वसिष्ठं जपतां वरम्॥ कृतकामो महीं सर्वां चचार तपसि स्थितः। एवं त्वनेन ब्राह्मण्यं प्राप्तं राम महात्मना॥

“The righteous Viśvāmitra, having attained the highest brāhmaṇahood, honoured Brahmarṣi Vasiṣṭha. His wish fulfilled, he wandered the whole earth established in austerity. Thus, O Rāma, this great soul attained brāhmaṇahood.” A variant account in the Mahābhārata concerning ritual food eaten by his mother belongs to another narration and may be understood through kalpa-bheda; it does not negate the explicit attainment described here.

Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa 1.65.26–28

5.5 Ārṣṭiṣeṇa, Sindhudvīpa, and Devāpi

Show Sanskrit

एवं सिद्धः स भगवानार्ष्टिषेणः प्रतापवान्॥ तस्मिंस्तीर्थे तदा राजन् सिन्धुद्वीपः प्रतापवान्। देवापिश्च महाराज ब्राह्मण्यं प्रापतुर्महत्॥ तथा च कौशिकस्तात तपोनित्यो जितेन्द्रियः। तपसा वै सुतप्तेन ब्राह्मणत्वमवाप्तवान्॥

Ārṣṭiṣeṇa became accomplished, while Sindhudvīpa and Devāpi attained great brāhmaṇahood. Viśvāmitra, son of Kuśika, likewise attained brāhmaṇahood through intense austerity and mastery of the senses. These events belong to Kṛta Yuga, whose characteristic practice was tapas. In Kali Yuga, the parallel means is kīrtana of the Lord’s name, as the discussion of Bhāgavatam 3.33.6 has shown.

5.6 Mataṅga

Skanda Purāṇa 5.2.60 narrates that Mataṅga, though raised in the household of Ṛṣi Sugati, was born from adultery between a barber and a brāhmaṇa woman and was therefore declared a caṇḍāla. He performed austerity seeking brāhmaṇahood. Indra directed him to worship Śiva, whereupon Mataṅga worshipped a liṅga in the Mahākāla forest.

Show Sanskrit

ब्राह्मण्यं दुर्लभं लब्धं लिङ्गस्यास्य प्रभावतः॥

“The difficult-to-attain state of brāhmaṇahood was obtained through the power of this liṅga.” Mataṅga subsequently became a great sage. This directly illustrates a caṇḍāla’s elevation through worship of the Lord. The Mahābhārata version stops when Indra emphasises how difficult brāhmaṇahood is to attain; the Skanda Purāṇa continues the narrative to its fulfilment. The accounts may also reflect kalpa-bheda.

Skanda Purāṇa

5.7 King Vītahavya

In Mahābhārata 13.3, Bhīṣma tells Yudhiṣṭhira that brāhmaṇahood is exceedingly difficult for the other three varṇas to attain. This praise of its rarity cannot be read as an absolute impossibility, for in the next chapter Yudhiṣṭhira himself cites Viśvāmitra and Vītahavya as counterexamples. Bhīṣma then narrates how the kṣatriya king Vītahavya attained brāhmaṇahood through the grace of Bhṛgu and concludes:

Show Sanskrit

एवं विप्रत्वमगमद्वीतहव्यो नराधिपः। भृगोः प्रसादाद्राजेन्द्र क्षत्रियः क्षत्रियर्षभ॥

“In this manner King Vītahavya, a kṣatriya, attained viprahood through the grace of Bhṛgu, O best of kṣatriyas.” Bhīṣma’s earlier statement is therefore arthavāda glorifying brāhmaṇahood, not a denial of change.

Mahābhārata

5.8 Triśaṅku and others

The Rāmāyaṇa contains three examples of degradation to caṇḍāla or niṣāda status:

  • Triśaṅku: अथ रात्र्यां व्यतीतायां राजा चण्डालतां गतः—“When the night had passed, the king attained caṇḍālahood” (Rāmāyaṇa 1.57.9). He fell after insulting his guru.
  • Mahodaya, a son of Vasiṣṭha: Viśvāmitra declares that, for insulting one who did not deserve insult, he would be reviled throughout the worlds and attain niṣādahood (Rāmāyaṇa 1.59.20).
  • Viśvāmitra’s sons: for insulting their father, they were cursed to roam the earth as dog-eating caṇḍālas for a thousand years (Rāmāyaṇa 1.62.7).

5.9 A verse of the Ṛgveda

Show Sanskrit

कारुरहं ततो भिषगुपलप्रक्षिणी नना। नानाधियो वसूयवोऽनु गा इव तस्थिमेन्द्रायेन्दो परि स्रव॥ ३॥

“I am a singer of hymns; my father is a physician, and my mother grinds grain. With different occupations, desiring wealth, we remain together like cattle. Flow, O Soma, for Indra.” The speaker is a kāru, a singer of Vedic hymns and therefore a brāhmaṇa, while his father is a physician. Manusmṛti 10.47 assigns medicine to the mixed Ambaṣṭha jāti, and 3.152 excludes physicians from rites for gods and ancestors. The hymn therefore presents a Vedic seer whose father performed the occupation of a lower jāti, supporting elevation through qualities.

Ṛgveda 9.112.3

5.10 Kavaṣa Ailūṣa

The eighth chapter of the Aitareya Brāhmaṇa recounts the story of Kavaṣa Ailūṣa. Sages holding a Soma sacrifice on the Sarasvatī objected that he was a gambler, the son of a slave woman, and a non-brāhmaṇa, asking how he could be initiated among them. They drove him into the desert to die of thirst.

Tormented by thirst, Kavaṣa perceived the Ṛgvedic mantra प्र देवत्रा ब्रह्मणे गातुरेत्वपः—“May the waters make a path to the gods for sacred prayer.” The Sarasvatī’s waters flowed around him. Seeing that the gods recognised him, the sages recalled him, accepted him into the Soma sacrifice, and performed the Āpāṃ Napāt rite using the mantra revealed to him. A man expelled as a slave woman’s son and non-brāhmaṇa thus became a Vedic seer and was admitted to a Soma rite.

These narratives supply thirteen individual examples of change and two examples of entire lineages changing varṇa. Many more could be cited, but these are sufficient to illustrate the principle already established by direct scriptural statements.

6. Views of other ācāryas

6.1 Mādhva ācāryas

Madhvācārya’s position has already been established from his commentary on the Gītā. Later teachers in his lineage express the same view.

Śrī Viśveśa Tīrtha, Gītā Sāroddhāra

“Śrī Madhvācārya has explained that the four divisions of varṇa do not belong to the human body but to the soul. Souls are categorised according to their nature, qualities, and propensities. The good souls worthy of salvation are divided into four groups. All possess a predominance of sattva; their differences arise from permutations of rajas and tamas.

“A śūdra with noble qualities such as self-control and truthfulness is indeed a brāhmaṇa; a person is identified as a brāhmaṇa only through noble conduct. In answering the Yakṣa, it is stated that virtuous character alone is the distinguishing cause of brāhmaṇahood.

“The varṇa system of the Gītā is not a classification based on accidental attributes of birth, but a classification of good souls in society. Such a system, based on essential and deeper qualities, produces harmony among different communities for the good of society as a whole.”

Bannanje Govindācārya, Madhvacharya: Life and Teachings

“The Gītā’s idea of four varṇas is quite distinct from the four castes prevalent today. It concerns the soul’s innermost nature or personality. The soul’s true colour must be discovered; that is the right social order. In such an order, the son of one low-born may be a noble brāhmaṇa, while a brāhmaṇa’s son may be a śūdra. Varṇa is not transmitted hereditarily; it is personal and determined by the individual’s own characteristics.”

6.2 Gauḍīya ācāryas

Jīva Gosvāmī

Commenting on Bhāgavatam 3.33.6 in his discussion of the Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu, Jīva explains that hearing, chanting, bowing, and remembering the Lord destroy the dog-eater’s prārabdha-karma, which caused his low birth and prevented sacrifice. Devotional acts simultaneously produce the merit and qualities required for sacrifice. Just as one born a brāhmaṇa still requires Vedic initiation with Gāyatrī, the purified dog-eater requires the corresponding initiation and training. The verse therefore teaches genuine elevation by eradicating obstructive karma; if even a dog-eater can be elevated in this way, the possibility is still clearer for others.

Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa

“The qualifications of a brāhmaṇa are required for the study and practice of Vedānta. These classifications are not determined by birth but by the individual’s qualities and activities, and they can be modified through experience and training. Thus, even a śūdra who takes up Vedic disciplines, refrains from meat-eating, intoxication, illicit sex, and gambling, and chants the Lord’s holy name under a self-realised teacher may develop the purity and intelligence of a brāhmaṇa and become qualified to study Vedānta.”

Satya Nārāyaṇa Dāsa

“Varṇa and jāti are separate and should not be confused. Jāti is based on birth, while varṇa is based on qualities. Some hold that anyone possessing the qualifications may be a brāhmaṇa regardless of birth. Indeed, this is how it was originally.”

Show Sanskrit

यथा काञ्चनतां याति कांस्यं रसविधानतः। तथा दीक्षाविधानेन द्विजत्वं जायते नृणाम्॥

“Just as base metal becomes gold through an alchemical process, a person attains dvijahood through the prescribed process of Vaiṣṇava initiation.” This accords with Madhva’s view that Vaiṣṇavas are sattva-predominant.

Sanātana Gosvāmī

Viśvanātha Cakravartin

शूद्रोऽप्यागमसम्पन्नो द्विजो भवति संस्कृतः॥

“Even a śūdra, perfected through the Āgamic procedure, becomes a consecrated dvija.”

Mahābhārata

6.3 Advaita ācāryas

Swami Kṛṣṇānanda

“The classification of society into four varṇas should not be understood as a rigid isolation of groups by birth and heredity. It is a cooperative system intended to preserve and prosper society through division of labour, based on people’s qualities and on what they can contribute according to aptitude, knowledge, and capacity. Svabhāva—one’s inherent nature—determines svadharma, one’s duty in society.”

Swami Chinmayānanda

“Casteism is a perversion and has no place in Hinduism. The caste system is universal and cannot be removed: it shows how the mind is cast into four moulds—thinkers, leaders, entrepreneurs, and workers. It is based on the temperament or ‘colour’ (varṇa) of the mind—sattva, rajas, and tamas—and on the nature of individual actions, or karma. It is not based on birth.”

6.4 Śaiva ācāryas

Sivaya Subramuniyaswami, Living with Siva

“The original caste system was based on behaviour. Its four divisions reflected an individual’s ability to manage body, mind, and emotions. If someone ceased to fulfil the dharma of his caste, society recognised that he had moved from one caste to another. The original system rested on self-discipline through education and personal sādhana, and on the unfolding of consciousness within each individual.”

Basava

“One who kills is a Māḍiga; one who eats filth is a Holeya. What has birth to do with a superior or inferior class? By birth, all are equal. Those who take refuge in Lord Kūḍalasaṅgamadeva and seek the welfare of all beings are the truly superior class.”

Like Vaiṣṇava teachers who describe devotees of Viṣṇu as sāttvika, Basava describes those surrendered to Śiva as superior through qualities, while degraded status follows degraded action.

Having assembled the evidence, the thesis may be restated:

  1. There are two forms of varṇa: jāti-based or aupādhika varṇa, acquired through birth in a family; and guṇa-based or svābhāvika varṇa, arising from a person’s qualities.
  2. One who belongs to a varṇa by birth may enter another through qualities or the absence of them. A śūdra may become a brāhmaṇa, a brāhmaṇa may fall to śūdrahood, and other transitions are likewise possible.
  3. Because of these principles—and because intermixture makes jāti increasingly difficult to determine—varṇa is predominantly based on guṇa, while birth-based varṇa is secondary.

8. Pragmatic perspective

8.1 Economic prosperity and social harmony

Show Sanskrit

धर्मादर्थः प्रभवति धर्मात्प्रभवते सुखम्॥

“From dharma arises prosperity; from dharma arises happiness.”

Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa 3.9.30

Scripture is the basis of dharma, but the soundness of an interpretation may also be tested by whether it produces the stated fruits: economic prosperity and enduring social harmony. “Happiness” here does not mean a person’s momentary preference, but the long-term welfare of society.

A system that ignores ability in favour of birth is economically inefficient. A person capable of being a merchant, warrior, or scholar may be barred from that vocation because of ancestry, while someone lacking the necessary aptitude may be compelled to perform it. The result is a larger number of unqualified people in every field and a corresponding loss of prosperity. Preventing qualified members of lower jāti groups from advancing also creates resentment and social division.

History repeatedly illustrates the advantage of systems that permit advancement by merit over rigid hereditary class structures. Western Europe’s more flexible institutions generally outperformed the hereditary serfdom of parts of Eastern Europe. Revolutionary France fielded a highly effective meritocratic army; Napoleon, born to minor Corsican nobility, and many of his generals advanced after inherited barriers were weakened. The Spanish Empire, despite its size, was eventually overtaken by rivals while maintaining a highly stratified colonial order.

In India, rigid hereditary restrictions weakened economic and military capacity and contributed to social discontent. Such grievances facilitated conversion under Muslim and British rule, and caste remains one of the most frequent criticisms directed at Hinduism. A rigidly birth-based system should therefore be rejected if society is to secure prosperity, harmony, and the strength of the tradition itself.

8.2 Implementation

The final question is how a system of varṇa by qualities can be implemented. Birth-based classification may appear easier, but the scriptural arguments above say that jāti becomes indeterminate through intermixture, whereas qualities can be observed directly. A genuine concern nevertheless remains: if varṇa were left entirely to personal choice, nearly everyone might claim to be a brāhmaṇa or kṣatriya.

Assignment cannot therefore rest on self-declaration. It must be made by the guru, who examines a student’s qualities and grants an appropriate varṇa. As already noted, several ācāryas accept Pañcarātrika or Āgamic initiation as capable of making a person a dvija irrespective of birth. The Mahābhārata states:

Show Sanskrit

शूद्रोऽप्यागमसम्पन्नो द्विजो भवति संस्कृतः॥

“Even a śūdra who has completed the Āgamic procedure becomes a consecrated dvija.”

Mahābhārata

Before such saṃskāra, all are to be treated as śūdras, as stated in the Skanda Purāṇa 6.239.3:

Show Sanskrit

जन्मना जायते शूद्रः संस्काराद्द्विज उच्यते॥

“By birth one is born a śūdra; through saṃskāra one is called a dvija.” In a system where the guru assigns varṇa after examining qualities and performing the appropriate initiation, social roles can be filled by qualified people and the economy can prosper.

खण्डिनी पूर्वपक्षाणां व्यवहारोपदेशदा। विजयी पठिता येन सद्वर्णार्थप्रकाशिनी॥ १०॥
पठति वा शृणोति वा यः कीर्तयति वा शुचिः। भवतां तस्य पादौ राज्ञां मकुटभूषणौ॥ ११॥
ज्योतिषानामिवादित्यः प्रणवश्चैव छन्दसाम्। तथैषा कलिशास्त्राणां विराजति महद्युता॥
