VP 3.16.1

हविष्यमत्स्यमांसैस्तु शशस्य नकुलस्य च । सौकरच्छागलैणेपरौरवैर्यगवयेन च

The ancestors attain satisfaction for one month through havis, for two months through fish, and for three months through meat; likewise through rabbit, mongoose, boar, goat, antelope, ruru deer, and gavaya.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

haviṣya-matsya-māṃsaiḥ tu — with havis, fish, and meat; śaśasya nakulasya ca — of rabbit and mongoose; saukara-chāgala-aiṇeya-rauravaiḥ — with boar, goat, antelope, and ruru deer; gavayena ca — and with gavaya

Śrīdhara

sadācāra-prasaṅga-anupra-saṅgena — as a continuation of the discussion of proper conduct; śrāddha-yogyāni ayogyāni ca dravyāṇi — materials suitable and unsuitable for śrāddha; haviṣyaiḥ vrīhy-ādibhiḥ — through havis made from rice and similar grains; matsya-māṃsaiḥ — through fish and meat; eṇaḥ — a kind of deer; ruruḥ pṛṣataḥ — ruru and pṛṣata deer; gavayaḥ go-sadṛśaḥ paśuḥ — the gavaya, a forest animal resembling a cow

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

haviṣyamatsyamāṃsaistu śaśasya nakulasya ca | saukaracchāgalaiṇeparauravairyagavayena ca ||1||

sadācāraprasaṅgānuprasaṅgenātha ṣoḍaśe | dravyāṇi śrāddhayogyāni tathāyogyāni cādiśat | tatra śrāddhe deyadravyapraśaṃsā haviṣyairvrīhyādibhiḥ matsyādīnāṃ navānāṃ māṃsaśca pratyekamekaikamāsavṛddhyā pitāmahāḥ pitarastṛptiṃ prayānti iti dvayoranvayaḥ | vrīhyādibhirekamāsaṃ matsyamāṃsairdvau māsāvityevaṃ daśamāsāntam matsyāmiṣe māṃsatvopacāraḥ | eṇo hariṇaviśeṣaḥ | ruruḥ pṛṣataḥ tadīyaiḥ | gavayo gosadṛśaḥ paśurāraṇyakaḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

सदाचारप्रसङ्गानुप्रसङ्गेनाथ षोडशे । द्रव्याणि श्राद्धयोग्यानि तथायोग्यानि चादिशत् । तत्र श्राद्धे देयद्रव्यप्रशंसा हविष्यैर्व्रीह्यादिभिः मत्स्यादीनां नवानां मांसश्च प्रत्येकमेकैकमासवृद्ध्या पितामहाः पितरस्तृप्तिं प्रयान्ति इति द्वयोरन्वयः । व्रीह्यादिभिरेकमासं मत्स्यमांसैर्द्वौ मासावित्येवं दशमासान्तम् मत्स्यामिषे मांसत्वोपचारः । एणो हरिणविशेषः । रुरुः पृषतः तदीयैः । गवयो गोसदृशः पशुरारण्यकः

Śrīdhara commentary.

As an incidental continuation of the discussion of proper conduct, this sixteenth chapter prescribes materials suitable and unsuitable for śrāddha. The first two verses are connected as follows: the ancestors attain satisfaction through havis made from rice and similar grains for one month, through fish for two months, and through the nine kinds of meat beginning with meat and ending with gavaya, with each increasing the satisfaction by one month. Here eṇa means a kind of deer, ruru means the spotted deer, and gavaya is a wild animal resembling a cow.

VP 3.16.2

औरभ्रगव्यैश्च तथा मासवृद्ध्या पितामहाः । प्रयान्ति तृप्तिं मांसैस्तु नित्यं वार्ध्रीणसामिपैः

The ancestors attain satisfaction through sheep and cow products with the satisfaction increasing month by month; through the meat of the Vārddhrīṇasa they are satisfied perpetually.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

aurabhra-gavyaiḥ ca tathā — with sheep and cow products likewise; māsa-vṛddhyā pitāmahāḥ prayānti tṛptim — with satisfaction increasing month by month the ancestors attain fulfillment; māṃsaiḥ tu nityam vārdhrīṇasa-āmiṣaiḥ — but with the meat of the Vārddhrīṇasa, they are satisfied perpetually

Śrīdhara

urabhraḥ meṣaḥ — urabhra is a sheep; gavyaṃ payaḥ pāyasaṃ vā — gavya means milk or payasa; saṃvatsaraṃ tṛptiḥ — satisfaction for a year; vādhriṇasa-māṃsaiḥ nityam tṛpyanti — they are always satisfied by vādhriṇasa meat; tripibaḥ — one who drinks three times; kṛṣṇa-grīvaḥ rakta-śirāḥ śveta-pakṣaḥ — black-necked, red-headed, and white-winged; vādhriṇasaḥ — this is called vādhriṇasa

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

aurabhragavyaiśca tathā māsavṛddhyā pitāmahāḥ | prayānti tṛptiṃ māṃsaistu nityaṃ vārdhrīṇasāmipaiḥ ||2||

urabhro meṣaḥ tadīyaiśca | gavyantu payaḥ pāyasaṃ vā | saṃvatsarantu gavyena payasā pāyasena ca | iti smṛteḥ | māṃsamadhyapāṭhāt māṃsamavetyante, tattu yugāntarīṇamityavadheyam | vādhrīṇasamāṃsaistu nityaṃ sarvakālaṃ tṛpyanti | vādhrīṇasaḥ smṛtiprasiddhaḥ | yathā— tripibantvindriyaṃ kṣīṇaṃ śvetaṃ vṛddhaprajāpatim | vādhrīṇasantu taṃ prāhuryājñikāḥ śrāddhakarmaṇi ॥ iti | jalapāne yasya mukhavat karṇau ca jalaṃ spṛśataḥ sa tripibaḥ | yadvā— kṛṣṇagrīvo raktaśirāḥ śvetapakṣo vihaṅgamaḥ | sa vai vādhrīṇasaḥ prokta ityeṣā naigamī śrutiḥ ॥ iti

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

उरभ्रो मेषः तदीयैश्च । गव्यन्तु पयः पायसं वा । संवत्सरन्तु गव्येन पयसा पायसेन च । इति स्मृतेः । मांसमध्यपाठात् मांसमवेत्यन्ते, तत्तु युगान्तरीणमित्यवधेयम् । वाध्रीणसमांसैस्तु नित्यं सर्वकालं तृप्यन्ति । वाध्रीणसः स्मृतिप्रसिद्धः । यथा— त्रिपिबन्त्विन्द्रियं क्षीणं श्वेतं वृद्धप्रजापतिम् । वाध्रीणसन्तु तं प्राहुर्याज्ञिकाः श्राद्धकर्मणि ॥ इति । जलपाने यस्य मुखवत् कर्णौ च जलं स्पृशतः स त्रिपिबः । यद्वा— कृष्णग्रीवो रक्तशिराः श्वेतपक्षो विहङ्गमः । स वै वाध्रीणसः प्रोक्त इत्येषा नैगमी श्रुतिः ॥ इति

Śrīdhara commentary.

Urabhra means sheep and its meat. Gavya means milk or payasa; the tradition says that milk or payasa gives satisfaction for a year. Some say gavya means meat. The meat is understood at the end because it occurs in the middle of the list; this refers to a very long period. Through Vārddhrīṇasa meat the ancestors are satisfied at all times. Vārddhrīṇasa is known from tradition: it is described as a bird with a black neck, red head, and white wings; ritual authorities also describe it as a white, aged goat whose milk is drunk three times. If its ears are touched by water as it drinks, it is called Tripiba; alternatively, the first bird-description is the traditional account.

VP 3.16.3

खड्गमांसमतीवात्र कालशाकं तथा मधु । शस्तानि कर्मण्यत्यन्ततृप्तिदानि नरेश्वर

Rhinoceros meat, Kāla greens, and honey are especially praised in the rite and give very great satisfaction, O king.

Viṣṇu Purāṇa

khaḍga-māṃsam atīva atra — rhinoceros meat here is especially [excellent]; kāla-śākam tathā madhu — likewise Kāla greens and honey; śastāni karmaṇi atyanta-tṛpti-dāni — are praised in the rite and give very great satisfaction; nareśvara — O king

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

khaḍgamāṃsamatīvātra kālaśākaṃ tathā madhu | śastāni karmaṇyatyantatṛptidāni nareśvara ||3||

Śrīdhara commentary.

No commentary.

VP 3.16.4

गयामुपेत्य यः श्राद्धं करोति पृथिवीपते । सफलं तस्य तज्जन्म जायते पितृतुष्टिदम्

O lord of the earth, whoever performs śrāddha after reaching Gayā makes his birth fruitful; it gives satisfaction to the ancestors.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

gayām upetya yaḥ śrāddhaṃ karoti — whoever performs śrāddha after reaching Gayā; pṛthivī-pate — O lord of the earth; saphalam tasya tat janma jāyate — his birth becomes fruitful; pitṛ-tuṣṭi-dam — giving satisfaction to the ancestors

Śrīdhara

tjjnm pitृtuṣṭidṃ st tsmin jnmni pitॄṇāmृṇādृttīrṇ ityrthḥ — The birth that reaches Gayā becomes fruitful and gives satisfaction to the ancestors; the meaning is that in that birth the ancestral debt has been repaid.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

gayāmupetya yaḥ śrāddhaṃ karoti pṛthivīpate | saphalaṃ tasya tajjanma jāyate pitṛtuṣṭidam ||4||

tajjanma pitṛtuṣṭidaṃ sat tasmin janmani pitṝṇāmṛṇādṛttīrṇa ityarthaḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तज्जन्म पितृतुष्टिदं सत् तस्मिन् जन्मनि पितॄणामृणादृत्तीर्ण इत्यर्थः

Śrīdhara commentary.

The birth that reaches Gayā becomes fruitful and gives satisfaction to the ancestors; the meaning is that in that birth the ancestral debt has been repaid.

VP 3.16.5

प्रशान्तिकास्सनीवाराश्श्यामाका द्विविधास्तथा । वन्यौषधीप्रधानास्तु श्राद्धार्हाः पुरुषर्षभ

Praśāntika, Sanīvāra, and the two kinds of Śyāmaka, being chiefly wild plants, are suitable for śrāddha, O best of men.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

praśāntikāḥ sanīvārāḥ śyāmakāḥ dvi-vidhāḥ tathā — Praśāntika, Sanīvāra, and the two kinds of Śyāmaka; vanya-auṣadhi-pradhānāḥ tu — chiefly wild plants; śrāddha-arhāḥ puruṣarṣabha — are suitable for śrāddha, O best of men

Śrīdhara

śyāmākā dvividhāḥ śvetāḥ kृṣṇāśc vnyauṣdhiprdhānā vkṣymāṇāḥ — Śyāmaka are of two kinds, white and black; the wild plants that will be listed are the principal ones.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

praśāntikāssanīvārāśśyāmākā dvividhāstathā | vanyauṣadhīpradhānāstu śrāddhārhāḥ puruṣarṣabha ||5||

śyāmākā dvividhāḥ śvetāḥ kṛṣṇāśca vanyauṣadhipradhānā vakṣyamāṇāḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

श्यामाका द्विविधाः श्वेताः कृष्णाश्च वन्यौषधिप्रधाना वक्ष्यमाणाः

Śrīdhara commentary.

Śyāmaka are of two kinds, white and black; the wild plants that will be listed are the principal ones.

VP 3.16.6

यवाः प्रियगवो मुद्गा गोधूमा व्रीहयस्तिलाः । निष्पावाः कोविदाराश्च सर्षपाश्चात्र शोभनाः

Barley, Priyaṅgu, mung beans, wheat, rice, sesame, beans, Kovidāra, and mustard are wholesome here.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

yavāḥ priyaṅgavaḥ mudgāḥ godhūmāḥ vrīhayaḥ tilāḥ — barley, Priyaṅgu, mung beans, wheat, rice, and sesame; niṣpāvāḥ kovidārāḥ ca sarṣapāḥ ca — beans, Kovidāra, and mustard; atra śobhanāḥ — are wholesome here

Śrīdhara

priyṅgvḥ kṅgvḥ — Priyaṅgu is Kaṅgu grain; — rice grains ripened in autumn; — Niṣpāva beans; — Kovidāra trees and their fruits; — white mustard

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

yavāḥ priyagavo mudgā godhūmā vrīhayastilāḥ | niṣpāvāḥ kovidārāśca sarṣapāścātra śobhanāḥ ||6||

priyaṅgavaḥ kaṅgavaḥ | vrīhayaḥ śaratpakvāni dhānyāni | niṣpāvā śimbyaḥ | kovidārā vṛkṣāstaiśca tatphalāni lakṣyante | sarṣapāḥ śvetāḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

प्रियङ्गवः कङ्गवः । व्रीहयः शरत्पक्वानि धान्यानि । निष्पावा शिम्ब्यः । कोविदारा वृक्षास्तैश्च तत्फलानि लक्ष्यन्ते । सर्षपाः श्वेताः

Śrīdhara commentary.

Priyaṅgu means Kaṅgu; the rice grains are those ripened in autumn; niṣpāva means beans; Kovidāra refers to the trees and, by extension, their fruits; the mustard is white.

VP 3.16.7

अकृताग्रयणं यच्च धान्यजातं नरेश्वर । राजमाषाणानणूंश्चैव मसूरांश्च विवर्जयेत्

O king, one should avoid any grain for which the first-grain offering has not been performed, as well as Rājamāṣa, Aṇu, and lentils.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

akṛta-āgrayaṇam yat ca dhānya-jātam — any kind of grain for which the first-grain offering has not been performed; rāja-māṣān aṇūn ca eva — Rājamāṣa and Aṇu; masūrān ca vivarjayet — and lentils one should avoid

Śrīdhara

vrjyān āh akृtāgryṇm ityādinā pryuṣitm itynten — he states the things to be avoided, from “unperformed Agrayaṇa” through “stale”; — Agrayaṇa is the prescribed offering when new grain arrives; — what is done there is not [forbidden]; — kidney beans, uncultivated beans, small fine rice

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

akṛtāgrayaṇaṃ yacca dhānyajātaṃ nareśvara | rājamāṣāṇānaṇūṃścaiva masūrāṃśca vivarjayet ||7||

varjyānāha, akṛtāgrayaṇam ity-ādi nā paryuṣitamityantena | navaśasyāgame sāgnervihitā iṣṭirāgrayaṇam | tatra kṛtaṃ yanna tat | rājamāṣān akṛṣṭamāsān aṇūn sūkṣmaśālīn

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

वर्ज्यानाह, अकृताग्रयणम् इत्य्-आदि ना पर्युषितमित्यन्तेन । नवशस्यागमे साग्नेर्विहिता इष्टिराग्रयणम् । तत्र कृतं यन्न तत् । राजमाषान् अकृष्टमासान् अणून् सूक्ष्मशालीन्

Śrīdhara commentary.

He now states the prohibited items, from akṛtāgrayaṇa through paryuṣita. When new grain arrives, the Āgrayaṇa offering is prescribed with fire; grain for which that has not been done is prohibited. Rājamāṣa means uncultivated māṣa, and Aṇu means fine-grained rice.

VP 3.16.8

अलाबुं गृञ्जनं चैव पलाण्डुं पिण्डमूलकम् । गाम्धारककरंभाणि लवणान्यौपराणि च

Bottle gourd, green radish, onion, lump-root, Gāndhāraka, Karambha, and saline salts are to be avoided.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

alābum gṛñjanam ca eva palāṇḍum piṇḍa-mūlakam — bottle gourd, green radish, onion, and lump-root; gāndhāraka-karambhāṇi — Gāndhāraka and Karambha; lavaṇāni auṣarāṇi ca — and saline salts one should avoid

Śrīdhara

gृñjnṃ hritmūlkṃ — Gṛñjana is green radish; — palāṇḍu is a variety of garlic; — piṇḍa-mūlaka is a root shaped like a lump; — Gāndhāraka is a kind of vegetable, or fermented gruel; — some say Karambha means unpuffed parched grain flour; — auṣara means produced in saline soil

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

alābuṃ gṛñjanaṃ caiva palāṇḍuṃ piṇḍamūlakam | gāmdhārakakaraṃbhāṇi lavaṇānyauparāṇi ca ||8||

gṛñjanaṃ haritamūlakaṃ, palāṇḍaṃ laśunabhedaṃ, piṇḍamūlakaṃ piṇḍākāramūlakam | etenānyamūlakābhyanujñā āyāti, gāndhāraṃ śākabhedaḥ kāñjikaṃ vā | karambhāṇi avikāsitā lājāḥ śākabheda ityeke | auṣarāṇi ūṣarabhūmijātāni

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

गृञ्जनं हरितमूलकं, पलाण्डं लशुनभेदं, पिण्डमूलकं पिण्डाकारमूलकम् । एतेनान्यमूलकाभ्यनुज्ञा आयाति, गान्धारं शाकभेदः काञ्जिकं वा । करम्भाणि अविकासिता लाजाः शाकभेद इत्येके । औषराणि ऊषरभूमिजातानि

Śrīdhara commentary.

Gṛñjana is green radish; palāṇḍu is a variety of garlic; piṇḍa-mūlaka is a root shaped like a lump. Gāndhāraka is a kind of vegetable, or fermented gruel; Karambha means unpuffed parched grain flour, according to some; auṣara means produced in saline soil.

VP 3.16.9

आरक्ताश्चैव निर्यासाः प्रत्यक्षलवणानि च । वर्ज्यान्येतानि वै श्राद्धे यच्च वाचा न शस्यते

Naturally red tree resins, visible salts, and whatever is not praised by authoritative speech are to be avoided at śrāddha.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

āraktāḥ ca eva niryāsāḥ — naturally red tree resins; pratyakṣa-lavaṇāni ca — and visible salts; varjyāni etāni vai śrāddhe — these are indeed to be avoided at śrāddha; yat ca vācā na śasyate — and whatever is not praised by speech

Śrīdhara

niryāsā — tree resins; — tree juices; — naturally reddish; — directly visible salts; — salts perceptible to the eye; — and whatever is prescribed; — is not praised by speech; — the meaning is that it is censured

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

āraktāścaiva niryāsāḥ pratyakṣalavaṇāni ca | varjyānyetāni vai śrāddhe yacca vācā na śasyate ||9||

niryāsā vṛkṣarasā āraktāḥ svabhāvataḥ | pratyakṣalavaṇāni cakṣurdeśyalavaṇāni | yacca vihitamapi vācā na śasyate nindyataḥ ityarthaḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

निर्यासा वृक्षरसा आरक्ताः स्वभावतः । प्रत्यक्षलवणानि चक्षुर्देश्यलवणानि । यच्च विहितमपि वाचा न शस्यते निन्द्यतः इत्यर्थः

Śrīdhara commentary.

Niryāsa means tree resins that are naturally red; visible salts are salts perceptible to the eye. Whatever is prescribed but is not praised in authoritative speech is also meant to be avoided.

VP 3.16.10

नक्ताहृतमनुच्छिष्टं तृप्यते न च यत्र गौः । दुर्गन्धि फेनिलं चांबु श्राद्धयोग्यं न पार्थिव

Water brought at night, fresh water from an unestablished source, water where a cow is not satisfied, and foul-smelling or foamy water are not suitable for śrāddha, O king.
