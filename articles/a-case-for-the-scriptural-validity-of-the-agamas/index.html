1 Introduction

1.1 Preface & Core Objective

The Problem: While the Vedas are universally honored as Śruti (the highest revealed knowledge), the vast corpus of Āgamas has frequently been dismissed or demeaned as non-Vedic (avaidika), delusory, or deceitful due to misinterpretation and isolated proof-texting.

Purpose: To systematically establish the objective scriptural position of the Āgamas and demonstrate their inherent validity (Prāmāṇya) across all traditional Hindu streams.

Primary Methodology: Rooted in the primary authority of Śruti (Veda-Saṃhitās, Brāhmaṇas, Āraṇyakas, and Upaniṣads) and extended through Itihāsa, Purāṇa, and Smṛti. The framework subordinates post-scriptural human opinion to explicit scriptural injunctions and applies Mīmāṃsā hermeneutics (Samanvaya) to reconcile apparent contradictions rather than discarding texts.

2 Scriptural Extension of Authority & The Scope of Revelation

2.1 Expansion of Valid Authority Beyond the Four Vedas

First, let us examine the claim that the only valid scripture for Hindus is the Vedas. This premise is proven incorrect by the Vedas themselves.

Extension to Smṛti

In the Taittirīya Saṃhitā of the Yajurveda (2.2.10.2), Manusmṛti is explicitly referenced:

Show Sanskrit

यद् वै मनुर् अवदत् तद् भेषजम्

yad vai manur avadat tad bheṣajam

“Whatever Manu said is medicine.” Because the authority and validity of the Manusmṛti is established directly by the Veda, anyone rejecting the Manusmṛti must simultaneously reject the validity of the Veda itself. This is how the category of Śāstra (scripture) is extended to authoritative texts outside of Śruti.

Extension to Itihāsa and Purāṇa

In the Chāndogya Upaniṣad (7.1.4), it is stated:

Show Sanskrit

नाम वा ऋग्वेदो यजुर्वेदः सामवेद आथर्वणश् चतुर्थ इतिहास-पुराणः पञ्चमो वेदानां वेदः पित्र्यो राशिर् दैवो निधिर् वाकोवाक्यं एकायनं देवविद्या ब्रह्मविद्या भूतविद्या क्षत्रविद्या नक्षत्रविद्या सर्पदेवजनविद्या नामैवैतन्नामोपास्स्वेति ॥ ७.१.४ ॥

nāma vā ṛgvedo yajurvedaḥ sāmaveda ātharvaṇaś caturtha itihāsa-purāṇaḥ pañcamo vedānāṃ vedaḥ pitryo rāśir daivo nidhir vākovākyaṃ ekāyanaṃ devavidyā brahmavidyā bhūtavidyā kṣatravidyā nakṣatravidyā sarpadevajanavidyā nāmaivaitannāmopāssveti || 7.1.4 ||

“Name indeed is the Rigveda, the Yajurveda, the Samaveda, the Atharvaveda as the fourth, the Itihasa-Purana as the fifth among the Vedas...” Therefore, the category of the Veda—and Śruti-level authority by extension—includes the Itihāsas (such as the Rāmāyaṇa, Mahābhārata, and Śiva Rahasya Itihāsa) as well as the 36 Purāṇas codified by Vedavyāsa (consisting of the 18 Mahā-Purāṇas and 18 Upā-Purāṇas).

Validation of Smṛtis through Itihāsa and Purāṇa The validity of the Smṛtis is further established across multiple Purāṇas and Itihāsas. For instance, the Mahābhārata (Book 14, Aśvamedhika Parva, Chapter 96) enumerates an extensive lineage of authoritative Smṛtis:

  • Manu, Vasiṣṭha, Kaśyapa, Gārgya, Gautama, and Gopālaka: Codes of law authored by these ancient sages.
  • Parāśara, Maitreya, Umā, Maheśvara, and Nandi: Ancient teachings, including the holy doctrines of Umā and Maheśvara and the purifying laws of Nandi.
  • Brahmā, Sanatkumāra (Kaumāra), Dhūmāyana, and Kāṇḍa-Vaiśvānara: Divine laws and teachings spoken by Brahmā and other exalted figures.
  • Bhārgava, Yājñavalkya, Mārkaṇḍeya, Bharadvāja, and Bṛhaspati: Authoritative law codes composed by these prominent rishis.
  • Kuṇi, Kuṇibāhu, Viśvāmitra, Sumantu, Jaimini, and Śākuneya: Dharmic codes authored across various sage traditions.
  • Pulastya, Pulaha, Agni (Pāvakīya), Agastya, Mudgala, Śāṇḍilya, and Śalābhayana: Sacred laws sung by primary sages and divine figures.
  • Vālakhilyas, Saptarṣis (Seven Sages), Āpastamba, Śaṅkha, and Likhita: Essential legal and moral frameworks codified by these revered groups and seers.
  • Prajāpati, Yama, Mahendra, Vyāghra, Vyāsa, and Vibhaṇḍaka: Traditions handed down through divine and patriarchal lineages.
  • Nārada, Kapota, Vidura, Bhrigu, and Aṅgiras: Wise sayings, dharmic traditions, and foundational moral codes.
  • Krauñca, Mṛdaṅga, Sūrya (Solar Traditions), Hārīta, Piśaṅga, Kaṭapāya, and Suvālaka: Ancient sung traditions and specialized legal lineages.
  • Uddālaka, Uśanas (Śukrācārya), and Vaiśampāyana: Laws and teachings passed down through major classical lineages.

Furthermore, the Parāśara Smṛti—one of the Smṛtis explicitly mentioned in the Mahābhārata—enumerates additional canonical texts in verses 1.1.12–1.1.15:

“O Father, please tell me about Dharma (righteousness/duty), for I deserve your grace. I have already heard of the codes of law (dharmas) authored by Manu, Vasistha, and Kashyapa;”

“...as well as those remembered from Gargya, Gautama, Ushanas (Shukracharya), Atri, Vishnu, Samvarta, Daksha, and Angiras;”

“...and those proclaimed by Shatatapa, Harita, Yajnavalkya, Apastamba, Shankha, and Likhita;”

“...as well as those composed by Katyayana and the sage Prachetas. I have heard all these as taught by you, and the teachings of the Vedic texts have not been forgotten by me.”

Because several of these Smṛtis cross-reference even more text lineages, this canonical list is illustrative rather than exhaustive.

In the Annapūrṇā Upaniṣad (Chapter 3, Verse 21)—which is Śruti, belongs to the Atharvaveda, and is part of the Muktikā Canon of 108 Upaniṣads—the Śaiva Āgamas are not subordinated, but are explicitly identified as the ultimate conclusion of all Śāstras, including the Vedas themselves:

Show Sanskrit

शिवः शैवागमस्थाना कालः कालैकवादिनाम् । यत्सर्वशास्त्रसिद्धान्तं यत्सर्वहृदयानुगमम् ॥ २१ ॥

śivaḥ śaivāgamasthānā kālaḥ kālaikavādinām / yatsarvaśāstrasiddhāntaṃ yatsarvahṛdayānugamam || 21 ||

“What Shiva is to the followers of the Shaiva Agamas, and as Time to those who uphold Time as the ultimate truth. It is the ultimate conclusion of all scriptures and that which resonates within every heart.”

Furthermore, the Chāndogya Upaniṣad (7.1.2) references the Āgamas under another traditional designation, Ekayāna:

Show Sanskrit

ऋग्वेदं भगवोऽध्येमि यजुर्वेदं सामवेदम् आथर्वणं चतुर्थम् इतिहास-पुराणं पञ्चमं वेदानां वेदं पित्र्यं राशिं दैवं निधिम् वाकोवाक्यं एकायनं देवविद्यां ब्रह्मविद्यां भूतविद्यां क्षत्रविद्यां नक्षत्रविद्यां सर्पदेवजनविद्याम् एतद् भगवोऽध्येमि ॥ ७.१.२ ॥

ṛgvedaṃ bhagavo’dhyemi yajurvedaṃ sāmavedam ātharvaṇaṃ caturtham itihāsa-purāṇaṃ pañcamaṃ vedānāṃ vedaṃ pitryaṃ rāśiṃ daivaṃ nidhim vākovākyaṃ ekāyanaṃ devavidyāṃ brahmavidyāṃ bhūtavidyāṃ kṣatravidyāṃ nakṣatravidyāṃ sarpadevajanavidyām etad bhagavo’dhyemi || 7.1.2 ||

In his work Āgama Prāmāṇya, Yāmunācārya explicitly identifies Ekayāna as referring to the Kashmir-Āgamas, affirming them as apauruṣeya (authorless/eternal) and treating them as Śruti when examining this verse:

Show Sanskrit

एकायनशाखाया अपौरुषेयत्वं तथा काश्मीरागमप्रामाण्ये प्रपञ्चितम् इति नेह प्रस्तूयते.

ekāyanaśākhāyā apauruṣeyatvaṃ tathā kāśmīrāgamaprāmāṇye prapañcitam iti neha prastūyate.

“...the eternality/authoritativeness of the Ekayana Shakha has been detailed by me in [the work] Kashmira-Agama-Pramanya, so it is not discussed here.”

Pūrva Pakṣa Objection: The Ekayāna Śākhā only refers to Vaiṣṇava or Pāñcarātra works, not other Āgamas.

Refutation & Validation: The Āgamas are explicitly acknowledged and validated by Vedavyāsa himself in the Mahābhārata (Śānti Parva, Mokṣadharma Parva, Chapter 349, Verse 64). Vyāsa asserts that speculative reasoning (hetu) cannot be used to invalidate either the Pāśupata or Pāñcarātra texts along with the Vedas, as they possess intrinsic validity (ātmapramāṇa):

Show Sanskrit

साङ्ख्यं योगः पाञ्चरात्रं वेदाः पाशुपतं तथा । आत्मप्रमाणान्य् एतानि न हन्तव्यानि हेतुभिः ॥

sāṅkhyaṃ yogaḥ pāñcarātraṃ vedāḥ pāśupataṃ tathā / ātmapramāṇāny etāni na hantavyāni hetubhiḥ ||

“Sankhya, Yoga, Pancaratra, the Vedas, and the Pashupata—these are self-evident authorities (atmapramana) and should not be invalidated by logical arguments.”

Furthermore, prominent Ācāryas within Yāmunācārya’s own Śrī Vaiṣṇava lineage confirm that the scope of authoritative Āgamas extends beyond Vaiṣṇava texts. Vedānta Deśika, in his Pāñcarātra Rakṣā, explicitly accepts the validity of the Pāśupata Āgamas while commenting on the aforementioned Mahābhārata verse:

Show Sanskrit

अत एव भगवतापि शिवादिरूपान्तरैर् यथार्हाधिकारिफलविशेषार्थिभ्यः तदुपदेशाः कृताः.

ata eva bhagavatāpi śivādirūpāntarair yathārhādhikāriphalaviśeṣārthibhyaḥ tadupadeśāḥ kṛtāḥ.

“Accordingly, the Lord (as Siva) revealed the Sastra for the sake of those who sought particular fruits (phala-viśeṣārthibhyah.) and in accordance with their respective fitness.”

Show Sanskrit

ततश् च साङ्ख्यादीनां तत्र तत्र पृथगुपदिष्टफलविशेषप्राप्तिपरतया प्रामाण्यम् इति सिद्धम्.

tataś ca sāṅkhyādīnāṃ tatra tatra pṛthagupadiṣṭaphalaviśeṣaprāptiparatayā prāmāṇyam iti siddham.

“...it follows, therefore, that the Sankhya and other systems are valid for those who seek the particular results (phala-viśeṣāh.) mentioned in them.”

To reinforce this point, Vedānta Deśika cites Lord Śiva directly from the Ahirbudhnya Saṃhitā (12.4–5):

Show Sanskrit

साङ्ख्यं योगं तथैवान्यच्छास्त्रं यत् परमं तपः । विस्तरेण हि तस्योक्तं मयैव विदुरेति वै ॥

sāṅkhyaṃ yogaṃ tathaivānyacchāstraṃ yat paramaṃ tapaḥ / vistareṇa hi tasyoktaṃ mayaiva vidureti vai ||

“Sankhya, Yoga, and other systems which are supreme austerity — these were spoken at length by Me alone, O Vidura.” Thus, non-Vaiṣṇava Āgamas are explicitly acknowledged by Śrī Vaiṣṇava Ācāryas as part of the broader Āgamic tradition identified with the Ekayāna Śākhā.

In the Śiva Purāṇa (Kailāsa Saṃhitā, Chapter 21, Verses 9–10), the Śaiva Āgamas are explicitly prescribed by Lord Śiva through a Vidhi-Vākya (injunction) for aspirants seeking spiritual realization:

Show Sanskrit

अधैर्यचित्तो यः कश्चित् समाधिं न च विन्दति । तदुपायं प्रवक्ष्यामि सावधानतया शृणु ॥ ९ ॥

adhairyacitto yaḥ kaścit samādhiṃ na ca vindati / tadupāyaṃ pravakṣyāmi sāvadhānatayā śṛṇu || 9 ||

“If there is such a person devoid of concentration who does not attain Samādhi, I shall mention the means of remedy for him. Listen attentively.”

Show Sanskrit

त्रिपदार्थपरिज्ञानं वेदान्तागमवाक्यजम् । श्रुत्वा गुरोर्मुखाद् योगं अभ्यसेत् स यमादिकम् ॥ १० ॥

tripadārthaparijñānaṃ vedāntāgamavākyajam / śrutvā gurormukhād yogaṃ abhyaset sa yamādikam || 10 ||

“On hearing from his preceptor the lore of Vedānta and Āgamas along with the explanation, he shall practise Yoga by means of Yama, Niyama, etc.”

Even if one attempts to dismiss references to the Śaiva Āgamas within the Śiva Purāṇa under the pretext of sectarian categorization, the Padma Purāṇa (Bhūmi-Khaṇḍa, Chapter 69, Verse 1) independently designates the Śivadharma Āgamas as Uttama (supreme/excellent):

Show Sanskrit

अथ धर्माः शिवेनोक्ताः शिवधर्मागमोत्तमाः । ज्ञेया बहुविधास् ते च कर्मयोगप्रभेदतः ॥ हिंसादिदोषनिर्मुक्ताः क्लेशायासविवर्जिताः । सर्वभूतहिताः शुद्धाः सूक्ष्मायासा महत्फलाः ॥ १ ॥

atha dharmāḥ śivenoktāḥ śivadharmāgamottamāḥ / jñeyā bahuvidhās te ca karmayogaprabhedataḥ // hiṃsādidoṣanirmuktāḥ kleśāyāsavivarjitāḥ / sarvabhūtahitāḥ śuddhāḥ sūkṣmāyāsā mahatphalāḥ || 1 ||

“Now, the supreme righteous duties (dharmas) spoken by Lord Shiva in the excellent Śivadharma Agamas are to be known in their many variations according to the divisions of Karma Yoga. They are free from faults such as violence (hisā), devoid of affliction and extreme exertion, beneficial to all living beings, pure, requiring subtle effort, yet yielding immense fruits.”

In the Skanda Purāṇa (Sūta Saṃhitā, Yajña-vaibhava Khaṇḍa, Chapter 22), the Bhairava, Śaiva, and Pāśupata Āgamas—along with the Vaiṣṇava Āgamas (referring to the Pāñcarātra Āgamas) and even the Jain and Bauddha Agamas—are declared to possess validity (prāmāṇya) as principal Āgamas:

2.2 Recognition of the Āgamas across Itihāsa and Purāṇa

Show Sanskrit

अथातः सम्प्रवक्ष्यामि मार्गप्रामाण्यनिर्णयम् । श्रद्धया सहिता यूयं शृणुध्व मुनिसत्तमाः ॥ वेदाश् च धर्मशास्त्राणि पुराण-भारतं तथा । वेदाङ्गाद्युपवेदाश् च कामिकाद्यागमान् अपि ॥ कापालं नाकुलम् चैव तथोपादानं द्विजर्षभाः । तथा पाशुपतं सौम्यं भैरवप्रमुखान् आगमान् ॥ तेषाम् एवोपभेदाश् च शतशोऽथ सहस्रशः । विष्ण्वागमांस् तथा बाह्यान् बुद्धार्हदागमान् अपि ॥ लोकायतं तर्कशास्त्रं बहुविस्तारसयुतम् । मीमांसामतिगम्भीराः साङ्ख्ययोगौ तथैव च ॥ ... तान्येव विस्तरेणैव सङ्ग्रहेण वा पुनः । कुर्वन्ति तानि नामानि कथितानि महर्षिभिः । अधिकारिविभेदेन नैकरूपा हि सदा द्विजाः । तर्कैरेते हि मार्गास्तु न हन्तव्या मनीषिभिः ॥

athātaḥ sampravakṣyāmi mārgaprāmāṇyanirṇayam / śraddhayā sahitā yūyaṃ śṛṇudhva munisattamāḥ // vedāś ca dharmaśāstrāṇi purāṇa-bhārataṃ tathā / vedāṅgādyupavedāś ca kāmikādyāgamān api // kāpālaṃ nākulam caiva tathopādānaṃ dvijarṣabhāḥ / tathā pāśupataṃ saumyaṃ bhairavapramukhān āgamān // teṣām evopabhedāś ca śataśo’tha sahasraśaḥ / viṣṇvāgamāṃs tathā bāhyān buddhārhadāgamān api // lokāyataṃ tarkaśāstraṃ bahuvistārasayutam / mīmāṃsāmatigambhīrāḥ sāṅkhyayogau tathaiva ca // ... tānyeva vistareṇaiva saṅgraheṇa vā punaḥ / kurvanti tāni nāmāni kathitāni maharṣibhiḥ / adhikārivibhedena naikarūpā hi sadā dvijāḥ / tarkairete hi mārgāstu na hantavyā manīṣibhiḥ ||

“Now I shall explain the decision regarding the authoritativeness of the paths (mārga). O best of sages, listen with faith! The Vedas, Dharma-shastras, Puranas, the Bharata (Mahabharata), the Vedangas, Upavedas, the Kamika and other Agamas, the Kapala, Nakula, Upadana, the Pashupata, Saumya, and Bhairava—these major Agamas, along with their sub-sects by the hundreds and thousands; the Vaishnava Agamas, and even the external Buddhist and Jaina Agamas, the Lokayata system, Logic (Tarka-shastra), the deep Mimamsa, and Sankhya and Yoga—the great sages have given names to these paths and taught them in detail or in brief according to the varying eligibility (adhikāra) of seekers. These paths should not be invalidated by wise men through logical arguments.”

Specific emphasis is laid on the principle that each of these Āgamic systems is articulated according to the varying adhikāra (competence/qualification) of seekers. This framework of Adhikāra-Bheda is central to addressing passages in certain Purāṇas that designate particular Āgamas as mohaka or delusory.

Pūrva Pakṣa Objection: This verse from the Skanda Purāṇa is merely Arthavāda (praise/exaggeration) and cannot be taken literally.

Refutation: The notion that statements within the Purāṇas can be casually dismissed as Arthavāda is explicitly rejected in scripture. The Nārada Purāṇa (Pūrva-Khaṇḍa, Chapter 1, Verses 57–58) strictly prohibits reading Arthavāda into the Purāṇas:

Show Sanskrit

पुराणेष्व् अर्थवादत्वं ये वदन्ति नराधमाः ॥ १.५७ ॥ तैर् अर्जितानि पुण्यानि क्षयं यान्ति द्विजोत्तमाः । समस्तकर्मनिर्मूलसाधनानि नराधमाः ॥ १.५८ ॥ पुराणान्य् अर्थवादेन ब्रुवन्न् अरकम् अश्नुते ॥

purāṇeṣv arthavādatvaṃ ye vadanti narādhamāḥ || 1.57 || tair arjitāni puṇyāni kṣayaṃ yānti dvijottamāḥ / samastakarmanirmūlasādhanāni narādhamāḥ || 1.58 || purāṇāny arthavādena bruvann arakam aśnute ||

“Those lowest among men who claim that the Purāṇas contain Arthavāda—all their accumulated merit and virtue are destroyed, O best of dvijas. By declaring the Purāṇas to be Arthavāda, such wretched men go to hell.”

Pūrva Pakṣa Objection: The Skanda Purāṇa is classified as a Tāmasika Purāṇa (e.g., in the Padma Purāṇa) and its statements are therefore non-authoritative.

Refutation: This line of objection creates a mutual contradiction. While the Padma Purāṇa classifies the Skanda Purāṇa as Tāmasika, the Skanda Purāṇa itself (Śaṅkara Saṃhitā, Dakṣa Khaṇḍa) conversely classifies the Vaiṣṇava Purāṇas—including the Padma Purāṇa—as Tāmasika:

Show Sanskrit

वैष्णवानि च चत्वारि तामसानि मनीश्वराः.

vaiṣṇavāni ca catvāri tāmasāni manīśvarāḥ.

“O foremost of sages! The four Vaishnava Puranas are considered Tamasic. In them, the duties of the Kshatriyas (warrior class) are heard, and Hari (Vishnu) is their presiding deity.”

Relying on one text to invalidate another when both make reciprocal claims results in Ātmāśraya (circular reasoning/self-dependence), which is an unacceptable defect in classical hermeneutics. According to Pūrva Mīmāṃsā hermeneutics, scriptures must be interpreted through inherent authority rather than selective rejection. As stated in the Mīmāṃsā Sūtras (1.1.5):

Show Sanskrit

औत्पत्तिकस् तु शब्दस्यार्थेन सम्बन्धः । तस्य ज्ञानम् उपदेशो ऽव्यतिरेकश् चार्थे ऽनुपलब्धे । तत्प्रमाणं बादरायणस्यानपेक्षत्वात् ॥ ५ ॥

autpattikas tu śabdasyārthena sambandhaḥ / tasya jñānam upadeśo ’vyatirekaś cārthe ’nupalabdhe / tatpramāṇaṃ bādarāyaṇasyānapekṣatvāt || 5 ||

“On the other hand, the relation of the word with its meaning is inborn and eternal; consequently, injunction is the means of knowing dharma, and it is unfailing in regard to objects not perceived by other means of knowledge. It is authoritative, according to Bādarāyaṇa, especially as it is independent and self-sufficient in its authority.”

Therefore, discarding explicit scriptural statements by labeling them as ”absurd” or Tāmasika violates the foundational principle of Anapekṣatva (independent self-sufficiency of scriptural authority).

Pūrva Pakṣa Objection: The Śaṅkara Saṃhitā and Sūta Saṃhitā are not legitimate parts of the Skanda Purāṇa.

Context of the Objection: It is true that standard modern print editions (such as those from Gita Press) structure the Skanda Purāṇa into seven Khaṇḍas totaling 81,000 verses. This structure

is referenced in the Nārada Purāṇa (Chapter 104, Verse 3), where Lord Brahmā states:

Show Sanskrit

स्कन्दाह्वयस् तत्र खण्डाः सप्तैव परिकल्पिताः । एकाशीतिसहस्रं तु स्कान्दं सर्वोघकृन्तनम् ॥ ३ ॥

skandāhvayas tatra khaṇḍāḥ saptaiva parikalpitāḥ / ekāśītisahasraṃ tu skāndaṃ sarvoghakṛntanam || 3 ||

“In that Purāṇa named Skanda, seven sections (khaṇḍas) have been designed. The Skanda Purāṇa consists of eightyone thousand (ekāśītisahasram) verses, and it destroys the entire accumulation of sins.”

Refutation & Textual Evidence: Lord Brahmā’s statement in the Nārada Purāṇa enumerates a specific recension of 81,000 verses, but nowhere does it state that this represents the absolute limit or entirety of the Skanda Purāṇa corpus. The internal testimony of the Skanda Purāṇa’s own Saṃhitā tradition affirms a larger, expanded recension of 100,000 verses across six Saṃhitās:

Show Sanskrit

स्वतन्त्रम् एकम् अद्वैतं प्रवदन्ति निरङ्कुशम् । तत्र स्कान्दं प्रवक्ष्यामि पुराणेषु त्रयोदशम् ॥ ४४ ॥ सहिताभिः तथा षड्भिः लक्षैर् ग्रन्थैर् विराजितम् । दश चैव पुराणानि सात्त्विकानि विदुर् बुधाः ॥ ४५ ॥

svatantram ekam advaitaṃ pravadanti niraṅkuśam / tatra skāndaṃ pravakṣyāmi purāṇeṣu trayodaśam || 44 || sahitābhiḥ tathā ṣaḍbhiḥ lakṣair granthair virājitam / daśa caiva purāṇāni sāttvikāni vidur budhāḥ || 45 ||

“They declare Him to be independent, one without a second (non-dual), and completely unrestricted. Among those Purāṇas, I shall now narrate the Skanda Purāṇa, which is the thirteenth. It shines brilliantly with six Saṃhitās and contains one hundred thousand verses. Wise scholars know that ten of the Purāṇas are Sāttvika.”

Combining the verse counts of the extant Saṃhitās (the Sūta Saṃhitā and Śaṅkara Saṃhitā) with the seven Khaṇḍas yields precisely 100,000 verses, matching the internal verse count specified within the Saṃhitā text itself. Furthermore, this 100,000-verse recension is explicitly recognized and validated by other major Purāṇas. The Bhaviṣya Purāṇa (Brāhma Parva, 1.1.103–105) explicitly states:

Show Sanskrit

सर्वाण्य् एव पुराणानि संज्ञेयानि नरर्षभ । द्वादशैव सहस्राणि प्रोक्तानीह मनीषिभिः ॥ १०३ ॥ पुनर्वृद्धिं गतानीह आख्यानैर् विविधैर् वृष । यथा स्कान्दं तथा चेदं भविष्यं कुरुनन्दन ॥ १०४ ॥ स्कान्दं शतसहस्रं तु लोकानां ज्ञातम् एव हि । भविष्यम् एतद् ऋषिणा लक्षार्धं संख्यया कृतम् ॥ १०५ ॥

sarvāṇy eva purāṇāni saṃjñeyāni nararṣabha / dvādaśaiva sahasrāṇi proktānīha manīṣibhiḥ || 103 || punarvṛddhiṃ gatānīha ākhyānair vividhair vṛṣa / yathā skāndaṃ tathā cedaṃ bhaviṣyaṃ kurunandana || 104 || skāndaṃ śatasahasraṃ tu lokānāṃ jñātam eva hi / bhaviṣyam etad ṛṣiṇā lakṣārdhaṃ saṃkhyayā kṛtam || 105 ||

“All the Purāṇas were originally known to consist of twelve thousand verses by the wise, O best of men. Later, they expanded through various narratives—just like the Skanda Purāṇa and this Bhaviṣya Purāṇa, O descendant of Kuru. The Skanda Purāṇa consists of one hundred thousand (śatasahasram) verses; indeed, this is well known among the people.”

Historical transmission further corroborates the antiquity and authority of the Saṃhitā tradition. The Kānda Purāṇam, authored in the 11th century CE by the Tamil Śaiva Ācārya Kacchiyappa Śivāchāriyār, is a direct adaptation of the Śaṅkara and Sūta Saṃhitās of the Skanda Purāṇa. This precedes the earliest citations of the Khaṇḍa recension found in the works of 13th-century commentators such as Rāmānujācārya and Madhvācārya. Consequently, both the Khaṇḍa and Saṃhitā recensions are authentic, historically transmitted branches of the Skanda Purāṇa corpus, and both possess full scriptural authority (prāmāṇya).

Pūrva Pakṣa Objection: Vedavyāsa’s Brahma Sūtras mandate the complete rejection of the Pāñcarātra and Pāśupata Āgamas.

Refutation & Contextual Analysis: The text of the Brahma Sūtras itself does not state that the Āgamas are to be rejected as a whole. Rather, Brahma Sūtra 2.2.42 specifically addresses a single cosmological point regarding origin:

Show Sanskrit

उत्पत्त्यसम्भवात् ॥ ४२ ॥

utpattyasambhavāt || 42 ||

“On account of the impossibility of origination.”

The sūtra targets a literal reading wherein the individual soul (Jīva) is generated or created—a notion contradicted by Śruti. While Ādi Śaṅkarācārya’s commentary on the Brahma Sūtras evaluates philosophical positions of the Pāśupata and Pāñcarātra schools, Śaṅkarācārya explicitly upholds the devotional and ritual validity (Pañcakāla Kriyā) prescribed in the Pāñcarātra Āgamas:

Show Sanskrit

...इति, तन् न निराक्रियते, “स एकधा भवति त्रिधा भवति” इत्यादिश्रुतिभ्यः परमात्मनोऽनेकधाभावस्याधिगतत्वात् । यद् अपि तस्य भगवतोऽभिगमनादिलक्षणम् आराधनम् अजस्रम् अनन्यचित्ततयाभिप्रेयते, तद् अपि न प्रतिषिध्यते, श्रुतिस्मृत्योर् ईश्वरप्रणिधानस्य प्रसिद्धत्वात् । यत् पुनर् इदम् उच्यते—वासुदेवात् सङ्कर्षण उत्पद्यते, सङ्कर्षणाच् च प्रद्युम्नः, प्रद्युम्नाच् चानिरुद्ध इति...

...iti, tan na nirākriyate, “sa ekadhā bhavati tridhā bhavati” ityādiśrutibhyaḥ paramātmano’nekadhābhāvasyādhigatatvāt / yad api tasya bhagavato’bhigamanādilakṣaṇam ārādhanam ajasram ananyacittatayābhipreyate, tad api na pratiṣidhyate, śrutismṛtyor īśvarapraṇidhānasya prasiddhatvāt / yat punar idam ucyate—vāsudevāt saṅkarṣaṇa utpadyate, saṅkarṣaṇāc ca pradyumnaḥ, pradyumnāc cāniruddha iti...

“...that is not refuted, from scriptural texts (Śruti) such as ‘He becomes one-fold, he becomes three-fold...’ (Chāndogya Upaniṣad 7.26.2), because the state of the Supreme Self (Paramātman) existing in manifold forms is well understood. Even though worship of the Lord characterized by approaching Him continuously with undivided focus is intended, that too is not prohibited, because devotion to the Supreme Lord (Īśvara-praṇidhāna) is well-established in both Śruti and Smṛti scriptures. As for what is further stated—that Saṅkarṣaṇa is born from Vāsudeva, Pradyumna from Saṅkarṣaṇa, and Aniruddha from Pradyumna...”

Furthermore, Ādi Śaṅkarācārya directly acknowledges the tradition of the 64 Bhairava Āgamas in Verse 31 of the Saundarya Laharī:

चतुःषष्ट्या तन्त्रैः सकलम् अतिसन्धाय भुवनं स्थितस् तत्तत् सिद्धिप्रसवपरतन्त्रैः पशुपतिः । पुनस् त्वन्निर्बन्धाद् अखिलपुरुषार्थैकघटना-स्वतन्त्रं ते तन्त्रं क्षितितलम् अवातीतरद् इदम् ॥ ३१ ॥

catuḥṣaṣṭyā tantraiḥ sakalam atisandhāya bhuvanaṃ sthitas tattat siddhiprasavaparatantraiḥ paśupatiḥ / punas tvannirbandhād akhilapuruṣārthaikaghaṭanā-svatantraṃ te tantraṃ kṣititalam avātītarad idam || 31 ||

Pūrva Pakṣa Objection: The Brahma Sūtras declare the Pāśupata and Śaiva Āgamas to be non-Vedic (Avaidika) and completely invalid.

Refutation & Hermeneutical Context: To properly address this objection, one must distinguish between the direct text of the Brahma Sūtras and the later commentary traditions. The sūtra frequently cited in this debate is Brahma Sūtra 2.2.45:

Show Sanskrit

विप्रतिषेधाच् च ॥ ४५ ॥

vipratiṣedhāc ca || 45 ||

“And on account of contradictions.”

When read in its proper contextual sequence (prakaraṇa) alongside the preceding sūtras (Brahma Sūtras 2.2.42–44: utpattyasambhavāt, na ca kartuh. karaṇam, and vijñānādibhāve vā tadapratiṣedhah.), this discussion addresses specific metaphysical formulations (such as the origination of the Jīva) rather than issuing a total rejection of the Āgamic corpus itself.

This contextual interpretation aligns directly with the hermeneutical principles of Pūrva Mīmāṃsā Sūtra 1.2.24:

Show Sanskrit

प्रकरणे सम्भवन्न् अपकर्षो न कल्प्येत, विध्यानर्थक्यं हि सम्प्रति ॥ २४ ॥

prakaraṇe sambhavann apakarṣo na kalpyeta, vidhyānarthakyaṃ hi samprati || 24 ||

“In the case of that which is compatible with its own context, detachment or removal should not be assumed; for otherwise, the injunction itself would become meaningless.”

Even in commentators who vigorously debate rival metaphysical positions, the validity of Āgamic worship and practice remains intact. As Śaṅkarācārya himself notes in his commentary on the adjacent sūtras: tanna nirākriyate — “That is not refuted.” Furthermore, responding to apparent philosophical dualisms between Śruti and Āgama, the Śaiva Ācārya Jayaratha, in his commentary Tantrālokaviveka on Abhinavagupta’s Tantrāloka, expounds on the secret nature of Brahman nahi parasyāṃ sam. vidi tadubhayasya sthūlasūkṣmarūpasya kaścidvirodhaḥ — “In the Supreme Consciousness, there is no contradiction whatsoever between those two forms—the gross and the subtle.” The dialectical description of the Supreme Being as transcending standard logical categories is not a defect or contradiction, but a foundational feature of Brahman. In the Bhagavad Gītā (Chapter 13, Verse 13), Lord Kṛṣṇa Himself defines the nature of Brahman using these exact paradoxes:

Show Sanskrit

ज्ञेयं यत् तत् प्रवक्ष्यामि यज् ज्ञात्वामृतम् अश्नुते । अनादि मत्-परं ब्रह्म न सत् तन् नासद् उच्यते ॥ १३ ॥

jñeyaṃ yat tat pravakṣyāmi yaj jñātvāmṛtam aśnute / anādi mat-paraṃ brahma na sat tan nāsad ucyate || 13 ||

“I shall now describe that which is to be known, knowing which one attains immortality. It is the beginningless Supreme Brahman, which is said to be neither being (sat) nor non-being (asat).”

Similarly, the Kālikā Purāṇa (Chapter 1, Verses 19–20) employs identical language to describe the transcendent and immanent nature of the Supreme Reality:

Show Sanskrit

व्यक्ताव्यक्तस्वरूपाय सदसद्व्यक्तरूपिणे । स्थूलाय सूक्ष्मरूपाय विश्वरूपाय वेधसे ॥ १९ ॥ नित्याय नित्यज्ञानाय निर्विकाराय चेतसे । विद्याऽविद्यास्वरूपाय कलारूपाय वै नमः ॥ २० ॥

vyaktāvyaktasvarūpāya sadasadvyaktarūpiṇe / sthūlāya sūkṣmarūpāya viśvarūpāya vedhase || 19 || nityāya nityajñānāya nirvikārāya cetase / vidyā’vidyāsvarūpāya kalārūpāya vai namaḥ || 20 ||

“Salutations to the Creator, whose nature is both manifest (vyakta) and unmanifest (avyakta), who embodies both being (sat) and non-being (asat); who is gross (sthūla) yet subtle (sūkṣma), and whose form is the entire universe; who is eternal, possessing eternal knowledge, unchanging consciousness, and who embodies both knowledge (vidyā) and ignorance (avidyā) as well as all creative arts (kalā).”

Far from indicating incoherence, these descriptions express the absolute transcendence of Brahman/Śakti beyond dualistic conceptual frameworks. They affirm that empirical logic based on mundane categories cannot invalidate the self-evident authority (svataḥprāmāṇya) of divine revelation across the Śruti, Purāṇa, and Āgama literature.

Pūrva Pakṣa Objection: The Pāśupata doctrine is inherently non-Vedic (Avaidika) and encourages practices opposed to Vedic orthodoxy.

Refutation & Canonical Evidence: This objection fails to account for the foundational texts of the Pāśupata tradition itself, which explicitly uphold the authority of the Vedas and the traditional Āśrama system. In the Pāśupata Sūtras (4.20), accompanied by Kaunḍinya’s commentary (Pañcārtha Bhāṣya), the trajectory of liberation for a Vedic practitioner is unambiguously defined:

Show Sanskrit

न कश्चिद् ब्राह्मणः पुनरावर्तते ॥ २० ॥

na kaścid brāhmaṇaḥ punarāvartate || 20 ||

“No Brāhmaṇa whatsoever returns again [to mundane existence].”

In his authoritative commentary, Kauṇḍinya details the exact scope and Vedic alignment of this sūtra:

Show Sanskrit

अत्र नकारोपदेशोऽन्याचरणप्रतिपत्तिप्रतिषेधार्थः — कश्चिद् इति गृहस्थाद्यः — स्थानमात्रवैलक्षण्यदर्शनाद् ब्राह्मणेष्व् एव कश्चिच्छब्दः — गृहस्थो ब्रह्मचारी वानप्रस्थो भिक्षुर् एकवेदो द्विवेदस् त्रिवेदश् चतुर्वेदश् च गायत्रीमात्रसारो वानेन विधिना रुद्रसमीपं प्राप्तः सन् न कश्चिद् ब्राह्मणः पुनर् आवर्तत इत्य् अर्थः — ब्राह्मणग्रहणं ब्राह्मण्यावधारणार्थम्, ब्राह्मण एव नान्य इत्य् अर्थः — क्षेत्रज्ञे च ब्राह्मणसंज्ञा — कस्मात्? उपचयजन्मयोगात् संस्कारयोगात्, श्रुतियोगाच् च ब्राह्मणः — पुनःशब्दः पुनरावृत्तिप्रतिषेधे — यथा पूर्वं संज्ञानादिभिर् गत्वा आवर्तते पुनः पुनः, तथानेन विधिना रुद्रसमीपं गत्वा न सकृद् आवर्तते — पुनः पुनः सर्वथापि नावर्तत इत्य् अर्थः — आङ् इति स्वशास्त्रोक्थमर्यादम् अधिकुरुते, अभिविध्यर्थं च — ये चानेन विधिना क्षपिताज्ञानकलुषपापमायादयः क्षीणास् ते पुनः पुनर् आवर्तन्ते — न तैः सह संयोगो भवति, न चापरं जन्म प्रतिपद्यत इत्य् अर्थः ॥ २० ॥

atra nakāropadeśo’nyācaraṇapratipattipratiṣedhārthaḥ — kaścid iti gṛhasthādyaḥ — sthānamātravailakṣaṇyadarśanād brāhmaṇeṣv eva kaścicchabdaḥ — gṛhastho brahmacārī vānaprastho bhikṣur ekavedo dvivedas trivedaś caturvedaś ca gāyatrīmātrasāro vānena vidhinā rudrasamīpaṃ prāptaḥ san na kaścid brāhmaṇaḥ punar āvartata ity arthaḥ — brāhmaṇagrahaṇaṃ brāhmaṇyāvadhāraṇārtham, brāhmaṇa eva nānya ity arthaḥ — kṣetrajñe ca brāhmaṇasaṃjñā — kasmāt? upacayajanmayogāt saṃskārayogāt, śrutiyogāc ca brāhmaṇaḥ — punaḥśabdaḥ punarāvṛttipratiṣedhe — yathā pūrvaṃ saṃjñānādibhir gatvā āvartate punaḥ punaḥ, tathānena vidhinā rudrasamīpaṃ gatvā na sakṛd āvartate — punaḥ punaḥ sarvathāpi nāvartata ity arthaḥ — āṅ iti svaśāstrokthamaryādam adhikurute, abhividhyarthaṃ ca — ye cānena vidhinā kṣapitājñānakaluṣapāpamāyādayaḥ kṣīṇās te punaḥ punar āvartante — na taiḥ saha saṃyogo bhavati, na cāparaṃ janma pratipadyata ity arthaḥ || 20 ||

“Here, the instruction of the negative particle (na) is intended to prohibit the adoption of any other conduct. The word kaścid (‘anyone’) refers to householders and others. Because the distinction is seen only in their stage of life (Āśrama), the word kaścid applies exclusively among the Brāhmaṇas. The meaning is that a householder (gṛhastha), a celibate student (brahmacārī), a forest-dweller (vānaprastha), or a mendicant (bhikṣu)—whether he knows one Veda, two Vedas, three Vedas, four Vedas, or has only the Gāyatrī mantra as his sole essence—having attained the proximity of Lord Rudra by this prescribed method, no such Brāhmaṇa returns. The use of the word Brāhmaṇa is to firmly restrict it to Brāhmaṇahood; meaning, a Brāhmaṇa alone and no one else. Furthermore, the designation Brāhmaṇa is applied to the Kṣetrajña (the conscious soul). Why? Because one becomes a Brāhmaṇa through the association with noble birth, the association with purifying sacraments (saskāras), and the association with Vedic learning (śruti). The word punah. (‘again’) is used to prohibit rebirth. Just as previously, having departed through mere worldly knowledge, one returns again and again, similarly, having approached Lord Rudra by this method, one does not return even once. Under no circumstances does one return again. The prefix Āṅ denotes the limit stated in one’s own scriptures and also signifies inclusive extension. Those whose ignorance, impurities, and māyā have been exhausted by this prescribed method do not return again. There is no further association with them, nor do they attain another birth. This is the meaning.” This foundational text proves that the Pāśupata system explicitly integrates Vedic learning (śruti-yoga), sacraments (saskāra-yoga), and traditional Āśrama stages as valid bases for spiritual practice and final liberation (duḥkhānta). Consequently, labeling the Pāśupata doctrine as fundamentally anti-Vedic contradicts its own primary canonical source.

Refutation & Hermeneutical Resolution: This conclusion is untenable when subjected to rigorous textual and Mīmāsaka analysis: 1. Specificity vs. Universal Invalidation: The text explicitly uses terms such as moha-śāstrāṇi, kuhaka, indrajālāni, and viruddhācaraṇa. Nowhere does the text make a blanket assertion that the 28 canonical Śaiva Āgamas broadly constitute these moha-śāstras. 2. Exegesis of Verse 70.43 and Adhikāra-Bheda: A close reading of Verse 70.43 reveals the core intent of the passage:

Show Sanskrit

तदा पाशुपतं शास्त्रं जायते वेदसंज्ञितम् ॥ ४३ ॥

tadā pāśupataṃ śāstraṃ jāyate vedasaṃjñitam || 43 ||

The term patita refers to an individual who has fallen or degraded from the standard Vedic social and religious order. For such a person who is outside ordinary Vedic eligibility, the Pāśupata Śāstra assumes a Veda-like function (Veda-sajñita). This provides primary Puranic evidence for the principle of Adhikāra- Bheda (differential qualification), demonstrating that Āgamic revelation accommodates seekers who are otherwise disconnected from direct Vedic injunctions. 3. Mīmāṃsā Interpretation of Designation: The term Vedasajñita cannot be casually discarded as empty praise or figure of speech. As established in Pūrva Mīmāṃsā Sūtra 1.4.2:

अपि वा नामधेयं स्याद् यदुत्पत्ताव् अपूर्वम् अविधायकत्वात् ॥ २ ॥

api vā nāmadheyaṃ syād yadutpattāv apūrvam avidhāyakatvāt || 2 ||

“Rather, it should be recognized as a proper name/designation (nāmadheya), since it applies to an original subject and does not function as an injunction.”

Because Veda-sajñita serves as a formal designation (nāmadheya) within the verse, it possesses direct interpretative value and cannot be dismissed as mere arthavāda—a position further fortified by the Nārada Purāṇa’s explicit prohibition against reading arthavāda into Puranic statements. Thus, far from disproving Āgamic validity, the Varāha Purāṇa affirms that the Pāśupata doctrine functions with Veda-like authority for its intended practitioners, perfectly aligned with the framework of Adhikāra-Bheda.

Refutation & Hermeneutical Re-interpretation: However, in the same passage, the purpose of Lord Viṣṇu and Lord Śiva creating these scriptures is explicitly explained:

Here, Mohaka does not mean malicious delusion; rather, Mohaka can also signify enchantment or allure. The correct interpretation of these verses is that Lord Śiva and Lord Viṣṇu, through their grace and mercy, created the Āgamas to allure people back toward the Vedas (Veda-mārga). This underlying harmony is expressed in the Āgamas themselves, such as in the Sarvollāsa Tantra (Chapter 7):

Pūrva Pakṣa Objection: The Kūrma Purāṇa asserts that Tantrikas attain Naraka for many kalpas.

Analysis of the Objection Verse: The objection relies on Verse 114 of the Kūrma Purāṇa:

Show Sanskrit

सृष्ट्वा तान् ऊचतुर् देवौ कुर्वाणाः शास्त्रचोदितम् । पतन्तो निरये घोरे बहून् कल्पान् पुनः पुनः ॥ ११४ ॥

sṛṣṭvā tān ūcatur devau kurvāṇāḥ śāstracoditam / patanto niraye ghore bahūn kalpān punaḥ punaḥ || 114 ||

“Having created them, the two gods said: ‘Those who act according to the injunctions of these śāstras, falling into terrible hell, [remain there] for many kalpas again and again.’”

Refutation & Hermeneutical Re-interpretation:

1. Semantic Re-evaluation via Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya Consider this alternative textual reading (pāṭha-antara): in medical treatises

such as the Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya, the phrase bahū-kalpa does not denote ”many kalpas” (cosmic time cycles), but rather ”of many methods” or therapeutic procedures. As is well established, the Āgamas function as an upāya (expedient means) and consist of diverse methods—including Pañcarātra, Śaiva, Pāśupata, etc. This usage is attested in Verse 28:

Show Sanskrit

बहुकल्पं बहुगुणं सम्पन्नं योग्यम् औषधम् ॥ २८ ॥

bahukalpaṃ bahuguṇaṃ sampannaṃ yogyam auṣadham || 28 ||

2. Re-ordering of Syntax (Pāṭha-krama) via Mīmāṃsā Rules We can re-arrange the word order of Verse 114 in accordance with Pūrva Mīmāṃsā Sūtra 5.4.1:

Show Sanskrit

क्रमकोपोऽर्थशब्दानां श्रुतिविशेषाद् अर्थपरत्वाच् च ॥ ५.४.१ ॥

kramakopo’rthaśabdānāṃ śrutiviśeṣād arthaparatvāc ca || 5.4.1 ||

“There is a disruption (modification) of the textual sequence of action-denoting words/mantras, because of explicit scriptural statement and because these words exist primarily to serve the execution of the action.”

However, a modification of textual sequence (pāṭha-krama) can only be invoked if there is a logical impossibility (anupapatti) in the literal reading of the verse. In Chapter 6, Verse 12 of the Hārīta Smṛti, it is stated:

Show Sanskrit

महाभागवतो विप्रस् तान्त्रिकः सर्वकर्मसु ॥ १२ ॥

mahābhāgavato vipras tāntrikaḥ sarvakarmasu || 12 ||

“A most exalted devotee of the Lord, a learned scholar, and is a Tantric adept in all actions.”

Furthermore, Chapter 1, Verse 26 states:

Show Sanskrit

अचक्रधारी यो विप्रो बहुवेदश्रुतोऽपि वा । स जीवन्नेव चण्डालो मृतो निरयम् आप्नुयात् ॥ २६ ॥

acakradhārī yo vipro bahuvedaśruto’pi vā / sa jīvanneva caṇḍālo mṛto nirayam āpnuyāt || 26 ||

“Whether a learned scholar or well-versed in many Vedas, if a person does not wear the mark of the Cakra (discus), they live as an outcast and, upon death, attain hell.”

The practice of wearing the Cakra (Cakra-dhāraṇa) is exclusive to the Pañcarātra Āgamas. If Verse 114 from the Kūrma Purāṇa were taken literally to suggest that following the Pañcarātra Āgamas leads to hell, it would create a direct contradiction with the Hārīta Smṛti. Therefore, the re-arranged and correct pāṭha-krama of this verse is:

Show Sanskrit

सृष्ट्वा तान् ऊचतुर् देवौ — पुनः पुनः घोरे निरये पतन्तः [जनाः], शास्त्रचोदितम् कुर्वाणाः बहून् कल्पान् [लभन्ते] —

sṛṣṭvā tān ūcatur devau — punaḥ punaḥ ghore niraye patantaḥ [janāḥ], śāstracoditam kurvāṇāḥ bahūn kalpān [labhante] —

“Having created them, the two gods declared: ’For those people who fall again and again into the terrible hell [of worldly suffering], performing that which is enjoined by these scriptures provides many therapeutic methods (bahūn kalpān).’”

3. Resolution via Smṛti Superiority Even if this contextual re-interpretation of the Kūrma Purāṇa is not accepted, Vyāsa Smṛti (Chapter 1, Verse 4) explicitly rules that when a conflict arises between a Purāṇa and a Smṛti, the statements of the Smṛti take precedence:

Show Sanskrit

śrutismṛtipurāṇānāṃ virodho yatra dṛśyate / tatra śrautaṃ pramāṇaṃ tu tayor dvaidhe smṛtir varā || 4 ||

“Where a contradiction is observed among Śruti, Smṛti, and Purāṇa, the Śruti is the supreme authority; in a conflict between Smṛti and Purāṇa, the Smṛti is superior.”

Pūrva Pakṣa Objection: The Āgamas are not the Vedas, and therefore they lack scriptural authority (prāmāṇya).

Establishing Śabda-Pramāṇa for the Āgamas: To establish the validity of the Āgamas, we must first examine the nature of valid knowledge (jñāna) as the sole means to liberation, and then demonstrate how the Āgamas qualify as valid verbal testimony (śabda-pramāṇa).

Jnāna as the Sole Means to Liberation: In the Ātmabodha (Verse 2), Ādi Śaṅkarācārya defines jñāna as the direct and indispensable cause of liberation:

Show Sanskrit

बोधोऽन्यसाधनेभ्यो हि साक्षान् मोक्षैकसाधनम् । पाकस्य वह्नितज्ज्ञानं विना मोक्षो न सिध्यति ॥ २ ॥

bodho’nyasādhanebhyo hi sākṣān mokṣaikasādhanam / pākasya vahnitajjñānaṃ vinā mokṣo na sidhyati || 2 ||

“Compared with all other forms of discipline, Knowledge of the Self (bodha) is the one direct means for liberation. Just as cooking cannot be accomplished without fire, so Liberation cannot be attained without wisdom.”

In Verse 3, he further clarifies the inherent limitations of ritual action (karma) in destroying fundamental ignorance (avidyā):

Show Sanskrit

अविरोधितया कर्म नाविद्यां विनिवर्तयेत् । विद्याविद्यां निहन्त्य् एव तेजस्तिमिरसङ्घवत् ॥ ३ ॥

avirodhitayā karma nāvidyāṃ vinivartayet / vidyāvidyāṃ nihanty eva tejastimirasaṅghavat || 3 ||

“Action cannot destroy ignorance, for it is not in conflict with or opposed to ignorance. Knowledge (vidyā) alone destroys ignorance, just as light destroys deep darkness.”

Epistemological Framework of Nyāya: The Nyāya system defines valid knowledge through four foundational means of cognition (pramāṇas). As stated in Nyāya Sūtra 1.1.3:

2.5 Āgamas as valid testimony and the reliability of Śiva

Show Sanskrit

प्रत्यक्षानुमानोपमानशब्दाः प्रमाणानि ॥ १.१.३ ॥

pratyakṣānumānopamānaśabdāḥ pramāṇāni || 1.1.3 ||

“Perception (pratyakṣa), inference (anumāna), comparison (upamāna), and verbal testimony (śabda) are the valid means of knowledge.”

Nyāya Sūtra 1.1.7 specifically defines the nature of śabdapramāṇa (which governs authoritative verbal testimony and revealed scriptures):

Show Sanskrit

आप्तोपदेशः शब्दः ॥ १.१.७ ॥

āptopadeśaḥ śabdaḥ || 1.1.7 ||

“The instruction of an āpta (a trustworthy/reliable authority) is śabda.”

Ācārya Vātsyāyana, in his foundational commentary (Bhāṣya) on this sūtra, explicitly defines the characteristics of an āpta:

Show Sanskrit

यः साक्षात्कृतधर्मा यथादृष्टस्यार्थस्य चिख्यापयिषया प्रयुक्त उपदेष्टा स आप्तः.

yaḥ sākṣātkṛtadharmā yathādṛṣṭasyārthasya cikhyāpayiṣayā prayukta upadeṣṭā sa āptaḥ.

“That person is called āpta (reliable) who possesses direct and immediate perception of reality (sākṣātkṛtadharmā), who is moved by the desire to impart the truth exactly as perceived, and who is fully capable of communicating it.”

Furthermore, according to Nyāya Sūtra 1.1.8 (dṛṣṭādṛṣṭārthatvāt), śabda-pramāṇa applies to both perceptible (dṛṣṭa) and imperceptible/transcendent (adṛṣṭa) entities. Because the Āgamas originate directly from the Supreme Being (Īśvara)—the ultimate, omniscient Āpta—their verbal instructions constitute an infallible Śabda-Pramāṇa capable of bestowing liberating knowledge.

The Āptatva of Lord Śiva: To establish the Āptatva (absolute reliability and trustworthiness) of Lord Śiva as the author of the Āgamas within the Nyāya framework, three criteria must be fulfilled: complete knowledge of reality (jñāna), freedom from error or deception, and the benevolent desire to communicate truth (icchā-śakti).

1. Jñāna: Does the Deity Know Reality As It Is? In the Kailāsa Saṃhitā of the Śiva Purāṇa (Chapter 9, Verse 21), it is established that Sadāśiva possesses direct, effortless, and omniscient perception of all existence:

Show Sanskrit

यद्यथावस्थितं वस्तु तत्तथैव सदाशिवः । अयत्नेनैव जानाति तस्मात् सर्वज्ञ उच्यते.

yadyathāvasthitaṃ vastu tattathaiva sadāśivaḥ / ayatnenaiva jānāti tasmāt sarvajña ucyate.

“Whatever object exists, exactly in whichever way it is situated, Sadāśiva knows it precisely in that very manner without any effort; therefore, He is called ’Omniscient’ (sarvajña).”

This passage directly aligns with Ācārya Vātsyāyana’s criterion of yathādṛṣṭasyārthasya (cognizing an object precisely as it exists in reality).

2. Freedom from Deception (Akhaṇḍa-Yathārthatva): Pūrva Pakṣa Objection: Omniscience alone is insufficient. Why assume Lord Śiva’s statements are free from error, bias, or intentional deception? To establish absolute truthfulness (akhaṇḍa-yathārthatva), the source must be completely free from avidyā (ignorance) and delusion. The Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad (4.18) defines Śiva as pure, untainted consciousness beyond all dualities and darkness:

Show Sanskrit

यदा तमस् तन् न दिवा न रात्रिर् न सन् न चासच् छिव एव केवलः । तद् अक्षरं तत् सवितुर् वरेण्यं प्रज्ञा च तस्मात् प्रसृता पुराणी ॥ १८ ॥

yadā tamas tan na divā na rātrir na san na cāsac chiva eva kevalaḥ / tad akṣaraṃ tat savitur vareṇyaṃ prajñā ca tasmāt prasṛtā purāṇī || 18 ||

“When there is no darkness [ignorance], there is neither day nor night, neither being (sat) nor non-being (asat), but Śiva alone, absolute and pure (kevalah.). That is the Imperishable (akṣaram), that is the adorable light of Savitr., and from That issued forth ancient Wisdom (prajñā).”

Because Lord Śiva is the pure, imperishable source of primal wisdom (prajñā) devoid of tamas (ignorance), his words are inherently free from error, delusion, or intent to deceive.

3. Icchā-Śakti: Śiva’s Desire to Reveal Truth: The desire to make known the truth as perceived (cikhyāpayiṣā) is demonstrated through Lord Śiva’s Icchā-Śakti (will/desire to illuminate). As revealed in the Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad (3.18):

Show Sanskrit

यो ब्रह्माणं विदधाति पूर्वं यो वै वेदांश् च प्रहिणोति तस्मै । तं ह देवम् आत्मबुद्धिप्रकाशं मुमुक्षुर् वै शरणम् अहं प्रपद्ये ॥ १८ ॥

yo brahmāṇaṃ vidadhāti pūrvaṃ yo vai vedāṃś ca prahiṇoti tasmai / taṃ ha devam ātmabuddhiprakāśaṃ mumukṣur vai śaraṇam ahaṃ prapadye || 18 ||

“To Him who at the beginning of creation brought forth Brahmā, and who verily delivered (revealed) the Vedas unto him, to that Lord, the illuminator of self-knowledge, I, desirous of liberation (mumukṣu), fly for refuge.”

The compound ātma-buddhi-prakāśam specifically characterizes Lord Śiva as the illuminator of Self-knowledge (ātmabuddhi) through revealed scriptures. If Lord Śiva is recognized as the ultimate revealer of ātma-buddhi who imparted the Vedas, there is no logical ground to deny that His secondary revelations—such as the Śaiva, Pāśupata, or Bhairava Āgamas—likewise convey ātma-buddhi. Given that illuminating spiritual knowledge is an essential attribute of Lord Śiva, and given that the Vedic corpus itself acknowledges the authority of Āgamic revelations, the Āgamas stand validated as self-authoritative (svataḥ-pramāṇa) through the divine Āptatva of Lord Śiva.

4. Upadeṣṭṛtva (Communication) & Application of Daṇḍa-pūpa Nyāya: Pūrva Pakṣa Objection: Even if Lord Śiva possesses complete knowledge (jñāna) and freedom from deception, He cannot be considered an Āpta for the Āgamas if He lacks the capacity to communicate (upadeṣṭṛtva) or transmit scripture effectively to human beings.

Refutation & Hermeneutical Resolution: This objection is directly refuted by the Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad (3.18), which explicitly affirms Lord Śiva’s active role in transmitting supreme revelation:

Show Sanskrit

यो वै वेदाश् च प्रहिणोति तस्मै.

yo vai vedāś ca prahiṇoti tasmai.

“Who indeed revealed and sent forth the Vedas unto Him (Brahmā).”

Because the Upaniṣad establishes Lord Śiva as the original revealer and communicator of the entire Vedic corpus, His capacity as an active upadeṣṭā (teacher/communicator) is indisputable. To establish the authority of the Āgamas from this premise, we apply the classical maxim of Daṇḍa-pūpa Nyāya (the Maxim of the Stick and the Cake): Daṇḍa-pūpa Nyāya dictates that if a stick and a cake are bound together, and a mouse is known to have eaten the hard stick, it is naturally and inevitably inferred that the softer cake tied to it has also been eaten. In this context, the vast, highly complex, and eternal Veda represents the daṇḍa (the greater/harder task), while the specialized Āgamas represent the pūpa (the secondary/easier task). Since it is authoritatively established that Lord Śiva revealed the monumental Vedic revelation—which is universally accepted as authoritative Śabda-Pramāṇa—it follows a

fortiori (with much less effort) that His secondary, specialized revelations, such as the Śaiva and Pāśupata Āgamas, are also authoritative. If the primary and greater work (Veda) is recognized as His infallible utterance, the secondary and auxiliary work (Āgama) originating from the exact same Āpta cannot be logically rejected.

5. Identity of the Revealers (Abheda) & Extension of Āptatva to Śākta and Vaiṣṇava Āgamas: The Āptatva of Devī and the authority of the Śākta Āgamas need not be established independently, as the Śiva Purāṇa (Vāyavīya Saṃhitā, Uttarabhāga, Chapter 4, Verse 9) explicitly defines the complete non-difference (*abheda*) between Śiva and Devī:

Show Sanskrit

यथा शिवस् तथा देवी यथा देवी तथा शिवः । नानयोर् अन्तरं विद्याच् चन्द्रचन्द्रिकयोर् इव ॥ ९ ॥

yathā śivas tathā devī yathā devī tathā śivaḥ / nānayor antaraṃ vidyāc candracandrikayor iva || 9 ||

“As is Śiva, so is the Goddess (Devī); as is the Goddess, so is Śiva. One should perceive no difference between them, just as there is no separation between the moon and the moonlight.”

Because there is absolute identity between Śiva and Devī, the Āptatva established for Lord Śiva applies inherently and identically to Devī, thereby validating the Śākta Āgamas. Furthermore, the Rudra-hṛdaya Upaniṣad (Verse 11) declares:

Show Sanskrit

उमाशङ्करयोगो यः स योगो विष्णुर् उच्यते । यस्तु तस्मै नमस्कारं कुर्याद् भक्तिसमन्वितः ॥ ११ ॥

umāśaṅkarayogo yaḥ sa yogo viṣṇur ucyate / yastu tasmai namaskāraṃ kuryād bhaktisamanvitaḥ || 11 ||

“The union of Umā and Śaṅkara is that very union called Viṣṇu. One should offer bowful respects to Him with devotion.”

To establish the Āptatva of Lord Viṣṇu from this scriptural premise, we apply the classical maxim of Yathā-Kāraṇa- Guṇa-Tathā-Kārya-Guṇa Nyāya (The Maxim that the Qualities of the Cause Manifest in the Effect): This Nyāya dictates that the qualities (guṇas) inherent in the constituent causes must necessarily manifest in the resulting union or effect. Since the constituent principles of this divine union—Śiva and Umā—are established Āptas characterized by absolute omniscience (sarvajñatva) and freedom from error, the non-different union itself (Lord Viṣṇu) inherently possesses the complete quality of Āptatva. Consequently, the Vaiṣṇava Āgamas (Pañcarātra) revealed by Lord Viṣṇu possess the exact same self-evident authority (ātma-prāmāṇya) as the Vedas and the Pāśupata Āgamas. This non-difference among manifestations extends equally to the fierce aspect of Bhairava. As stated in the Padma Purāṇa (Uttara-khaṇḍa, Chapter 18, Verse 128):

Show Sanskrit

स्वयं च भैरवरूपं कृत्वा तन्मध्यगः पपौ । तीक्ष्णदंष्ट्रा महाकायास् तदासन् योगिनीगणाः ॥ १२८ ॥

svayaṃ ca bhairavarūpaṃ kṛtvā tanmadhyagaḥ papau / tīkṣṇadaṃṣṭrā mahākāyās tadāsan yoginīgaṇāḥ || 128 ||

“He Himself assumed the form of Bhairava, stood among them, and drank. The groups of Yoginīs then became large-bodied with sharp fangs.”

Therefore, in all matters concerning Āptatva and scriptural revelation, complete abheda (non-difference) must be recognized among the divine forms (Śiva, Devī, Viṣṇu, Bhairava) who revealed the Āgama-śāstras.

Maṅgalācaraṇa & Conclusion To conclude this defense of Sarva-Āgama-Prāmāṇya, we offer our humble obeisances unto the Supreme Divine Manifestations and the Sacred Revealed Texts through the following concluding Maṅgalācaraṇa:

Show Sanskrit

नमः शिवायाभयदाय नित्यं देव्यै च तस्यै जगदीश्वरायै । श्री-भैरवायात्म-विशुद्धि-दात्रे नमोऽस्तु सर्वागमपूजिताय ॥ १ ॥

namaḥ śivāyābhayadāya nityaṃ devyai ca tasyai jagadīśvarāyai / śrī-bhairavāyātma-viśuddhi-dātre namo’stu sarvāgamapūjitāya || 1 ||

“Salutations forever unto Śiva, the bestower of fearlessness; unto the Goddess (Devī), the Supreme Controller of the universe; unto Śrī Bhairava, the purifier of the Self; and salutations unto the holy Āgamas worshipped by all.”

Thus, the Āgamas stand united with the Vedas as eternal, infallible, and self-authoritative (svataḥ-pramāṇa) guideposts leading all seeking souls (mumukṣus) toward ultimate liberation (mokṣa).

Show Sanskrit

॥ इति श्री-सर्वागम-प्रामाण्य-वादः सम्पूर्णम् ॥

|| iti śrī-sarvāgama-prāmāṇya-vādaḥ sampūrṇam ||
