VP 1.1.1

जितं ते पुण्डरीकाक्ष नमस् ते विश्वभावन ।
नमस् तेऽस्तु हृषीकेश महापुरुष पूर्वज ॥ १ ॥

Victory be to thee, Puṇḍarīkākṣa; adoration be to thee, Viśvabhāvana; glory be to thee, Hṛṣīkeśa, Mahāpuruṣa, and Pūrvaja.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

jitam te — victory be to you; puṇḍarīkākṣa — O lotus-eyed one; namaḥ te — obeisance to you; viśvabhāvana — O creator and sustainer of the universe; namaḥ te astu — homage be to you; hṛṣīkeśa — O lord of the senses; mahāpuruṣa — O great spirit; pūrvaja — O one who existed before creation.

Śrīdhara

jitaṃ ta iti — on the words “Victory to you”; tatrāṃśe prathame adhyāye — in the first chapter of the first section; maitreyeṇa parāśare — by Maitreya to Parāśara; pravṛtty-arthaṃ purāṇasya — in order to set the Purāṇa in motion; praśnāḥ dvāviṃśatiḥ kṛtāḥ — twenty-two questions were made; puṇḍarīkākṣa iti — the word Puṇḍarīkākṣa; hṛdayākhyaṃ puṇḍarīkam aśnute vyāpnoti — he pervades the lotus called the heart; puṇḍarīke iva akṣiṇī yasya — whose eyes are like lotuses; atra śvetatvam avivakṣitam — whiteness is not intended here; lohitatvena eva nayana-utkarṣāt — because excellence of the eyes is through redness alone; śivārādhanārthaṃ puṇḍarīkīkṛtam akṣi — the eye made lotus-like for worship of Śiva; puṇḍarīkaṃ paraṃ dhāma akṣam avyayam — the lotus is the supreme abode and akṣa the imperishable; te iti sambandhe ṣaṣṭhī — te is a genitive in relation; tava jitaṃ jayo 'stu — may victory be yours; te tvayā jitaṃ sarvotkarṣeṇa sthitam — victory stands established through you; etena namaskāra ākṣiptaḥ — by this a salutation is implied; viśva-bhāvana viśva-utpādaka — Viśvabhāvana means creator of the universe; hṛṣīkāṇām indriyāṇām īśa — lord of the senses; tat-pravṛtti-hetutvāt — because he causes their activity; prāṇasya prāṇam — the life of life; cakṣuṣaś cakṣuḥ — the eye of the eye; śrotrasya śrotram — the ear of the ear; manaso manaḥ — the mind of the mind; mahāpuruṣa iti jīva-vyavacchedāya — Mahāpuruṣa distinguishes him from the individual soul; nitya-mukta-svabhāvatvāt — because his nature is eternally liberated; puri śarīre śayanāt puruṣatvam — Puruṣa because he lies in the body; pūrva-ja iti viśva-utpādakatve hetuḥ — Pūrvaja is the reason for his being creator; sṛṣṭeḥ pūrvam svataḥ prādurbhūtatvāt — because he appeared independently before creation; anyeṣāṃ tad-adhīnatvāt — because all others depend on him; bahu-vighna-sambhavāt — because many obstacles are possible; maṅgala-bāhulyam āvaśyakam — abundance of auspiciousness is necessary; bhūtātmā — the elemental self; pradhānātmā — the self identified with primordial nature; indriyātmā — the sensory self; paramātmā — the Supreme Self; ātmābhihitaḥ — called the individual self.

Viṣṇu Purāṇa

jitaṃ te puṇḍarīkākṣa namas te viśvabhāvana | namas te’stu hṛṣīkeśa mahāpuruṣa pūrvaja || 1 ||

Śrīdhara

jitaṃ ta iti | tatrāṃśe prathame’dhyāye maitreyeṇa parāśare | pravṛttyarthaṃ purāṇasya praśnā dvāviṃśatiḥ kṛtāḥ || puṇḍarīkākṣeti | hṛdayākhyaṃ puṇḍarīkam aśnute vyāpnotīti tathā | yad vā, puṇḍarīke iva akṣiṇī yasyeti | atra śvetatvam avivakṣitaṃ, lohitatvenaiva nayanotkarṣāt | śivārādhanārthaṃ puṇḍarīkīkṛtam akṣi yeneti vā | puṇḍarīkam paraṃ dhāma akṣam avyayam ucyate ityādi ślokoktavyutpattyā puṇḍarīkīkṣeti sambodhanam iti vā | te iti sambandhe ṣaṣṭhī | tava jitaṃ jayo’stv ityarthaḥ | yad vā, te tvayā jitaṃ sarvotkarṣeṇa sthitam iti | etena namaskāra ākṣiptaḥ | viśvabhāvana viśvotpādaka | hṛṣīkāṇām indriyāṇām īśa tat pravṛttihetutvāt | prāṇasya prāṇam utu cakṣuṣaś cakṣuḥ śrotrasya śrotraṃ manaso ye mano viduḥ iti śruteḥ | mahāpuruṣeti jīvavyavacchedāya mahattvaṃ ca, dvā suparṇā sayujā sakhāyā ityādi śruteḥ | nityamuktasvabhāvatvāc ca | puri śarīre śayanāt tu puruṣatvam | viśvotpādakatve hetuḥ pūrvajeti | sṛṣṭeḥ pūrvam eva svataḥ prādurbhūtatvenānanyathāsiddhatvena vānyeṣāṃ tadadhīnatvāt | nanv ekasmād eva namaskārān maṅgalasiddhau kṛtaṃ bahubhir namaskārair iti cet, na, asya śāstrasyātiśreyaḥsādhanatvena bahuvighnasambhāvanayā maṅgalabāhulasya āvaśyakatvāt | pañcātmā harir īrita iti | tatra puṇḍarīkākṣeti bhūtātmā, viśvabhāvaneti pradhānātmā | hṛṣīkeśeti indriyātmā, mahāpuruṣeti paramātmā | pūrvajety ātmābhihitaḥ ||1 ||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

जितं त इति ।

तत्रांशे प्रथमेऽध्याये मैत्रेयेण पराशरे ।
प्रवृत्त्यर्थं पुराणस्य प्रश्ना द्वाविंशतिः कृताः ॥

पुण्डरीकाक्षेति । हृदयाख्यं पुण्डरीकम् अश्नुते व्याप्नोतीति तथा । यद् वा, पुण्डरीके इव अक्षिणी यस्येति । अत्र श्वेतत्वम् अविवक्षितं, लोहितत्वेनैव नयनोत्कर्षात् । शिवाराधनार्थं पुण्डरीकीकृतम् अक्षि येनेति वा । पुण्डरीकम् परं धाम अक्षम् अव्ययम् उच्यते इत्यादि श्लोकोक्तव्युत्पत्त्या पुण्डरीकीक्षेति सम्बोधनम् इति वा । ते इति सम्बन्धे षष्ठी । तव जितं जयोऽस्त्व् इत्यर्थः । यद् वा, ते त्वया जितं सर्वोत्कर्षेण स्थितम् इति । एतेन नमस्कार आक्षिप्तः ।

विश्वभावन विश्वोत्पादक । हृषीकाणाम् इन्द्रियाणाम् ईश तत् प्रवृत्तिहेतुत्वात् । प्राणस्य प्राणम् उतु चक्षुषश् चक्षुः श्रोत्रस्य श्रोत्रं मनसो ये मनो विदुः इति श्रुतेः ।

महापुरुषेति जीवव्यवच्छेदाय महत्त्वं च, द्वा सुपर्णा सयुजा सखाया इत्यादि श्रुतेः । नित्यमुक्तस्वभावत्वाच् च । पुरि शरीरे शयनात् तु पुरुषत्वम् । विश्वोत्पादकत्वे हेतुः पूर्वजेति । सृष्टेः पूर्वम् एव स्वतः प्रादुर्भूतत्वेनानन्यथासिद्धत्वेन वान्येषां तदधीनत्वात् ।

नन्व् एकस्माद् एव नमस्कारान् मङ्गलसिद्धौ कृतं बहुभिर् नमस्कारैर् इति चेत्, न, अस्य शास्त्रस्यातिश्रेयःसाधनत्वेन बहुविघ्नसम्भावनया मङ्गलबाहुलस्य आवश्यकत्वात् ।

पञ्चात्मा हरिर् ईरित इति । तत्र पुण्डरीकाक्षेति भूतात्मा, विश्वभावनेति प्रधानात्मा । हृषीकेशेति इन्द्रियात्मा, महापुरुषेति परमात्मा । पूर्वजेत्य् आत्माभिहितः ॥१॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

On the words “Victory to you.” In the first chapter of the first section, Maitreya puts twenty-two questions to Parāśara in order to set the Purāṇa in motion.

“Puṇḍarīkākṣa” means “he who pervades the lotus called the heart,” or “he whose eyes are like lotuses.” The whiteness of the lotus is not intended here, since the excellence of the eyes lies in their redness. It may also mean “he whose eye was made lotus-like for the worship of Śiva,” or be an address formed from the traditional derivation in which the lotus is the supreme abode and akṣa is the imperishable. “Te” is a genitive in a relation: “may victory be yours”; alternatively, “victory stands established through you.” Thus a salutation is implied.

“Viśvabhāvana” means the creator of the universe. “Hṛṣīkeśa” means lord of the senses, because he causes them to function, as the śruti says: “the life of life, the eye of the eye, the ear of the ear, and the mind of the mind.” “Mahāpuruṣa” marks him off from the individual soul, in keeping with the teaching of the two associated birds, and because his nature is eternally liberated. He is called puruṣa because he lies in the body. “Pūrvaja” explains his role as creator: he appeared before creation, independently, while all others depend upon him.

If one salutation would establish auspiciousness, why are many offered? Because this scripture is a supreme means to blessedness and many obstacles may arise; an abundance of auspiciousness is therefore necessary. Alternatively, the five epithets express the fivefold Hari: “Puṇḍarīkākṣa” is the elemental self, “Viśvabhāvana” the self identified with primordial nature, “Hṛṣīkeśa” the sensory self, “Mahāpuruṣa” the Supreme Self, and “Pūrvaja” the individual self.

VP 1.1.2

सदक्षरं ब्रह्म य ईश्वरं पुमान्
गुणोर्मिसृष्टिस्थितिकालसंलयः ।
प्रधानसौभाग्यादिजगत्परम्पर्सूः
स नोऽस्तु विष्णुर् मतिभूतिमुक्तिदः ॥ २ ॥

May that Viṣṇu, who is the existent, imperishable Brahman, who is Īśvara and spirit; who with the waves of the three qualities is the cause of creation, preservation, and destruction; who is the parent of nature, intellect, and the other ingredients of the universe; be to us the bestower of understanding, wealth, and final emancipation.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

sat-akṣaram brahma — existent, imperishable Brahman; yaḥ īśvaram pumān — who is Īśvara and spirit; guṇormi — the waves of the qualities; sṛṣṭi-sthiti-kāla-saṃlayaḥ — creation, preservation, time, and dissolution; pradhāna-saubhāgyādi — nature, intellect, and the rest; jagat-paramparā-sūḥ — source of the succession of the universe; saḥ naḥ astu — may he be for us; mati-bhūti-mukti-daḥ — giver of understanding, prosperity, and liberation.

Śrīdhara

śrotṛ-pravṛtty-aṅgaṃ sambandhaṃ prayojanaṃ ca — the means of the listener’s engagement, relation, and purpose; pratipādayitum āha — to explain he says; sad-akṣaram iti — the words existent and imperishable; atiprasiddho viṣṇuḥ — the supremely renowned Viṣṇu; vyāpana-śīlaḥ — one whose nature is pervasion; deśa-kāla-svarūpato 'vaccheda-abhāvāt — because he is not delimited by place, time, or nature; viśateḥ — from the root viś, to enter; tat sṛṣṭvā tat evānuprāviśat — after creating that, he entered into it; matī-bhūti-mukti-daḥ — giver of understanding, prosperity, and liberation; tattva-jñāna-udrekeṇa — through the rise of knowledge of reality; adhikāri-bhedāt — according to distinctions among aspirants; uttamāṃ buddhiṃ aiśvaryaṃ muktiṃ ca dadāti — he gives excellent intellect, sovereignty, and liberation; brahma iti pūrṇam — Brahman means fullness; sat sarva-anusyūtam — existent, woven through all; akṣaram vikāraṃ nirākaroti — imperishable denies modification; puruṣaḥ kūṭasthaḥ — the Person is changeless; īśvaraḥ kartum akartum anyathā kartuṃ samarthaḥ — the Lord is able to create, not create, or create otherwise; guṇāḥ sattva-rajas-tamāṃsi — the qualities are sattva, rajas, and tamas; teṣām ūrmiḥ kṣobha-janitā — their waves are produced by disturbance; sṛṣṭi-sthiti-kāla-saṃhārāḥ — creation, preservation, time, and dissolution; sarva-adhiṣṭhānaḥ — the support of all; pradhāna-buddhi-ādīnāṃ jagat-prapañcānāṃ sūḥ — source of primordial nature, intellect, and the world; pralaye śakty-arpana-dvārā — through the giving of power at dissolution; vedāntināṃ sad-akṣaraṃ brahma — for Vedāntins, the existent imperishable Brahman; pātañjalānām īśvaraḥ — for the Pātañjalas, the Lord; sāṅkhyānāṃ pumān puruṣaḥ — for the Sāṅkhyas, the Person.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

sadakṣaraṃ brahma ya īśvaraṃ pumān guṇormi-sṛṣṭi-sthiti-kāla-saṃlayaḥ | pradhāna-saubhāgyādi-jagat-paramparāsūḥ sa no’stu viṣṇur matibhūti-muktidaḥ || 2 ||

Śrīdhara

śrotṛpravṛttyaṅgaṃ sambandhaṃ prayojanañca pratipādayitum āha—sadakṣaram iti | so’tiprasiddho viṣṇurvyāpinaśīlo deśakālasvarūpato vyavacchedābhāvāt | viśatervā viṣṇuḥ praveśanaśīlaḥ, tat sṛṣṭvā tadevānuprāviśad iti śruteḥ | matibhūtimuktido’stu—matibhūtyā tattvajñānodrekeṇa muktidaḥ | yadvā adhikāribhedāt matim uttamāṃ buddhiṃ bhūtim aiśvaryaṃ muktiñca dadātīti tathā | viṣṇupadasya praveśanaśīlārthatve mūrttatvaṃ prāptaṃ nirākaroti brahmeti pūrṇam ity arthaḥ | tadapi kuta ityata āha—sat, sarvānusyūtam | idaṃ sadidaṃ saditi sarvatra pratīyamānatvād anaṣṭam iti yāvat | akṣaram iti vikāraṃ nirākaroti | pumān, kūṭasthaḥ kutastarhi martyādipradattamata āha īśvaraḥ kartumakartumanyathā kartuṃ samarthaḥ | guṇāḥ sattvarajastamāṃsi teṣāmūrmayaḥ kṣobhajanitāḥ sṛṣṭisthitikālāḥ, kālaḥ, saṃhāraḥ, teṣāṃ saṃlayaḥ saṃśleṣo’dhyāso yasmin sa tathā, sarvādhiṣṭhānatveneśvaratvamavyāhatam ity arthaḥ | nanu pradhānādeva sṛṣṭyādi iti cedata āha—pradhānabuddhyādīnāṃ jagatprapañcānāṃ sūḥ savitā | pradhānasyānāditayā kathaṃ tajjanakatvam iti cenna, pralaye tasyākiñcitkaratvena śaktyarpaṇadvārā jananopacārāt | yadvā pradhānadvārā buddhyādisūrity arthaḥ | yadvā yo viṣṇurvedāntināṃ sadakṣaraṃ brahma pātañjalānāṃ kleśakarmavipākāśayairaparāmṛṣṭa īśvaraḥ | sāṅkhyānāṃ pumān puruṣaḥ | taduktaṃ kāpilaiḥ na prakṛtirna vikṛtiḥ puruṣa iti | sa matibhūtimuktido’stvity anvayaḥ | śeṣaṃ samānam ||2 ||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

श्रोतृप्रवृत्त्यङ्गं सम्बन्धं प्रयोजनञ्च प्रतिपादयितुम् आह—सदक्षरम् इति । सोऽतिप्रसिद्धो विष्णुर्व्यापिनशीलो देशकालस्वरूपतो व्यवच्छेदाभावात् ।

विशतेर्वा विष्णुः प्रवेशनशीलः, तत् सृष्ट्वा तदेवानुप्राविशद् इति श्रुतेः । मतिभूतिमुक्तिदोऽस्तु—मतिभूत्या तत्त्वज्ञानोद्रेकेण मुक्तिदः । यद्वा अधिकारिभेदात् मतिम् उत्तमां बुद्धिं भूतिम् ऐश्वर्यं मुक्तिञ्च ददातीति तथा । विष्णुपदस्य प्रवेशनशीलार्थत्वे मूर्त्तत्वं प्राप्तं निराकरोति ब्रह्मेति पूर्णम् इत्य् अर्थः । तदपि कुत इत्यत आह—सत्, सर्वानुस्यूतम् । इदं सदिदं सदिति सर्वत्र प्रतीयमानत्वाद् अनष्टम् इति यावत् । अक्षरम् इति विकारं निराकरोति । पुमान्, कूटस्थः कुतस्तर्हि मर्त्यादिप्रदत्तमत आह ईश्वरः कर्तुमकर्तुमन्यथा कर्तुं समर्थः ।

गुणाः सत्त्वरजस्तमांसि तेषामूर्मयः क्षोभजनिताः सृष्टिस्थितिकालाः, कालः, संहारः, तेषां संलयः संश्लेषोऽध्यासो यस्मिन् स तथा, सर्वाधिष्ठानत्वेनेश्वरत्वमव्याहतम् इत्य् अर्थः । ननु प्रधानादेव सृष्ट्यादि इति चेदत आह—प्रधानबुद्ध्यादीनां जगत्प्रपञ्चानां सूः सविता । प्रधानस्यानादितया कथं तज्जनकत्वम् इति चेन्न, प्रलये तस्याकिञ्चित्करत्वेन शक्त्यर्पणद्वारा जननोपचारात् । यद्वा प्रधानद्वारा बुद्ध्यादिसूरित्य् अर्थः । यद्वा यो विष्णुर्वेदान्तिनां सदक्षरं ब्रह्म पातञ्जलानां क्लेशकर्मविपाकाशयैरपरामृष्ट ईश्वरः । साङ्ख्यानां पुमान् पुरुषः । तदुक्तं कापिलैः न प्रकृतिर्न विकृतिः पुरुष इति । स मतिभूतिमुक्तिदोऽस्त्वित्य् अन्वयः । शेषं समानम् ॥२॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

To explain the listener’s engagement, the relation of the work to its subject, and its purpose, he says “the existent, imperishable Brahman.” Viṣṇu is supremely renowned and is one who pervades, since he is not delimited by place, time, or his own nature.

The name Viṣṇu may also derive from viś, “to enter,” for the śruti says that after creating the world he entered it. “May he grant understanding, prosperity, and liberation”: by understanding he grants liberation through the rise of knowledge of reality; or, according to the qualification of the aspirant, he gives excellent intellect, sovereignty, and liberation. If “Viṣṇu” in the sense of “one who enters” seems to imply a formed body, the word “Brahman” removes that implication and declares his fullness. “Sat” means that he is present throughout all things and therefore does not perish; “akṣara” denies modification; “Pumān” denotes the changeless one; and “Īśvara” means one able to create, not create, or create otherwise.

The qualities are sattva, rajas, and tamas. Their waves, produced by disturbance, are creation, preservation, time, and dissolution; he is that in whom their conjunction or superimposition occurs. As the support of all, his lordship is unobstructed. If creation is attributed to primordial nature alone, the verse says that he is the source of primordial nature, intellect, and the rest of the world. Although primordial nature is beginningless, during dissolution it is powerless; the giving of power by Viṣṇu is therefore figuratively called its generation. Alternatively, he produces intellect and the rest through primordial nature. For Vedāntins he is the existent imperishable Brahman; for the Pātañjalas, the Lord untouched by affliction, action, result, and latent impression; and for the Sāṅkhyas, the Person. The remaining construction is the same: may that Viṣṇu grant understanding, prosperity, and liberation.

VP 1.1.31

प्रवृत्ते च निवृत्ते च कर्मण्यस्तमला मतिः ।
मत्प्रसादादसन्दिग्धा तव वत्स भविष्यति ॥ ३१ ॥

Whether engaged in religious rites or abstaining from them, your understanding, through my favour, shall be perfect and free from doubt.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

pravṛtte ca nivṛtte ca karmaṇi — in active and renounced works; astamalā matiḥ — an unclouded understanding; mat-prasādāt — through my favour; asandigdhā — free from doubt; tava vatsa bhaviṣyati — shall be yours, child.

Śrīdhara

pravṛtte ca nivṛtte ca karmaṇi — in active and renounced action; astamalā ajñāna-viparyaya-śūnyā matiḥ — the mind free from ignorance and error; te ca karmaṇī smṛti-prasiddhe — those two actions are established in Smṛti; iha vā amutra kāmyaṃ pravṛttam abhidhīyate — desired action here or in the next world is called active; vairāgya-jñāna-pūrvaṃ tu nivṛttam upadiśyate — action preceded by dispassion and knowledge is taught as renounced.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

pravṛtte ca nivṛtte ca karmaṇy astamalā matiḥ | mat-prasādād asandigdhā tava vatsa bhaviṣyati || 31 ||

Śrīdhara

pravṛtte ca nivṛtte ca karmaṇi astamalā ajñānaviparyayaśūnyā matirbhaviṣyati | te ca karmaṇī smṛtiprasiddhe | iha vāmutra kāmyaś ca pravṛttamabhidhīyate | vairāgyajñāna pūrvantu nivṛttamupadiśyate iti ||31 ||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

प्रवृत्ते च निवृत्ते च कर्मणि अस्तमला अज्ञानविपर्ययशून्या मतिर्भविष्यति । ते च कर्मणी स्मृतिप्रसिद्धे । इह वामुत्र काम्यश् च प्रवृत्तमभिधीयते । वैराग्यज्ञान पूर्वन्तु निवृत्तमुपदिश्यते इति ॥३१॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

In active and renounced action, the mind will be free from ignorance and error. These two kinds of action are established in the teaching of the Smṛti. Actions undertaken here or in the next world for the sake of desired results are called “active”; actions preceded by dispassion and knowledge are taught as “renounced.”
