VP 2.14.1

निशम्य तस्येति वचः परमार्थ-समन्वितम् ।
प्रश्रयावनतो भूत्वा तम् आह नृपतिर् द्विजम् ॥१॥

Having heard these remarks, full of profound truth, the king was highly pleased with the Brahman, and respectfully thus addressed him:

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

niśamya — having heard; tat — that; vacaḥ — speech; paramārtha-samanvitam — endowed with highest truth; praśraya-avanataḥ — bowed respectfully; nṛpatiḥ — the king; dvijam — to the Brahman

Śrīdhara

nāham-iti-vākya — the statement beginning “I do not”; pramāṇa-asambhava — the means of knowledge being impossible; prameya-asambhāvanā — doubt about the object of knowledge; bhūyaḥ bhūyaḥ pṛcchati — asks again and again

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

niśamya tasyeti vacaḥ paramārtha-samanvitam | praśrayāvanato bhūtvā tam āha nṛpatir dvijam ||1||

Śrīdhara

nāham ity-ādi vākyena pramāṇāsambhave gate | prameyāsambhavāśaṅkī bhūyo bhūyo'tha pṛcchati ||1||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

नाहम् इत्य्-आदि वाक्येन प्रमाणासम्भवे गते । प्रमेयासम्भवाशङ्की भूयो भूयोऽथ पृच्छति ॥१॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Having heard the statements beginning with “I do not carry [the palanquin],” and finding no difficulty in the means of knowledge, the king—still fearing that the object might be impossible to establish—asks again and again.

VP 2.14.2

भगवन् यत् त्वया प्रोक्तं परमार्थ-मयं वचः ।
श्रुते तस्मिन् भ्रमन्तीव मनसो मम वृत्तयः ॥२॥

What you have said is no doubt the truth; but in listening to it my mind is much disturbed.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

bhagavan — O venerable one; yat — what; tvayā proktam — spoken by you; paramārtha-mayam — full of highest truth; śrute tasmin — when that was heard; manasaḥ vṛttayaḥ — movements of my mind; bhramanti iva — wander as if

Śrīdhara

nāham-ādi-vākya-śravaṇa — hearing the statement beginning “I do not”; pramāṇa-asambhāvanā — doubt about the means of knowledge; śreyasām paramārthatva — the ultimate status of the various goods; ātmā eva paramārthaḥ — the Self alone is the Supreme Truth; pṛcchati — he asks

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

bhagavan yat tvayā proktaṃ paramārtha-mayaṃ vacaḥ | śrute tasmin bhramantīva manaso mama vṛttayaḥ ||2||

Śrīdhara

nāhaṃ pīvetyādivākyaśravaṇataḥ pramāṇāsambhāvanāyāṃ nivṛttāyāmapi nānādarśanaprasiddhaśreyasāṃ paramārthatve sati ātmaiva paramārtha ityasya prameyasya asambhāvanayā pṛcchati—bhagavann iti daśabhiḥ ||2||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

नाहं पीवेत्यादिवाक्यश्रवणतः प्रमाणासम्भावनायां निवृत्तायामपि नानादर्शनप्रसिद्धश्रेयसां परमार्थत्वे सति आत्मैव परमार्थ इत्यस्य प्रमेयस्य असम्भावनया पृच्छति—भगवन्न् इति दशभिः ॥२॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Although hearing the statement beginning “I do not [carry it]” removes doubt about the means of knowledge, he asks because he cannot conceive how the highest good taught by various schools can be the Supreme Truth, with the Self alone as that truth; he begins with “Bhagavan.”

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

śāstrāntara-sammata śreyas — goods accepted by other scriptures; ātma-tattva — the truth of the Self; paramārtha — the Supreme Truth; bheda — distinction; vikalpa — alternative

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

bhūpa pṛcchasi kiṃ śreyaḥ paramārthaṃ nu pṛcchasi | śreyāṃsy aparamārthāni aśeṣāṇi ca bhūpate ||12||

Śrīdhara

tatra śāstrāntarasammatebhyaḥ śreyobhya ātmatattvameva paramārtha iti nirṇetuṃ śreyaḥ paramārthayorbhedaṃ vikalpapūrvakam āha—bhūpa iti | nu vikalpe śreyāṃsi paramārthabhūtāni na veti vikalpe, aparamārthāni iti pāṭhe na vidyate paramārthabhūto'rtho yeṣu tāni tathā ||12||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तत्र शास्त्रान्तरसम्मतेभ्यः श्रेयोभ्य आत्मतत्त्वमेव परमार्थ इति निर्णेतुं श्रेयः परमार्थयोर्भेदं विकल्पपूर्वकम् आह—भूप इति । नु विकल्पे श्रेयांसि परमार्थभूतानि न वेति विकल्पे, अपरमार्थानि इति पाठे न विद्यते परमार्थभूतोऽर्थो येषु तानि तथा ॥१२॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

To establish that the Self alone is the Supreme Truth among the goods accepted by other scriptures, he distinguishes śreyas from paramārtha, beginning with “O king.” Here “nu” expresses an alternative: are the desirable ends ultimate or not? In the reading aparāmarthāni, they are things in which no ultimate reality is found.

VP 2.14.13

देवताराधनं कृत्वा धन-सम्पदम् इच्छति ।

but there are many things which are considered best, as well as those which are the great ends (or truths) of life. To him who, by the worship of the gods, seeks for wealth, prosperity, children, or dominion, each of these is respectively best.

He now sets out seven goods, each progressively higher: for one who desires wealth, sons, or a kingdom, that desired object itself is the good.

VP 2.14.15

आत्मा ध्येयः सदा भूप योग-युक्तैस् तथा परम् ।
श्रेअय्स् तस्यैव संयोगः श्रेयो यः परमात्मनः ॥१५॥

Best is that which yields the best recompense, although it be not solicited. Self-contemplation, ever practised by devout ascetics, is to them the best. But best of all is the identification of soul with the supreme spirit.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Meditation on the Self is the good; the union of that self with the Supreme Self is the higher good.

VP 2.14.16

VP 2.14.17

धर्माय त्यज्यते किं नु परमार्थो धनं यदि ।

VP 2.14.19

एवं न परमार्थोऽस्ति जगत्य् अस्मिन् चराचरे ।
परमार्थो हि कार्याणि कारणानाम् अशेषतः ॥१९॥
