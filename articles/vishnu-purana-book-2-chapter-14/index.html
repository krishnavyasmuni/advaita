VP 2.14.1

निशम्य तस्येति वचः परमार्थ-समन्वितम्।
प्रश्रयावनतो भूत्वा तम् आह नृपतिर् द्विजम्॥१॥

Having heard these remarks, full of profound truth, the king was highly pleased with the Brahman, and respectfully thus addressed him:

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

नाहम् इत्य्-आदि वाक्येन प्रमाणासम्भवे गते । प्रमेयासम्भवाशङ्की भूयो भूयोऽथ पृच्छति ॥१॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Having heard the statements beginning with “I do not carry [the palanquin],” and finding no difficulty in the means of knowledge, the king—still fearing that the object might be impossible to establish—asks again and again.

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

नाहं पीवेत्यादिवाक्यश्रवणतः प्रमाणासम्भावनायां निवृत्तायामपि नानादर्शनप्रसिद्धश्रेयसां परमार्थत्वे सति आत्मैव परमार्थ इत्यस्य प्रमेयस्य असम्भावनया पृच्छति—भगवन्न् इति दशभिः ॥२॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Although hearing the statement beginning “I do not [carry it]” removes doubt about the means of knowledge, he asks because he cannot conceive how the highest good taught by various schools can be the Supreme Truth, with the Self alone as that truth; he begins with “Bhagavan.”

स च एक एव, न तु प्रतिदेहं भिन्नः । व्यापी साक्षितया सर्व-व्यापकः । समः एक-रूपः, न तु ज्ञानादि-परिणामैर् अनेक-रूपः । शुद्धः कर्तृत्वादि लुब्य [?] शून्यः । अतो जन्म-वृद्ध्य्-आदिभिर् देहादि-विकारैर् असंस्पृष्टः । अत एव सर्व-गतो, न तु देह-परिणामः । अतो मध्य-परिमाणत्वाभावाद् अव्ययः ॥२९॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

The self’s consciousness remains one in its own and others’ bodies; apparent difference is caused by limiting adjuncts. Those who take the adjuncts as the self’s intrinsic nature see falsely.

VP 2.14.32

वेणु-रन्ध्र-विभेदेन भेदः षड्जादि-सञ्ज्ञितः।

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

The flute analogy shows how one air appears as different notes when it passes through different holes. Likewise, differences in embodied experience do not alter the underlying nature of the self.
