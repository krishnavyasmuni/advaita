VP 2.12.1

रथस् त्रि-चक्रः सोमस्य कुन्दाभास् तस्य वाजिनः ।
वाम-दक्षिणतो युक्ता दश तेन चरत्य् असौ ॥१॥

The chariot of the moon has three wheels, and is drawn by ten horses, of the whiteness of jasmine, five on the right half of the yoke and five on the left.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

rathaḥ — chariot; tri-cakraḥ — three-wheeled; somasya — of the moon; kundābhāḥ — jasmine-white; vājinaḥ — horses; daśa — ten.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

rathas tri-cakraḥ somasya kundābhās tasya vājinaḥ | vāma-dakṣiṇato yuktā daśa tena caraty asau ||1 ||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

उक्ता चतुर्भिरध्यायैरष्टमासै रवेः स्थितिः । अथ सोमादिसंस्थोक्त्या तत्प्रपञ्चोपसंहृतिः कुन्दाभाः श्वेताः रविरथाद् विशेषम् आह—वामतो दक्षिणतश्च युक्तास्तस्य रथस्याश्वा दश इति । तेन रथेन ॥१॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Śrīdhara says that the chapter now turns from the sun to the moon: its white horses are yoked five on each side, and its chariot follows the same path and support as the sun’s.

VP 2.12.2

वीथ्याश्रयाणि ऋक्षाणि ध्रुवाधारेण वेगिना ।
ह्रास-वृद्धि-क्रमस् तस्य रश्मीनां सवितुर् यथा ॥२॥

It moves along the asterisms, divided into ranges, as before described; and, in like manner as the sun, is upheld by Dhruva, the cords that fasten it being tightened or relaxed in the same way as it proceeds on its course.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

vīthi-āśrayāṇi — resting in the paths; ṛkṣāṇi — asterisms; dhruva-ādhāreṇa — supported by Dhruva; hāsa-vṛddhi-kramaḥ — order of waning and waxing; raśmīnām — of rays.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

vīthyāśrayāṇi ṛkṣāṇi dhruvādhāreṇa veginā | hrāsa-vṛddhi-kramas tasya raśmīnāṃ savitur yathā ||2 ||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

नागवीथ्याद्याश्रयाण्यश्विन्यादीनि नक्षत्राणि प्रागुदित्याचरति । ह्रासवृद्धिक्रम इति । उदितो वर्द्धमानाभिरा मध्याह्नादित्यत्र सवितृरश्मीनां यथोक्तस्तथा तस्यापि ॥२॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

The moon travels through the asterisms of the lunar paths, with its waxing and waning following the pattern already explained for the sun.

VP 2.12.3

अर्कस्येव हि तस्याश्वाः सकृद् युक्ता वहन्ति ते ।
कल्पम् एकं मुनि-श्रेष्ठ वारि-गर्भ-समुद्भवाः ॥३॥

The horses of the moon, sprung from the bosom of the waters, drag the car for a whole Kalpa, as do the coursers of the sun.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

arkasya iva — like the sun’s; aśvāḥ — horses; sakṛt-yuktāḥ — yoked once; kalpam ekam — for one Kalpa; vāri-garbha-samudbhavāḥ — sprung from the waters.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

arkasyeva hi tasyāśvāḥ sakṛd yuktā vahanti te | kalpam ekaṃ muni-śreṣṭha vāri-garbha-samudbhavāḥ ||3 ||

VP 2.12.4

क्षीणं पीतं सुरैः सोमम् आप्याययति दीप्तिमान् ।
मैत्रेयैक-कलं सन्तं रश्मिनैकेन भास्करः ॥४॥

The radiant sun supplies the moon, when reduced by the draughts of the gods to a single Kalā, with a single ray;

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

kṣīṇam — diminished; pītam — drunk; somam — moon; suraiḥ — by gods; āpyāyayati — restores; eka-kalam — one digit; raśminā ekena — with one ray.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

kṣīṇaṃ pītaṃ suraiḥ somam āpyāyayati dīptimān | maitreyaika-kalaṃ santaṃ raśminaikena bhāskaraḥ ||4 ||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

क्षीणस्य सोमस्य पुनः पूर्णस्य सतो देवतादितर्पकत्वम् आह—क्षीणम् इति चतुर्भिः । पञ्चदश्यां कलायां दर्शे पितृभिः पीतायाः षोडस्याः कलायाः अवशिष्टत्वादेककलं सन्तम् इत्युक्तम् । एकेन रश्मिना सुषुम्णाख्येन ॥४॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

The sun restores the moon when the gods have reduced it to a single digit, supplying one ray called Suṣumṇā.

VP 2.12.5

क्रमेण येन पीतोऽसौ देवैस् तेन निशाकरम् ।
आप्याययत्य् अनुदिनं भास्करो वारि-तस्करः ॥५॥

and in the same proportion as the ruler of the night is exhausted by the celestials, it is replenished by the sun, the plunderer of the waters.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

krameṇa — in order; yena — by which; pītaḥ — drunk; tena — by that; niśākaram — moon; āpyāyayati — restores; anudinam — daily; bhāskaraḥ — sun.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

krameṇa yena pīto'sau devais tena niśākaram | āpyāyayaty anudinaṃ bhāskaro vāri-taskaraḥ ||5 ||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

येन क्रमेण प्रतिपत्प्रभृति देवैर्वह्न्यादिभिरसौ पीतस्तेनैव प्रतिदिनं तं निशाकरमाप्याययति । अलक्षितं वारि तस्करवत् हरतीति तथा वारितस्करः । एवं गृहीतेन वारिणा सोममापूरयतीत्यर्थः ॥५॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Whatever the gods consume from the moon day by day, the sun restores in the same order; the sun is called a thief because he takes up water unseen.

VP 2.12.6

सम्भृतं चार्ध-मासेन तत्-सोमस्थं सुधामृतम् ।
पिबन्ति देवा मैत्रेय सुधाहारा यतोऽमराः ॥६॥

For the gods, Maitreya, drink the nectar and ambrosia accumulated in the moon during half the month, and from this being their food they are immortal.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

sambhṛtam — collected; ardha-māsena — in half a month; sudhāmṛtam — nectar of ambrosia; pibanti — drink; devāḥ — gods; sudhāhārāḥ — nectar-eaters; amaraḥ — immortal.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

sambhṛtaṃ cārdha-māsena tat-somasthaṃ sudhāmṛtam | pibanti devā maitreya sudhāhārā yato'marāḥ ||6 ||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

सुधामृतं सुधारूपममृतं मृत्युभेषजम् ॥६॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

The ambrosia is nectar in the form of the life-giving medicine of immortality.

VP 2.12.7

त्रयस्त्रिंशत्-सहस्राणि त्रयस्त्रिंशच्-छतानि च ।
त्रयस्त्रिंशत् तथा देवाः पिबन्ति क्षणदाकरम् ॥७॥

Thirty-six thousand three hundred and thirty-three divinities drink the lunar ambrosia.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

trayastriṃśat-sahasrāṇi — thirty-three thousand; trayastriṃśat-śatāni — thirty-three hundreds; devāḥ — gods; pibanti — drink; kṣaṇadā-karam — maker of night.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

trayastriṃśat-sahasrāṇi trayastriṃśac-chatāni ca | trayastriṃśat tathā devāḥ pibanti kṣaṇadākaram ||7 ||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

क्षणदाकरं सोमम् ॥७॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

The phrase “night-maker” means the moon.

VP 2.12.8

कला-द्वयावशिष्टस् तु प्रविष्टः सूर्य-मण्डलम् ।
अमाख्य-रश्मौ वसति अमावास्या ततः स्मृता ॥८॥

When two digits remain, the moon enters the orbit of the sun, and abides in the ray called Amā; whence the period is termed Amāvāsyā.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

kalā-dvaya-avaśiṣṭaḥ — with two digits remaining; praviṣṭaḥ — entered; sūrya-maṇḍalam — solar orb; amā-rāśmi — Amā ray; amāvāsyā — new moon.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

kalā-dvayāvaśiṣṭas tu praviṣṭaḥ sūrya-maṇḍalam | amākhya-raśmau vasati amāvāsyā tataḥ smṛtā ||8 ||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

पितॄणां सोमपानप्रकारम् आह—कलाद्वय इति सार्धैः षड्भिः ॥८॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Śrīdhara explains the way the moon reaches the fathers: the remaining two digits are approached at the new moon.

VP 2.12.9

अप्सु तस्मिन्न् अहो-रात्रे पूर्वं विशति चन्द्रमाः ।
ततो वीरत्सु वसति प्रयात्य् अर्कं ततः क्रमात् ॥९॥

In that orbit the moon is immersed for a day and night in the water; thence it enters the branches and shoots of the trees; and thence goes to the sun.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

apsu — in waters; ahorātre — for day and night; candramāḥ — moon; vīrutsu — in plants; prayāti — goes; arkam — sun.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

apsu tasminn aho-rātre pūrvaṃ viśati candramāḥ | tato vīratsu vasati prayāty arkaṃ tataḥ kramāt ||9 ||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

सूर्यमण्डलप्रवेशक्रमम् आह—अप्स्व् इति ॥९॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

This verse explains the moon’s passage into the sun’s orb.

VP 2.12.10

छिनत्ति वीरुधो यस् तु वीरुत्-संस्थे निशाकरे ।
पत्रं वा पातयत्य् एकं ब्रह्म-हत्यां स विन्दति ॥१०॥

Consequently any one who cuts off a branch, or casts down a leaf, when the moon is in the trees (the day of its rising invisible), is guilty of Brahmanicide.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

chīnatti — cuts; vīrudhaḥ — plants; patram — leaf; brahma-hatyām — Brahmanicide; vindati — incurs.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

chinatti vīrudho yas tu vīrut-saṃsthe niśākare | patraṃ vā pātayaty ekaṃ brahma-hatyāṃ sa vindati ||10 ||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

प्रसङ्गाद् दर्शे वीरुधां छेदकर्तारं निन्दति—छिनत्ति इति ॥१०॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

The text condemns cutting vegetation on the new-moon day because the moon is then abiding in the plants.

VP 2.12.11

सोमं पञ्चदशे भागे किञ्चिच् छिष्टे कलात्मके ।
अपराह्णे पितृ-गणा जघन्यं पर्युपासते ॥११॥

When the remaining portion of the moon consists of but a fifteenth part, the progenitors approach it in the afternoon,

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

somam — moon; pañcadaśe bhāge — at the fifteenth part; kiñcit śiṣṭe — a little remaining; aparāhṇe — in afternoon; pitṛ-gaṇāḥ — fathers; upāsate — approach.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

somaṃ pañcadaśe bhāge kiñcic chiṣṭe kalātmake | aparāhṇe pitṛ-gaṇā jaghanyaṃ paryupāsate ||11 ||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तद् एवं दर्शाहोरात्रे अप्सु वीरुत्सु च प्रवेशादुपक्षीणप्राये कलात्मके पञ्चदशे भागे किञ्चिच्छिष्टे सति तं जघन्यं पश्चादवशिष्टं सोममपराह्ने पितृगणाः पर्युपासते पानार्थं परितः सेवन्ते ॥११॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

After the moon has entered water and plants and is nearly exhausted, the fathers approach the small remnant in the afternoon to drink it.

VP 2.12.12

पिबन्ति द्वि-कलाकारं शिष्टा तस्य कला तु या ।
सुधामृत-मयी पुण्या ताम् इन्दोः पितरो मुने ॥१२॥

and drink the last portion, that sacred Kalā which is composed of ambrosia, and contained in the two digits of the form of the moon.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

dvi-kala-ākāram — two-digit form; śiṣṭā kalā — remaining digit; sudhāmṛtamayī — made of nectar; puṇyā — sacred; pitarāḥ pibanti — fathers drink.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

pibanti dvi-kalākāraṃ śiṣṭā tasya kalā tu yā | sudhāmṛta-mayī puṇyā tām indoḥ pitaro mune ||12 ||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

ततश्च तस्येनोर्द्विकलाकारः शिष्टद्विकलो य आकारस्तत्र शिष्टा पञ्चदशी या कला तां पितरः पिबन्ति, न तु षोडशीम् । द्विकलं सोमम् इति पाठे द्विकलं पर्युपास्य तस्य शिष्टामेककलां पिबन्तीत्यर्थः । द्विलवम् इति पाठे लवः सूक्ष्मः कलावयवः, द्विलवपरिमितं कालं तां शिष्टां कलां पिबन्तीत्यर्थः ॥१२॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

The fathers drink the remaining fifteenth digit, not the sixteenth; alternate readings explain the measure as either two digits or two very small units of time.

VP 2.12.13

निःसृतं तद् अमावास्यां गभस्तिभ्यः सुधामृतम् ।
मासं तृप्तिम् अव्याप्याग्र्यां पितरः सन्ति निर्वृताः ।
सौम्या बर्हिषदश् चैव अग्निष्व् आत्ताश् च ते त्रिधा ॥१३॥

Having drunk the nectar effused by the lunar rays on the day of conjunction, the progenitors are satisfied, and remain tranquil for the ensuing month. These progenitors (or Pitris) are of three classes, termed Saumyas, Varhishadas, and Agnishvāttas.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

nissṛtam — issued; amāvāsyām — at new moon; sudhāmṛtam — nectar; pitaraḥ — fathers; saumyāḥ, barhiṣadaḥ, agniṣvāttāḥ — three classes.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

niḥsṛtaṃ tad amāvāsyāṃ gabhastibhyaḥ sudhāmṛtam | māsaṃ tṛptim avyāpyāgryāṃ pitaraḥ santi nirvṛtāḥ | saumyā barhiṣadaś caiva agniṣv āttāś ca te tridhā ||13 ||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

अमावस्याम् अमावास्यायां तत् सोमस्य गभस्तिभ्यो निःसृतं सुधामृतं पीत्वेति शेषः ॥१३॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

The nectar issuing from the moon’s rays at new moon is understood; having drunk it, the fathers remain satisfied for a month. They are threefold: Saumyas, Barhiṣadas, and Agniṣvāttas.

VP 2.12.14

एवं देवान् सिते पक्षे कृष्ण-पक्षे तथा पितॄन् ।
वीरुधश् चामृत-मयैः शीतैर् अप्य् अरमाणुभिः ॥१४॥

In this manner the moon, with its cooling rays, nourishes the gods in the light fortnight, the Pitris in the dark fortnight, and vegetables with the cool nectary aqueous atoms it sheds upon them;

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

sitapakṣe — in bright fortnight; kṛṣṇa-pakṣe — in dark fortnight; pitṝn — fathers; śītaiḥ — cool; amṛta-mayaiḥ — nectar-like; paramāṇubhiḥ — particles.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

evaṃ devān site pakṣe kṛṣṇa-pakṣe tathā pitṝn | vīrudhaś cāmṛta-mayaiḥ śītair apy aramāṇubhiḥ ||14 ||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तद् एवममृतद्वारा सोमाद् देवादीनां तर्पणम् उक्त्वा तन्निष्पादितान्नादिनापि तेषां तर्पणम् आह—एवम् इति । सिते पक्षे देवानाप्याययति देवानां शुक्लपक्षे यजनात् स्नानात् तस्मादापूर्यमाणपक्षे यजन्ते इति श्रुतेः । कृष्णपक्षे पितॄन् अपरपक्षे पितृणाम् इति श्रुतेः ॥ अपपरमाणुभिरपां सूक्ष्मैरंशैः ॥१४॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

The moon nourishes gods in the bright fortnight and fathers in the dark fortnight through worship, and nourishes plants with the cool subtle particles of water.

VP 2.12.15

वीरुधौषधि-निष्पत्त्या मनुष्य-पशु-कीटकान् ।
आप्याययति शीतांशुः प्राकाश्याह्लादनेन तु ॥१५॥

and through their development it sustains men, animals, and insects; at the same time gratifying them by its radiance.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

vīrudh-oṣadhi-niṣpatti — growth of plants and herbs; manuṣya-paśu-kīṭakān — men, animals, insects; āpyāyayati — nourishes; śītāṃśuḥ — cool-rayed moon.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

vīrudhauṣadhi-niṣpattyā manuṣya-paśu-kīṭakān | āpyāyayati śītāṃśuḥ prākāśyāhlādanena tu ||15 ||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

sad-bhāva evaṃ bhavato mayokto jñānaṃ yathā satyam asatyam anyat | etat tu yat saṃvyavahāra-bhūtaṃ tatrāpi coktaṃ bhuvanāśritaṃ te ||45 ||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

उपसंहरति सद्भाव इति । सद्भावः परमार्थः । एषो भवत इति सुलोपाभावस्त्वार्षः । ननु चान्यस्य सर्वस्यासत्यत्वे किमर्थमेतद्भवनाद्याश्रितं बहु वर्णितं तत्राह—एतत्तु यज्ज्ञानव्यतिरिक्तं तत् सर्वं संव्यवहारभूतं सम्यक् परमार्थोपयोगिनि व्यवहारे भूतं स्थितं, नतु सतः सत्यं तत्रापि च विशेषतस्तुभ्यं यत् भुवनाश्रितमुक्तम् ॥४५॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

“Being” means ultimate reality. The worldly account was nevertheless given because all that is other than knowledge remains useful within ordinary conduct, especially for the listener.

VP 2.12.46

यज्ञः पशुर् वह्निर् अशेष-ऋत्विक् सोमः सुराः स्वर्ग-मयश् च कामः ।
इत्य्-आदि-कर्माश्रित-मार्ग-दृष्टं भूर्-आदि-भोगाश् चफलानि तेषाम् ॥४६॥

The sacrifice, the victim, the fire, the priests, the Soma juice, the gods, the desire for heaven, the path pursued by acts of devotion and the rest, and the worlds that are their consequences, have been displayed to you.

yajñaḥ paśur vahnir aśeṣa-ṛtvik somaḥ surāḥ svarga-mayaś ca kāmaḥ | ity-ādi-karmāśrita-mārga-dṛṣṭaṃ bhūr-ādi-bhogāś caphalāni teṣām ||46 ||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

यज्ञः पशुर् इत्यादिकर्माश्रितमार्गदृष्टञ्चोक्तं भूरादिभोगाश्च तत्फलानीत्युक्तम् ॥४६॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

The sacrifice, victim, fire, priests, Soma, gods, heavenly desire, and their results are the path dependent on action described above.

VP 2.12.47

यच् चैतद् भुवन-गतं मया तवोक्तं सर्वत्र व्रजति हि तत्र कर्म-वश्यः ।
ज्ञात्वैवं ध्रुवम् अचलं सदैक-रूपं तत् कुर्याद् विशति हि येन वासुदेवम् ॥४७॥

In that universe which I have described, he for ever migrates who is subject to the influence of works; but he who knows Vāsudeva to be eternal, immutable, and of one unchanging, universal form, may continue to perform them, as thereby he enters into the deity.

bhuvana-gatam — moving in the universe; karma-vaśaḥ — subject to action; dhruvam — eternal; acalam — unmoving; sadā-eka-rūpam — always one-form; viśati — enters; vāsudevam — Vasudeva.

Transliteration

yac caitad bhuvana-gataṃ mayā tavoktaṃ sarvatra vrajati hi tatra karma-vaśyaḥ | jñātvaivaṃ dhruvam acalaṃ sadaika-rūpaṃ tat kuryād viśati hi yena vāsudevam ||47 ||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तेषु वैराग्योत्पादनेन स्वकर्मानुष्ठानद्वारा श्रीवासुदेवप्राप्त्यर्थम् इत्य् आह—यच्चैतद् इति । कर्मवश्यो जीवो नाना योनीर्ब्रजतीति ज्ञात्वा विरक्तः सन् ध्रुवं नित्यमचलं परिपूर्णम् अतः सर्वदैकरूपं वासुदेवं येनोपायेन स्वकर्मादिना तद्भजनात्तत्प्रसादाल्लब्धज्ञानेन विशति तद् एव कुर्यादित्यर्थः ॥४६॥
इति श्रीधरकृतायामात्मप्रकाशाभिधायां स्वप्रकाशाख्यायां वा श्री-विष्णु-पुराण-टीकायां द्वितीयांशे द्वादशोऽध्यायः ॥
इति श्री-विष्णु-पुराणे द्वितीयांशे द्वादशोऽध्यायः ॥
—ओ)०(ओ—

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Knowing that the bound soul wanders through many births, one should become dispassionate and perform the practice by which, through one’s own action and the Lord’s grace, one enters the eternal, unchanging Vāsudeva.
