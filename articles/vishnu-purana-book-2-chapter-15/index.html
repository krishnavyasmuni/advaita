VP 2.15.1

इत्य् उक्ते मौनिनं भूयश् चिन्तयानं महीपतिम्।
प्रत्युवाचाथ विप्रोऽसाव् अद्वैतन्तर्गतां कृतष्टम्॥१॥

When this had been said, the Brahman then addressed the king, who was silent and still thinking, with a teaching concerning non-duality.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

ity ukte — when this was said; mauninam — the silent/meditating one; bhūyaḥ cintayānam — thinking further; mahīpatim — the king; pratyuvāca atha — then he replied; vipraḥ asau — that Brahman; advaita-antargatām kathām — a teaching concerning non-duality

Śrīdhara

pramāṇa-tarkataḥ — through means of knowledge and reasoning; samyak — properly; māne meye api niścite — even when means and object are ascertained; buddhi-asambhāvanā-utthityai — to remove intellectual doubt; Nidāgha-ākhyānam īryyate — the account of Nidāgha is uttered; iti uktē — when this is said; ātma-aikye nirūpite api — even when the unity of the self is established; viparīta-bhāvanā-dārḍhyāt — because contrary conviction is strong; buddhi-asambhāvanayā — through doubt in the intellect; mauninam tūṣṇīm cintayānam — the silent one sitting and thinking; bhūyaḥ vipraḥ pratyuvāca — the Brahman again replied

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

ityukte mauninaṃ bhūyaścintayānaṃ mahīpatim | pratyuvācātha vipro’sāvadvaitāntargatāṃ kathām ||1||

Śrīdhara

pramāṇatarkataḥ samyaṅ māne meye’pi niścite | buddhyasambhāvanotthityai nidāghākhyānamīryyate || ityukte pramāṇopapattibhyāmātyaikye nirūpite’pi viparīte bhāvanādārḍhyāt buddhyasambhāvanayā mauninaṃ tuṣṇīṃ cintayānaṃ bhūyo vipraḥ pratyuvāca ||1-2||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

प्रमाणतर्कतः सम्यङ् माने मेयेऽपि निश्चिते । बुद्ध्यसम्भावनोत्थित्यै निदाघाख्यानमीर्य्यते ॥ इत्युक्ते प्रमाणोपपत्तिभ्यामात्यैक्ये निरूपितेऽपि विपरीते भावनादार्ढ्यात् बुद्ध्यसम्भावनया मौनिनं तुष्णीं चिन्तयानं भूयो विप्रः प्रत्युवाच ॥१-२॥

Śrīdhara commentary (verses 1–2).

Even when the means of knowledge and the object of knowledge have been properly ascertained through valid knowledge and reasoning, the account of Nidāgha is uttered to remove intellectual doubt. Although the unity of the self has been established through valid knowledge and reasoning, because contrary conviction is strong, the Brahman again addressed the silent one who was thinking, with doubt in his understanding.

Śrīdhara commentary (verses 25–25).

Not only am I of this nature; you and others also are all of this nature. An objection is raised: ‘There is the perception “I go,” and the perception of you and others going in bounded ways; how can what is contrary to perception be stated?’ The answer is: ‘Nor are you [you].’ The body perceived as limited and imagined by you to be the agent of going is not you. Likewise, the others perceived as localized are not those true selves. I too am not as I am seen by you; rather, the Supreme Self, the object of the usages ‘you,’ ‘I,’ and so on, is all of us. Therefore there is no contradiction with perception.

त्वयाप्यनयैव दृष्ट्या भोक्तव्यमित्याह—तदेतद् इति । मृष्टामृष्टविचारशीलं यत् मनः तत् समतालम्बनशीलं कार्यं, यतो मृष्टं भुञ्जानस्यापि मनसः साम्यं मुक्तये भवति ॥३१-३३॥

Śrīdhara commentary (verses 31–33).

With this same vision you too should eat. The mind that considers ‘this is fine’ and ‘this is unfine,’ and ‘I am a god’ and ‘I am a human,’ should be made to rest in equality, because sustained equality is for liberation.

प्रज्ञादानाय इति—प्रज्ञा अत्र उपासनात्मिका, विज्ञाय प्रज्ञां कृवीत् इति श्रुतेः ॥३४॥

‘For giving insight’: insight here has the nature of contemplative practice, as the scripture says, ‘Having known insight, one should cultivate it.’

VP 2.15.35

एवम् एकम् इदं विद्धि न भेदि सकलं जगत्।
वासुदेवाभिधेयस्य स्वरूपं परमात्मअः॥३५॥

“Know this whole universe to be one and undivided: it is the form of the Supreme Self called Vāsudeva.”

Śrīdhara

na bhedi — not divided; bhedavat na bhavati — it does not become possessed of difference

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

evamekamidaṃ viddhi na bhedi sakalaṃ jagat | vāsudevābhidheyasya svarūpaṃ paramātmanaḥ ||35||

Śrīdhara

na bhedi bhedavanna bhavati ||35-36||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

न भेदि भेदवन्न भवति ॥३५-३६॥

Śrīdhara commentary (verses 35–36).

Thus the meaning is made clear. Know the nature of the self as one and equal in the form of knowledge, as stated. ‘Not divided’ means free from distinctions such as gods and humans, and also free from hunger and thirst. Further, the entire conscious and non-conscious universe is the body of the Supreme Self called Vāsudeva, in the sense that he pervades it. The oneness taught here is equality in the form of knowledge, not identity of the individual self and the Supreme Self. Because the world is taught to be Vāsudeva’s body, identity of their essences is not intended.
