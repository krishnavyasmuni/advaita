VP 3.15.1

और्व उवाच— ब्राह्मणान् भोजयेच् छ्राद्धे यद् गुणांस् तान् निबोध मे

O King, learn from me the qualifications of the Brāhmaṇas who are to be fed at a śrāddha.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
brāhmaṇān bhojayet śrāddhe — one should feed Brāhmaṇas at a śrāddha;
yad-guṇān tān nibodha me — learn from me their qualifications
Śrīdhara
iti kṛtyān vidhīn atra — thus, the prescribed acts and procedures here;
śrāddhīyān brāhmaṇān api — the Brāhmaṇas suitable for śrāddha also;
bhoktuḥ kartṛś ca varjyāṇi — what the eater and performer must avoid;
pārvaṇa-śrāddha-prayogān vakṣyan — about to explain the procedure of the fortnightly śrāddha;
yad-guṇān yair guṇaiḥ yuktān — those possessing the required qualities;
ṣaḍ-aṅga-vit — one who knows the six Vedāṅgas
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

brāhmaṇānbhojayecchrāddhe yadguṇāṃstānnibodha me ||1||

iti kṛtyān vidhīnatra śrāddhīyān brāhmaṇānapi | bhoktuḥ kartṛśca varjyāni prāha pañcadaśe muniḥ | atha pārvaṇaśrāddhaprayogān vakṣyan prathamaṃ śrāddhīyān brāhmaṇānāha—brāhmaṇān iti tribhiḥ | yad-guṇān yair guṇair yuktān | dvitīyakaṭhikasthāstrayo'nuvākāstriṇāciketāḥ, tadadhyāyī tadanuṣṭhātā ca triṇāciketaḥ | madhuvātā iti tryṛcādhyāyī tadvṛtaśca trimadhuḥ | brāhmaṇenmān ityādyanuvākatrayādhyāyī tadvṛtaśca trisuparṇaḥ | ṣaṭ aṅgāni yasya taṃ vedamadhīte vetti vā ṣaḍaṅgavit

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

इति कृत्यान् विधीनत्र श्राद्धीयान् ब्राह्मणानपि । भोक्तुः कर्तृश्च वर्ज्यानि प्राह पञ्चदशे मुनिः । अथ पार्वणश्राद्धप्रयोगान् वक्ष्यन् प्रथमं श्राद्धीयान् ब्राह्मणानाह—ब्राह्मणान् इति त्रिभिः । यद्-गुणान् यैर् गुणैर् युक्तान् । द्वितीयकठिकस्थास्त्रयोऽनुवाकास्त्रिणाचिकेताः, तदध्यायी तदनुष्ठाता च त्रिणाचिकेतः । मधुवाता इति त्र्यृचाध्यायी तद्वृतश्च त्रिमधुः । ब्राह्मणेन्मान् इत्याद्यनुवाकत्रयाध्यायी तद्वृतश्च त्रिसुपर्णः । षट् अङ्गानि यस्य तं वेदमधीते वेत्ति वा षडङ्गवित्

Śrīdhara commentary.

The sage describes here the prescribed acts and procedures, the Brāhmaṇas fit for śrāddha, and what the eater and performer must avoid. Now, while about to explain the procedure of the fortnightly śrāddha, he first describes the Brāhmaṇas fit for it, beginning with “Brāhmaṇas,” in three verses. “Their qualifications” means those endowed with these qualities. Triṇāciketa means one who has studied and practices the three Anuvākas of the second section of the Kāṭhaka. Trimadhu means one who has studied and practices the three ṛcs beginning “Madhuvātā”; alternatively, one devoted to the Madhu-vidyā taught in the Chāndogya. Trisuparṇa means one who has studied and practices the three Anuvākas beginning “Brahmametu mām”; alternatively, the three ṛcs. Ṣaḍaṅgavit is one who studies or knows the Veda together with its six limbs.

Śrīdhara commentary.

A daughter’s son is a dauhitra; Kutapa is the eighth muhūrta. With the reading “dauhitra,” it means a special kind of ghee, explained by the verse about the cow that eats grass on the new moon. Some say dauhitra means a vessel shaped like a sword; some say Kutapa is a blanket made from goat hair. Speaking of silver, seeing it, and similar acts are also included. A silver or silver-coated vessel is called a pure vessel for the ancestors; the gift and sight of silver are praised in the Matsya and Vāyu texts.

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

सोमाधारः पितृगणः अग्निष्वात्तादेराधिकारिको गणः सोमाधारः सोमोपजीवित्वात् । योगाधारश्चन्द्रमास्तेषामेवयोगबलेन पुनश्चन्द्रस्याप्यायनात् । तथा च हरिवंशे— एतेऽस्मत्पितरस्तात\! योगिनां योगवर्द्धनाः । आप्याययन्ति ये पूर्वं सोमं योगबलेन वै ॥ तथा वायुः— श्राद्धे प्रीताः पुनः सोमं पितरो योगमाश्रिताः । आप्याययन्ति योगेन त्रैलोक्यं येन जीवति ॥ तस्मात् श्राद्धे योगिनिमन्त्रणं शस्तम् इति

Śrīdhara commentary.

The ancestral host has Soma as its support: it is the group including the Agniṣvāttas, who depend on Soma. The Moon has yoga as its support, because through the power of their yoga the Moon is replenished again. The Harivaṃśa says: “These are our ancestors, dear one, increasers of the yogins’ yoga; they first replenish Soma through the power of yoga.” The Vāyu likewise says: “The ancestors, pleased at the śrāddha and abiding in yoga, again replenish Soma through yoga, by which the three worlds live.” Therefore inviting a yogin at a śrāddha is praised.
