VP 1.2.1

अविकाराय शुद्धाय नित्याय परमात्मने।
सदैकरूपरूपाय विष्णवे सर्वजिष्णवे॥१॥

Glory to the unchangeable, holy, eternal, supreme Viṣṇu, of one universal nature, the mighty over all:

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

avikārāya — to the unchanging; śuddhāya — to the pure; nityāya — to the eternal; paramātmane — to the Supreme Self; sadaika-rūpa-rūpāya — whose form is always one; viṣṇave — to Viṣṇu; sarva-jit — who conquers all.

Śrīdhara

atha — now; purāṇa-pratipādyam — the subject of the Purāṇa; viṣṇoḥ svarūpam — Viṣṇu’s nature; parāpara-rūpeṇa — in higher and lower forms; purāṇārtham — the Purāṇa’s meaning; vikārādi-prasaṅgam — the implication of change; ekam eva rūpam — whose form is one alone; sarva-jit — who surpasses all agents and causes.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

avikārāya śuddhāya nityāya paramātmane |
sadaikarūparūpāya viṣṇave sarvajiṣṇave ||1||

Śrīdhara

athaitat purāṇapratipādyaṃ viṣṇoḥ svarūpaṃ parāpararūpeṇa praṇaman vistareṇa purāṇārthaṃ vaktum āha—avikārāyeti navabhiḥ | ślokaḥ tatra tāvat pūrvādhyāyānte purāṇārthaṃ saṅkṣepaślokena jagadupādānakartṛtvadyuktavā prāptaṃ vikārādiprasaṅgaṃ vārayan praṇamati | loke hi kriyāśrayatvāt kartā vikārī dṛṣṭaḥ | rāgādimāṃścānityaś ca, sāhaṅkāraś ca etad vyāvarttayati-avikārāyeti caturbhiḥ padaiḥ | upādānakāraṇam api mṛtasuvarṇādi-ghaṭakaṭakādinānāpariṇāmairanekarūpaṃ dṛṣṭaṃ, tad vyāvarttayati-ekarūpam eva rūpaṃ yasya tasmai | sadeti sarvatrānvayaḥ | tatra hetuḥ, sarvajiṣṇave-acintyaiśvaryeṇa sarvān kartṛkāraṇādīn jetum atiśayituṃ śīlaṃ yasya tasmai, evambhūtāya viṣṇave nama ityuttareṇānvayaḥ ||1||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

अथैतत् पुराणप्रतिपाद्यं विष्णोः स्वरूपं परापररूपेण प्रणमन् विस्तरेण पुराणार्थं वक्तुम् आह—अविकारायेति नवभिः । श्लोकः तत्र तावत् पूर्वाध्यायान्ते पुराणार्थं सङ्क्षेपश्लोकेन जगदुपादानकर्तृत्वद्युक्तवा प्राप्तं विकारादिप्रसङ्गं वारयन् प्रणमति । लोके हि क्रियाश्रयत्वात् कर्ता विकारी दृष्टः । रागादिमांश्चानित्यश् च, साहङ्कारश् च एतद् व्यावर्त्तयति-अविकारायेति चतुर्भिः पदैः । उपादानकारणम् अपि मृतसुवर्णादि-घटकटकादिनानापरिणामैरनेकरूपं दृष्टं, तद् व्यावर्त्तयति-एकरूपम् एव रूपं यस्य तस्मै । सदेति सर्वत्रान्वयः । तत्र हेतुः, सर्वजिष्णवे-अचिन्त्यैश्वर्येण सर्वान् कर्तृकारणादीन् जेतुम् अतिशयितुं शीलं यस्य तस्मै, एवम्भूताय विष्णवे नम इत्युत्तरेणान्वयः ॥१॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

After saluting Viṣṇu, whose higher and lower forms are the subject of this Purāṇa, the meaning is explained at length, beginning with nine verses headed by “avikārāya.” At the end of the preceding chapter, the Purāṇa’s meaning was stated in brief, including Viṣṇu’s being the material and efficient cause of the world; this could suggest that he undergoes change, so that implication is removed here. In ordinary experience an agent is changeable because he is the locus of action, and is associated with attachment and other conditions; the words “avikārāya” and the following terms remove those implications. A material cause is also seen to take many forms through transformation, as clay or gold becomes pots and ornaments; “sadaikarūparūpāya” removes that implication, meaning “to him whose form is always one.” “Sada” is to be understood with all the terms. “Sarvajiṣṇu” means one who, through inconceivable sovereignty, surpasses all agents, causes, and the rest. To such Viṣṇu the salutation is offered.

VP 1.2.2

नमो हिरण्यगर्भाय हरये शङ्कराय च।
वासुदेवाय ताराय सर्गस्थित्य् अन्तकारिणी॥२॥

to him who is Hiraṇyagarbha, Hari, and Śaṅkara, the creator, the preserver, and destroyer of the world:

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

namaḥ — obeisance; hiraṇyagarbhāya — to Hiraṇyagarbha; haraye — to Hari; śaṅkarāya ca — and to Śaṅkara; vāsudevāya — to Vāsudeva; tārāya — to the deliverer; sarga-sthity-antakāriṇī — who causes creation, preservation, and dissolution.

Śrīdhara

brahmādi-tri-rūpeṇa — in the three forms beginning with Brahmā; sarga-sthity-anta-kāriṇe — who causes creation, preservation, and dissolution; saṃsāra-pravartakatvam — the power that sets worldly existence in motion; tārāya — to the deliverer; bhaktānām — of devotees.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

namo hiraṇyagarbhāya haraye śaṅkarāya ca |
vāsudevāya tārāya sargasthity antakāriṇī ||2||

Śrīdhara

brahmāditrirtirūpeṇa praṇamati, hiraṇyagarbhādirūpāya, taiḥ rūpaiḥ sargasthity antakāriṇe vāsudevāyaḥ namaḥ | tad evaṃ saṃsārapravartakatvam uktvā tannivartakatvena praṇamati | tārāya bhaktānāṃ tārakāya ca ||2||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

ब्रह्मादित्रिर्तिरूपेण प्रणमति, हिरण्यगर्भादिरूपाय, तैः रूपैः सर्गस्थित्य् अन्तकारिणे वासुदेवायः नमः । तद् एवं संसारप्रवर्तकत्वम् उक्त्वा तन्निवर्तकत्वेन प्रणमति । ताराय भक्तानां तारकाय च ॥२॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Vāsudeva is saluted in the three forms beginning with Hiraṇyagarbha—Brahmā, Viṣṇu, and Śaṅkara—which perform creation, preservation, and dissolution. Having set worldly existence in motion, Vāsudeva is now saluted as the one who brings it to an end. “Tārāya” means “to the deliverer,” the one who carries devotees across saṃsāra.

VP 1.2.3

एकानेक-स्वरूपाय स्थूल-सूक्ष्मात्मने नमः।
अव्यक्त-व्यक्त-रूपाय विष्णवे मुक्ति-हेतवे॥३॥

to Vāsudeva, the liberator of his worshippers: to him, whose essence is both single and manifold; who is both subtile and corporeal, indiscrete and discrete: to Viṣṇu, the cause of final emancipation,

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

eka-aneka-svarūpāya — whose nature is one and many; sthūla-sūkṣmātmane — whose self is gross and subtle; avyakta-vyakta-rūpāya — whose form is unmanifest and manifest; viṣṇave — to Viṣṇu; mukti-hetave — the cause of liberation.

Śrīdhara

eka-anekam svarūpam — one and many as his nature; sthūla-sūkṣma-ātmā — gross and subtle self; avyaktaṃ — the cause; vyaktaṃ — the effect; ubhaya-rūpa — having both forms; mukti-hetu — cause of liberation.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

ekāneka-svarūpāya sthūla-sūkṣmātmane namaḥ |
avyakta-vyakta-rūpāya viṣṇave mukti-hetave ||3||

Śrīdhara

kiñca ekamanekaṃ svarūpaṃ yasya, sthūlaḥ sūkṣmaścātmā svarūpaṃ yasya | virūddhasva-rūpadvayaṃ kathamityatrāha-avyaktavyaktarūpāya | avyaktaṃ kāraṇam, vyaktaṃ kāryaṃ tadubhayarūpāya | ato’vyaktarūpatvādekarūpāya ca | vyaktarūpatvādanekarūpāya sthūlātmane cety arthaḥ | evaṃ sarvātmatvenopāsakānāṃ muktihetave namaḥ ||3||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

किञ्च एकमनेकं स्वरूपं यस्य, स्थूलः सूक्ष्मश्चात्मा स्वरूपं यस्य । विरूद्धस्व-रूपद्वयं कथमित्यत्राह-अव्यक्तव्यक्तरूपाय । अव्यक्तं कारणम्, व्यक्तं कार्यं तदुभयरूपाय । अतोऽव्यक्तरूपत्वादेकरूपाय च । व्यक्तरूपत्वादनेकरूपाय स्थूलात्मने चेत्य् अर्थः । एवं सर्वात्मत्वेनोपासकानां मुक्तिहेतवे नमः ॥३॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

He has one and many forms, and his self is gross and subtle. How can two opposite forms belong to one? The answer is “avyakta-vyakta-rūpāya”: the unmanifest is the cause, the manifest is the effect, and Viṣṇu has both forms. Thus he is one in unmanifest form and many in manifest form; as the Self of all, he is the cause of liberation for worshippers.

VP 1.2.4

सर्ग-स्थिति-विनाशानां जगतो यो जगन्-मयः।
मूल-भूतो नमस् तस्मै विष्णवे परमात्मने॥४॥

Glory to the supreme Viṣṇu, the cause of the creation, existence, and end of this world; who is the root of the world, and who consists of the world.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

sarga-sthiti-vināśānām — of creation, preservation, and destruction; jagataḥ — of the world; yaḥ — who; jagan-mayaḥ — who is the world; mūla-bhūtaḥ — the root cause; viṣṇave — to Viṣṇu; paramātmane — to the Supreme Self.

Śrīdhara

kārya-kāraṇatvam — the status of effect and cause; sarga-ādi — creation and the rest; mūla-bhūta — the root; jagan-maya — pervading the world; sarva-vyāpin — all-pervading; kārya-kāraṇābhyām — through effect and cause.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

sarga-sthiti-vināśānāṃ jagato yo jagan-mayaḥ |
mūla-bhūto namas tasmai viṣṇave paramātmane ||4||

Śrīdhara

kāryakāraṇatvamabhinayan praṇamati-sargeti | jagataḥ sargādīnāṃ yo mūlabhūto jaganmayaś ca ato viṣṇave sarvavyāpine tathā paramātmane kāryakāraṇābhyāṃ parama utkṛṣṭa ātmā svarūpaṃ yasya tatsaṅgarahitāyety arthaḥ ||4||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

कार्यकारणत्वमभिनयन् प्रणमति-सर्गेति । जगतः सर्गादीनां यो मूलभूतो जगन्मयश् च अतो विष्णवे सर्वव्यापिने तथा परमात्मने कार्यकारणाभ्यां परम उत्कृष्ट आत्मा स्वरूपं यस्य तत्सङ्गरहितायेत्य् अर्थः ॥४॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Viṣṇu is both cause and effect. He is the root and essence of the world’s creation, preservation, and destruction; therefore he is all-pervading and is the Supreme Self, transcendent through both cause and effect and untouched by their limitations.

VP 1.2.5

आधार-भूतं विश्वस्याप्य् अणीयांसम् अणीयसाम्।
प्रणम्य सर्व-भूत-स्थम् अच्युतं पुरुषोत्तमम्॥५॥

Having glorified him who is the support of all things; who is the smallest of the small; who is in all created things; the unchanged, imperishable Puruṣottama;

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

ādhāra-bhūtam — the support; viśvasya — of the universe; aṇīyāṃsam aṇīyasām — smaller than the smallest; sarva-bhūta-stham — dwelling in all beings; acyutam — the imperishable; puruṣottamam — the Supreme Person.

Śrīdhara

ādhāra-bhūta — the support; aṇīyāṃsam — more subtle; sarva-bhūteṣu — in all beings; niyantṛtvena — as controller; acyuta — unchanged; aparicchinna — not limited by them.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

ādhāra-bhūtaṃ viśvasyāpy aṇīyāṃsam aṇīyasām |
praṇamya sarva-bhūta-stham acyutaṃ puruṣottamam ||5||

Śrīdhara

ādhārabhūtamity-ādīnāṃ praṇamya kathayāmīti caturthenānvayaḥ | aṇīyasām api aṇīyāṃsamatisūkṣmatamaṃ sarvabhūteṣu niyantṛtvena sthitam | bhūtāntaḥsthatve’pyacyutaṃ tairaparicchinnam ||5||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

आधारभूतमित्य्-आदीनां प्रणम्य कथयामीति चतुर्थेनान्वयः । अणीयसाम् अपि अणीयांसमतिसूक्ष्मतमं सर्वभूतेषु नियन्तृत्वेन स्थितम् । भूतान्तःस्थत्वेऽप्यच्युतं तैरपरिच्छिन्नम् ॥५॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

The words beginning “ādhārabhūtam” are connected with “having bowed, I shall relate.” Viṣṇu is subtler than the subtlest and abides in all beings as their controller. Though he is present within beings, he is not limited or circumscribed by them.

VP 1.2.6

ज्ञान-स्वरूपम् अत्यन्त-निर्मलं परमार्थतः।
तम् एवार्थ-स्वरूपेण भ्रान्ति-दर्शनतः स्थितम्॥६॥

who is one with true wisdom, as truly known; eternal and incorrupt; and who is known through false appearances by the nature of visible objects:

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

jñāna-svarūpam — whose nature is knowledge; atyanta-nirmalam — supremely pure; paramārthataḥ — in reality; tam eva — that very one; artha-svarūpeṇa — as the form of objects; bhrānti-darśanataḥ — through deluded perception; sthitam — appearing as established.

Śrīdhara

artha-svarūpa — form of an object; dṛśya-rūpa — visible form; draṣṭṛ-jīva — the witnessing individual; bhrānti-jñāna — deluded knowledge; pratīta — appearing.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

jñāna-svarūpam atyanta-nirmalaṃ paramārthataḥ |
tam evārtha-svarūpeṇa bhrānti-darśanataḥ sthitam ||6||

Śrīdhara

arthasvarūpeṇa dṛśyarūpeṇa draṣṭrajīvabhrāntijñānena sthitaṃ pratītam ||6||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

अर्थस्वरूपेण दृश्यरूपेण द्रष्ट्रजीवभ्रान्तिज्ञानेन स्थितं प्रतीतम् ॥६॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

“Artha-svarūpeṇa” means in the form of an object, the visible form. That form is apprehended through the deluded knowledge of the witnessing individual; in reality Viṣṇu is pure knowledge and supremely stainless.

VP 1.2.7

विष्णुं ग्रसिष्णुं विश्वस्य स्थितौ सर्गे तथा प्रभुम्।
प्रणम्य जगताम् ईशम् अजम् अक्षयम् अव्ययम्॥७॥

having bowed to Viṣṇu, the destroyer, and lord of creation and preservation; the ruler of the world; unborn, imperishable, undecaying:

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

viṣṇum — Viṣṇu; grasiṣṇum — the devourer; viśvasya — of the universe; sthitau — in preservation; sarge — in creation; prabhum — the lord; jagatām īśam — the ruler of worlds; ajam — unborn; akṣayam — imperishable; avyayam — undecaying.

Śrīdhara

grasiṣṇu — the devourer; kāla-rūpeṇa — in the form of Time; apunarukta — not redundant; namasya-svarūpa-bheda — difference in the form saluted; akṣara — imperishable; acala — immovable.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

viṣṇuṃ grasiṣṇuṃ viśvasya sthitau sarge tathā prabhum |
praṇamya jagatām īśam ajam akṣayam avyayam ||7||

Śrīdhara

viṣṇuṃ grasiṣṇum ity anena kālarūpeṇa praṇāmaḥ | ataḥ sargasthity antakāriṇe ity anenāpaunaruktam ata eva namasyasvarūpabhedadyotanārthaṃ praṇamyaitasyāvṛttiḥ | akṣaram acalam ||7-8||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

विष्णुं ग्रसिष्णुम् इत्य् अनेन कालरूपेण प्रणामः । अतः सर्गस्थित्य् अन्तकारिणे इत्य् अनेनापौनरुक्तम् अत एव नमस्यस्वरूपभेदद्योतनार्थं प्रणम्यैतस्यावृत्तिः । अक्षरम् अचलम् ॥७-८॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

“Viṣṇu, the devourer” is a salutation to him in the form of Time. It is therefore not redundant with the earlier salutation to the one who causes creation, preservation, and dissolution; the repetition indicates a difference in the form being saluted. “Akṣara” means imperishable and “acala” means immovable.

VP 1.2.8

कथयामि यथा-पूर्वं दक्षाद्यैर् मुनि-सत्तमैः।
पृष्टः प्रोवाच भगवान् अब्ज-योनिः पितामहः॥८॥

I will relate to you that which was originally imparted by the great father of all (Brahmā), in answer to the questions of Dakṣa and other venerable sages, and repeated by them to Purukutsa, a king who reigned on the banks of the Narmadā.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

kathayāmi — I shall relate; yathā-pūrvam — as formerly; dakṣādyaiḥ — by Dakṣa and the others; muni-sattamaiḥ — the noblest sages; pṛṣṭaḥ — being asked; provāca — spoke; bhagavān — the Blessed Lord; abja-yoniḥ — the lotus-born.

Śrīdhara

grasiṣṇu — the devourer; kāla-rūpeṇa — in the form of Time; apunarukta — not redundant; namasya-svarūpa-bheda — difference in the form saluted; akṣara — imperishable; acala — immovable.

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

kathayāmi yathā-pūrvaṃ dakṣādyair muni-sattamaiḥ |

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

After the position of the egg’s interior has been described, its exterior is described. Beyond the shell-like earth covering, the egg is successively enclosed by water, fire, air, and ether, each ten times larger than the layer it encloses. If the earth covering measures a crore yojanas, the water covering is ten times that, the fire covering ten times the water, and so on: the seven coverings from earth onward increase tenfold. The covering of Prakṛti is the eighth and immeasurable. Śuka says that the egg is externally covered by eight coverings beginning with earth; therefore, distinguishing Prakṛti, the text calls the other seven “natural” coverings.

Beyond the shell-like earth covering, the egg is successively enclosed by water, fire, air, and ether, each ten times larger than the layer it encloses. If the earth covering measures a crore yojanas, the water covering is ten times that, the fire covering ten times the water, and so on: the seven coverings from earth onward increase tenfold. The covering of Prakṛti is the eighth and immeasurable. Śuka says that the egg is externally covered by eight coverings beginning with earth; therefore, distinguishing Prakṛti, the text calls the other seven “natural” coverings.
