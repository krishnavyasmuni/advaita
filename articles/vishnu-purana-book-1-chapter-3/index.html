VP 1.3.1

निर्गुणस्याप्रमेयस्य शुद्धस्याप्य् अमलात्मनः।
कथं सर्गादि-कर्तादि-कर्तृत्वं ब्रह्मणोऽभ्युपगम्यत॥१॥

Śrīdhara

tad evaṃ brahmaṇaḥ sṛṣṭyādikartṛtvamuktam, tatra śaṅkate-nirguṇasya iti-sattvādiguṇarahitasya | aprameyasya-deśakālādyanavacchinnasya | śuddhasya adehasya sahakāraśūnyasyeti vā | evam amalātmanaḥ puṇyapāpasaṃskāraśūnyasya rāgādiśūnyasyeti vā | evambhūtasya brahmaṇaḥ kathaṃ sargādikartṛtvamiṣyate, etadvilakṣaṇasyaiva loke ghaṭādiṣu kartṛtvādidarśanādity arthaḥ ||1||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तद् एवं ब्रह्मणः सृष्ट्यादिकर्तृत्वमुक्तम्, तत्र शङ्कते-निर्गुणस्य इति-सत्त्वादिगुणरहितस्य । अप्रमेयस्य-देशकालाद्यनवच्छिन्नस्य । शुद्धस्य अदेहस्य सहकारशून्यस्येति वा । एवम् अमलात्मनः पुण्यपापसंस्कारशून्यस्य रागादिशून्यस्येति वा । एवम्भूतस्य ब्रह्मणः कथं सर्गादिकर्तृत्वमिष्यते, एतद्विलक्षणस्यैव लोके घटादिषु कर्तृत्वादिदर्शनादित्य् अर्थः ॥१॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

Thus Brahmā’s authorship of creation and the rest has been stated; the objection is now raised. “Of the qualityless one” means one free from the qualities beginning with sattva. “Immeasurable” means not delimited by place, time, and the like. “Pure” may mean bodiless and without auxiliary causes; “stainless in self” may likewise mean free from the impressions of merit and demerit and from attachment and the rest. How can Brahmā, being such, be accepted as the creator of creation and so forth, when in the world agency over pots and similar things is seen only in one different from this? That is the meaning.

मायिनं तु महेश्वरम् [श्वे. ६.८] इत्य्-आदिः ।
यद् वा, एवं योजना-सर्वेषां भावानां पावकस्योष्णताशक्तिवदचिन्त्यज्ञानगोचराः शक्तयः सन्त्य् एव । ब्रह्मणः पुनस्ताः स्वभावभूताः स्वरूपादभिन्नाः शक्तयः, परास्य शक्तिर् विविधैव श्रूयते इत्य्-आदिश्रुतेः । अतो मणिमन्त्रादिभिरग्न्यौष्ण्यवन्न केनचिद् विहन्तुं शक्यते, अत एव तस्य निरङ्कुशम् ऐश्वर्यम् । तथा च श्रुतिः—च वा अयम् आत्मा सर्वस्य वशी सर्वस्येशानः सर्वस्याधिपतिर् [बृ.आ.उ. ४.४.२२] इत्य्-आदिः । तपतां श्रेष्ठ इति सम्बोधयन् या कापि तपःशक्तिः स्वयं वेद्येति सूचयति । यत एवम्, अतो ब्रह्मणो हेतोः सर्गाद्या भवन्ति, नात्र काचिद् अनुपपत्तिर् इत्य् अर्थः ॥२॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

The objection is answered in a verse and a half beginning “śaktayaḥ.” In the world, the powers of all entities—such as jewels and mantras—are beyond ordinary thought, yet they are accessible to knowledge inferred from the impossibility of explaining their effects otherwise. Alternatively, they are inconceivable because their being different, non-different, and so on cannot be established by reasoning; they are known only through such necessary inference. Since this is so, Brahman—Śrī Viṣṇu—also possesses essential powers that are the causes of creation and the other cosmic functions, just as fire possesses heat and the power to burn. Therefore Brahmā’s creative agency is possible even though he is free from the guṇas, because he possesses inconceivable powers. The śruti says: “He has neither work nor instrument; his equal or superior is nowhere seen. His supreme power is heard of as manifold, and its knowledge, strength, and action are intrinsic.” It also teaches, “Know Māyā to be Prakṛti and the Lord of Māyā to be the great Lord,” and similar passages. Another arrangement of the argument is this: every entity has powers, like fire’s power of heat, that are inaccessible to discursive thought but known through their proper evidence. In Brahman these powers are intrinsic and non-different from his nature, as the śruti says that his supreme power is manifold. They cannot be obstructed by anything, just as the heat of fire cannot be prevented by jewels or mantras; therefore his sovereignty is unrestricted. The śruti also says, “This Self is the ruler of all, the lord of all, and the sovereign of all.” By addressing Parāśara as “best of those who practise austerity,” the text indicates that the power of austerity is directly known by the ascetic himself. Since this is so, creation and the other functions arise from Brahman, and there is no inconsistency.

bhū-bhūbhṛt-sāgarādīnām aśeṣāṇāṃ ca sattama ||7||

Śrīdhara

tad eva nimeṣādikrameṇa nirūpayati-kāṣṭhetyādinā | akṣipakṣanikṣepopalakṣitaḥ kālo nimeṣaḥ | pañcadaśa nimeṣāḥ ekā kāṣṭhoktā, triṃśat kāṣṭhā ekā kalā | triṃśata kalāḥ ekā ghaṭikā | te dve mauhūrtiko vidhiḥ muhūrtamānaprakāraḥ ||7||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

तद् एव निमेषादिक्रमेण निरूपयति-काष्ठेत्यादिना । अक्षिपक्षनिक्षेपोपलक्षितः कालो निमेषः । पञ्चदश निमेषाः एका काष्ठोक्ता, त्रिंशत् काष्ठा एका कला । त्रिंशत कलाः एका घटिका । ते द्वे मौहूर्तिको विधिः मुहूर्तमानप्रकारः ॥७॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

The text now explains the same matter in the order beginning with the twinkling of an eye. A Nimeṣa is the time marked by the movement of the eyelid. Fifteen Nimeṣas are said to make one Kāṣṭhā, and thirty Kāṣṭhās one Kalā. Thirty Kalās make one Ghaṭikā; two Ghaṭikās make the rule or measure of a Muhūrtta.

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

The text now explains the solar course through solar months. By “those” the solar months are intended, since they differ little from the lunar months. A solar month is the Sun’s conjunction with one sign beginning with Aries; six such months make an Ayana. The joining in “two Ayanas” is an archaic usage. The text then turns to divine reckoning: the southern Ayana is the night of the gods and the northern Ayana their day. A year of the gods is to be understood from the stated number of such day-and-nights.

Śrīdhara

mahākalpa iti-avāntarakalpa eva puṣkara-prādurbhāvādi-guṇair mahattvān mahā-kalpa ity ucyate ||27||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

महाकल्प इति-अवान्तरकल्प एव पुष्कर-प्रादुर्भावादि-गुणैर् महत्त्वान् महा-कल्प इत्य् उच्यते ॥२७॥

Śrīdhara’s Commentary.

“Mahā-Kalpa” means an intermediate Kalpa that is called great because of qualities such as the manifestation of Puṣkara and the rest.

VP 1.3.28

द्वितीयस्य परार्धस्य वर्तमानस्य वै द्विज।
वाराह इति कल्पोऽयं प्रथमः परिकीर्तितः॥२८॥

The Kalpa (or day of Brahmā) termed Vārāha is the first of the second period of Brahmā's existence.
