VP 3.1.1

कथिता गुरुणा सम्यग्भूसमुद्रादिसंस्थितिः । सूर्यादीनां च संस्थानं ज्योतिषां चातिविस्तरात्

What was fully narrated by you, my teacher, is the arrangement of the earth, oceans, and the rest; also the arrangement of the sun and other luminaries, and of the stars in great detail.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

kathitā gurunā samyak — fully narrated by the teacher; bhū-samudra-ādi-saṃsthitiḥ — arrangement of earth, oceans, and the rest; sūrya-ādīnām ca saṃsthānam — arrangement of the sun and others; jyotiṣām ca ati-vistarāt — and of the luminaries in great detail

Śrīdhara

parānanda-padāmbhoja-śrīdharaḥ śrīdharo yatiḥ — Śrīdhara is the ascetic whose refuge is the lotus of supreme bliss.; purāṇe vaiṣṇave vyākhyāt tṛtīyāṃśe yathāmati — He has explained the Vaiṣṇava Purāṇa in the third Book, according to his understanding.; aṃśa-dvayena sṛṣṭyādi-dvi-sapta-bhuvanoktibhiḥ — Through the two sections, with their accounts of creation and the fourteen worlds.; adhyāropya niṣiddhaṃ tad rāja-dvija-saduktibhiḥ — There, by the good statements of kings and Brahmins, what is to be rejected is superimposed and explained.; tṛtīyāṃśe manu-vyāsa-dharmādyāḥ sthiti-hetavaḥ — In the third Book, the causes of the order connected with Manu, Vyāsa, dharma, and the rest.; varṇyante'tra niṣedhāya kauravya-pṛtanā yathā — Are described here for prohibition, as [the Kaurava army is described].; atrāpi prathame'dhyāye sapta-manvantarāṇi tu — Here too, in the first chapter, the seven Manvantaras.; bhavad-bhūtāni kathitāny avatārā harer api — The past and future are narrated, as are the descents of Hari.; vṛtta-kīrtana-pūrvakaṃ manvantarāṇāṃ svarūpaṃ pṛcchati — He asks about the nature of the Manvantaras after recounting the preceding matters.; kathitā iti caturbhiḥ — This is done in four verses beginning with 'what was narrated.'

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

kathitā guruṇā samyagbhūsamudrādisaṃsthitiḥ | sūryādīnāṃ ca saṃsthānaṃ jyotiṣāṃ cātivistarāt ||1||

Śrīdhara

parānandapadāmbhojaśrīdharaḥ śrīdharo yatiḥ | purāṇe vaiṣṇave vyākhyāt tṛtīyāṃśe yathāmati || aṃśadvayena sṛṣṭyādidvisaptabhuvanoktibhiḥ | adhyāropya niṣiddhaṃ tad rājadvijasaduktibhiḥ || tṛtīyāṃśe manuvyāsadharmādyāḥ sthitihetavaḥ | varṇyante'tra niṣedhāya kauravyapṛtanā yathā || atrāpi prathame'dhyāye saptamanvantarāṇi tu | bhavadbhūtāni kathitānyavatārā harer api || vṛttakīrtanapūrvakaṃ manvantarāṇāṃ svarūpaṃ pṛcchati—kathitā iti caturbhiḥ

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

परानन्दपदाम्भोज-श्रीधरः श्रीधरो यतिः । पुराणे वैष्णवे व्याख्यात् तृतीयांशे यथामति ॥ अंशद्वयेन सृष्ट्यादिद्विसप्तभुवनोक्तिभिः । अध्यारोप्य निषिद्धं तद् राजद्विजसदुक्तिभिः ॥ तृतीयांशे मनुव्यासधर्माद्याः स्थितिहेतवः । वर्ण्यन्तेऽत्र निषेधाय कौरव्यपृतना यथा ॥ अत्रापि प्रथमेऽध्याये सप्तमन्वन्तराणि तु । भवद्भूतानि कथितान्यवतारा हरेरपि ॥ वृत्तकीर्तनपूर्वकं मन्वन्तराणां स्वरूपं पृच्छति—कथिता इति चतुर्भिः

Śrīdhara commentary (source range 1).

Śrīdhara, the ascetic whose refuge is the lotus of supreme bliss, has explained the Vaiṣṇava Purāṇa in the third Book according to his understanding. Through the two sections, with their accounts of creation and the fourteen worlds, what is to be rejected is explained through the good statements of kings and Brahmins. In the third Book, the causes of the order connected with Manu, Vyāsa, dharma, and the rest are described here for prohibition, as [the Kaurava army is described]. Here too, in the first chapter, the seven Manvantaras—past and future—are narrated, as are the descents of Hari. Maitreya asks about the nature of the Manvantaras after recounting the preceding matters, in four verses beginning with 'what was narrated.'

VP 3.1.5

अतीतानागतानीह यानि मन्वन्तराणि वै । तान्यहं भवतः सम्यक्कथयामि यथाक्रमम्

I will properly narrate to you, in sequence, the Manvantaras that are past and those that are future.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

Śrīdhara

iha vārāha-kalpe — in this Varāha Kalpa; yathākramam — by ‘in sequence’; krama-prāptam vartamānam api manvantaram — the present Manvantara, which has arrived in the sequence; jñeyam — is to be understood

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

इह वाराहकल्पे । यथाक्रममित्यनेन क्रमप्राप्तं वर्तमानमपि मन्वन्तरं ज्ञेयम्

Śrīdhara commentary (source range 5).

In the Varāha Kalpa. By the expression ‘in sequence,’ the present Manvantara, which has arrived in the sequence, is also to be understood.

VP 3.1.8

स्वायंभुवं तु कथितं कल्पादावन्तरं मया । देवास्तत्रर्षयश्चैव यथावत्कथिता मया

I have already described the Svāyambhuva Manvantara at the beginning of the Kalpa; the gods and sages there were also properly described by me.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

svāyambhuvam tu kathitam — the Svāyambhuva has been described; kalpa-ādau antaram mayā — by me as the interval at the beginning of the Kalpa; devāḥ tatra ṛṣayaḥ ca eva — the gods and sages there also; yathāvat kathitāḥ mayā — properly described by me

Śrīdhara

kalpasya ādau — at the beginning of the Kalpa; svāyambhuva-manvantaram tu kathitam — the Svāyambhuva Manvantara was described; prathamāṃśe — in the first Book; devāḥ yāma-ākhyāḥ — the gods called Yāmas; ṛṣayaḥ marīcy-ādayaḥ — the sages beginning with Marīci; cakārāt indraḥ yajñaḥ — by ‘and,’ Indra Yajña; manu-putrau priyavrata-uttānapādau — the two sons of Manu, Priyavrata and Uttānapāda; kathitau — were described

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

svāyaṃbhuvaṃ tu kathitaṃ kalpādāvantaraṃ mayā | devāstatrarṣayaścaiva yathāvatkathitā mayā ||8||

Śrīdhara

kalpasya ādau svāyambhuvamanvantarantu kathitaṃ, prathamāṃśe devā yāmākhyā ṛṣayo marīcyādayaḥ | cakārādindro yajñaḥ | manuputrau priyavratottānapādau kathitau ||8||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

कल्पस्य आदौ स्वायम्भुवमन्वन्तरन्तु कथितं, प्रथमांशे देवा यामाख्या ऋषयो मरीच्यादयः । चकारादिन्द्रो यज्ञः । मनुपुत्रौ प्रियव्रतोत्तानपादौ कथितौ

Śrīdhara commentary (source range 8).

At the beginning of the Kalpa, the Svāyambhuva Manvantara was described. In the first Book, the gods called Yāmas and the sages beginning with Marīci were [described]. By the word ‘and,’ Indra Yajña is included. The two sons of Manu, Priyavrata and Uttānapāda, were described.

Śrīdhara commentary (source range 34).

‘Vasumān’ and ‘renowned in the world’ are two qualifying expressions. Pṛṣadhra became Vasumān, possessed of strength, without agitation even after learning of Vaśiṣṭha’s curse; he became renowned in the world through abandoning all attachment and attaining liberation. Thus the statement that there were nine very powerful sons is justified.

VP 3.1.35

विष्णुशक्तिरनौपम्या सत्त्वोद्रिक्ता स्थितौ स्थिता । मन्वन्तरेष्वशेषेषु देवत्वेनाधितिष्ठति

Word-for-word

Viṣṇu Purāṇa

Śrīdhara

evaṃ tarhi manuputradevendrarṣibhiḥ pañcabhireva jagataḥ pālane siddhe kṛtaṃ viṣṇoḥ pālakatvena ityāśaṅkya sarveṣvapi manvantareṣu pālane pravṛttasya viṣṇoradhiṣṭhātṛtvaṃ darśayati—viṣṇuśaktir ityādinā yāvadadhyāyasamāptiḥ | devayajñādirūpadevatābhāvena viṣṇoḥ svarūpabhūtā śaktiḥ, tasya viṣṇoḥ aṃśena svāyambhuvamanvantare ākūtyāṃ mātari yajñasañjñaḥ utpannaḥ jajñe babhūvetyarthaḥ yadvā prathame antare avasare brahmaṇo mānasaḥ yaḥ utpanno ruciḥ tasmāt pituḥ jajñe iti ||35-36||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

एवं तर्हि मनुपुत्रदेवेन्द्रर्षिभिः पञ्चभिरेव जगतः पालने सिद्धे कृतं विष्णोः पालकत्वेन इत्याशङ्क्य सर्वेष्वपि मन्वन्तरेषु पालने प्रवृत्तस्य विष्णोरधिष्ठातृत्वं दर्शयति—विष्णुशक्तिर् इत्यादिना यावदध्यायसमाप्तिः । देवयज्ञादिरूपदेवताभावेन विष्णोः स्वरूपभूता शक्तिः, तस्य विष्णोः अंशेन स्वायम्भुवमन्वन्तरे आकूत्यां मातरि यज्ञसञ्ज्ञः उत्पन्नः जज्ञे बभूवेत्यर्थः यद्वा प्रथमे अन्तरे अवसरे ब्रह्मणो मानसः यः उत्पन्नो रुचिः तस्मात् पितुः जज्ञे इति

Śrīdhara commentary (source range 35–36).

Having supposed that the world’s protection is accomplished by only five—the sons of Manu, Indra, and the sages—and asked what purpose Viṣṇu’s role as protector serves, the text shows Viṣṇu’s presiding authority in protection throughout all the Manvantaras, beginning with ‘the power of Viṣṇu’ and continuing to the end of the chapter. The power that is the essential nature of Viṣṇu, in the form of the divinity of Yajña and the like, is meant. By a portion of Viṣṇu, Yajña was born in the Svāyambhuva Manvantara in the mother Ākūti. Alternatively, in the first interval, he was born from the father Ruci, who arose from Brahmā’s mind.
