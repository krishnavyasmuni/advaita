VP 3.2.1

प्रोक्तान्य् एतानि भवता सप्त-मन्वन्तराणि वै।
भविष्याण्य् अपि विप्रर्षे ममाख्यातुं त्वम् अर्हसि

You have told me the seven Manvantaras; O foremost of brahman-seers, you should also tell me the future ones.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
proktānyetāni bhavatā sapta manvantarāṇi vai — these have been told by you;
indeed the seven Manvantaras;
bhaviṣyāṇyapi viprarṣe mamākhyātuṃ tvamarhasi — the future ones also;
O best of seers, you should tell me
Śrīdhara
dvitīye sapta manvādīn vakṣyannādau raveḥ sutaḥ . yathā sāvarṇirabhavaditihāsaṃ tamabravīt — In the second chapter he is going to speak of the seven Manus and the rest;
first, the Sun’s son told him the account of how Sāvarṇi came to be.
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

proktānyetāni bhavatā sapta manvantarāṇi vai | bhaviṣyāṇyapi viprarṣe mamākhyātuṃ tvamarhasi ||1||

Śrīdhara

dvitīye sapta manvādīn vakṣyannādau raveḥ sutaḥ । yathā sāvarṇirabhavaditihāsaṃ tamabravīt ||1||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

द्वितीये सप्त मन्वादीन् वक्ष्यन्नादौ रवेः सुतः । यथा सावर्णिरभवदितिहासं तमब्रवीत् ॥१॥

Śrīdhara commentary.

In the second chapter he is going to speak of the seven Manus and the rest; first, the Sun’s son told him the account of how Sāvarṇi came to be.

VP 3.2.5

छाया-सञ्ज्ञा ददौ शापं यमाय कुपिता यदा।
तदान्येयम् असौ बुद्धिर् त्य् आसीद् यम-सूर्ययोः

When Shadow-Saṃjñā became angry, she cursed Yama. Then Yama and the Sun understood that this was another woman, not Saṃjñā.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
chāyāsaṃjñā dadau śāpaṃ yamāya kupitā yadā — Shadow-Saṃjñā gave a curse;
to Yama when angry;
tadā'nyeyamasau buddhirityāsīdyamasūryayoḥ — then the thought “this is another woman”;

tad atinirdayatvam ālakṣya — seeing that extreme harshness;
na iyam asau saṃjñā kintu anyā iyam — she is not Saṃjñā but another woman;
saṃhatiḥ yama-sūryayoḥ buddhiḥ abhūt — Yama and the Sun understood this
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

chāyāsaṃjñā dadau śāpaṃ yamāya kupitā yadā | tadā'nyeyamasau buddhirityāsīdyamasūryayoḥ ||5||

Śrīdhara

chāyaiva sañjñārūpeṇa sthitā chāyāsañjñā । sā kadācit svāpatyatrayasnehavatī ātmani pādaprahārodyatāya yamāya pādaste patatviti śāpaṃ dadau । tadātinirdayatvamālakṣya neyamasau sañjñā, kintu anyeyaṃ saṃhoti yamasūryayorbuddhirabhūdityarthaḥ ||5||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

छायैव सञ्ज्ञारूपेण स्थिता छायासञ्ज्ञा । सा कदाचित् स्वापत्यत्रयस्नेहवती आत्मनि पादप्रहारोद्यताय यमाय पादस्ते पतत्विति शापं ददौ । तदातिनिर्दयत्वमालक्ष्य नेयमसौ सञ्ज्ञा, किन्तु अन्येयं संहोति यमसूर्ययोर्बुद्धिरभूदित्यर्थः ॥५॥

Śrīdhara commentary.

Shadow-Saṃjñā stood in Saṃjñā’s form. Once, partial to her three children, she cursed Yama, who had raised his foot to strike her: “May your foot fall off.” Seeing her extreme harshness, Yama and the Sun understood that she was not Saṃjñā but another woman.

VP 3.2.7

वाजि-रूप-धरः सोऽयं तस्यां देवाव् अथाश्विनौ।
जनयामास रेवन्तं रेतसोऽन्ते च भास्करः

Taking a horse’s form, Bhāskara fathered the two Aśvins and Revanta in her; Revanta was born at the end of the semen.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa
vājirūpadharasso'tha tasyāṃ devāvathāśvinau — he, taking a horse form;
begot the two Aśvins in her;
janayāmāsa revantaṃ retaso'nte ca bhāskaraḥ — Revanta;
at the end of the semen, Bhāskara
Śrīdhara
tataḥ ca atinirbandhaṃ pṛṣṭayā tayā — then, when she was pressed with persistent questioning;
na aham saṃjñā kintu tasyāḥ chāyā iti ākhyāte — having said, “I am not Saṃjñā, but her shadow”;
vivasvān sūryaḥ samādhinā — Vivasvān the Sun through meditation;
uttara-kuruṣu araṇye — in the forest of Uttarakuru;
aśvāṃ vaḍavā-rūpāṃ tapasi sthitāṃ saṃjñām dadarśa — he saw Saṃjñā, in a mare's form, engaged in austerity;
tataḥ saḥ api aśva-rūpa-dharaḥ — then he too assumed a horse's form;
tasyām aśvinau devau revantam ca trayam ajījanat — in her he fathered the two gods Aśvins and Revanta, the three;
retasaḥ ante — at the end of the semen;
revanta-nāma-niruktiḥ — the derivation of the name Revanta
Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

vājirūpadharasso'tha tasyāṃ devāvathāśvinau | janayāmāsa revantaṃ retaso'nte ca bhāskaraḥ ||7||

Śrīdhara

tataścātinirbandhaṃ pṛṣṭayā tayā nāhaṃ sañjñā kintu tasyāśchāyeti ākhyāte vivasvān sūryaḥ samādhinottarakuruṣvaraṇye aśvāṃ vaḍavārūpāṃ tapasi sthitāṃ sañjñāṃ dadṛśe dadarśa । tataḥ so'pi vivasvānaśvarūpadharastasyāmaśvinau devau revantañceti trayamajījanat । retaso'nta iti revantanāmaniruktiḥ ||7||

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

ततश्चातिनिर्बन्धं पृष्टया तया नाहं सञ्ज्ञा किन्तु तस्याश्छायेति आख्याते विवस्वान् सूर्यः समाधिनोत्तरकुरुष्वरण्ये अश्वां वडवारूपां तपसि स्थितां सञ्ज्ञां ददृशे ददर्श । ततः सोऽपि विवस्वानश्वरूपधरस्तस्यामश्विनौ देवौ रेवन्तञ्चेति त्रयमजीजनत् । रेतसोऽन्त इति रेवन्तनामनिरुक्तिः ॥७॥

Śrīdhara commentary.

After she was pressed with questions and said, “I am not Saṃjñā but her shadow,” Vivasvān the Sun saw Saṃjñā through meditation, in Uttarakuru, in a mare’s form, practising austerity. Then he too took a horse’s form and begot in her the two Aśvins and Revanta; “at the end of the semen” explains the name Revanta.
