VP 2.4.1

क्षारोदेन यथा द्वीपो जम्बू-सञ्ज्ञोऽभिचेष्टितः।
संवेष्ट्य क्षारम् उदधिं प्लक्ष-द्वीपस् तथा स्थितः

Now, Paraasharar starts to explain to Maitreya about all the other dveepaas which are there around Jamboo Dveepa. Just like the island which is called Jamboo Dveepa is surrounded by the salt ocean, surrounding the salty ocean all around is the Plaksha Dveepa.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

śrīparāśara uvāca kṣārodena yathā dvīpo jambūsaṃjño'bhiveṣṭitaḥ — Now; saṃveṣṭya kṣāramudadhiṃ plakṣadvīpastathā sthitaḥ — Paraasharar starts to explain to Maitreya about all the other dveepaas which are there around Jamboo Dveepa. Just like the island which is called Jamboo Dveepa is surrounded by the salt ocean, surrounding the salty ocean all around is the Plaksha Dveepa.

Śrīdhara

VP 2.4.4

सुखोदयस् तथानन्दः शिवः क्षेमक एव च।
ध्रुवश् च सप्तमस् तेषां प्लक्षद्वीपेश्वरा हि ते

and the Dwípa was divided amongst them, and each division was named after the prince to whom it was subject.

Word-for-word
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

sukhodayastathānandaḥ śivaḥ kṣemaka eva ca — and the Dwípa was divided amongst them, and each division was named after the prince to whom it was subject.; dhruvaśca saptamasteṣāṃ plakṣadvīpeśvarā hi te — and the Dwípa was divided amongst them, and each division was named after the prince to whom it was subject.

Śrīdhara

atra ca jambūdvīpaḥ lavaṇābdhiḥ ca vistārataḥ lakṣa-yojana-pramāṇau — here Jambūdvīpa and the salt ocean are each one hundred thousand yojanas in extent; etam uttarottaram dvīpa-abdhi-yugmam — each successive island-and-ocean pair; pūrvasmāt dvitīya-abdhi-yugmāt dvi-guṇam draṣṭavyam — is to be understood as twice the preceding pair; plakṣādi-dvīpa-pañcake — in the five islands beginning with Plakṣa; saptasaptabhiḥ maryādā-giribhiḥ bhaktāni — divided by seven boundary mountains; ubhayataḥ samudra-sparśāni sapta sapta varṣāṇi — seven regions on each side reaching the ocean; puṣkara-dvīpe tu madhyataḥ mānasottareṇa vibhakte dve varṣe — in Puṣkara-dvīpa, Manasottara divides the middle into two regions; sarvatra varṣādhipati-saṃjñābhiḥ eva varṣāṇāṃ saṃjñā — everywhere, the regions are named after their rulers; śaila-nadyādayaḥ sva-sva-saṃjñābhiḥ eva nirdiṣṭāḥ — mountains, rivers, and the rest are designated by their own names; plakṣadvīpeśvarāḥ pūrvādi-varṣa-krameṇa — the rulers of Plakṣa-dvīpa in the order of its regions

Transliteration
Viṣṇu Purāṇa

sukhodayastathānandaḥ śivaḥ kṣemaka eva ca | dhruvaśca saptamasteṣāṃ plakṣadvīpeśvarā hi te ||4||

atra ca jambūdvīpo lavaṇābdhiśca vistārato lakṣayojanapramāṇau । etamuttarottaraṃ dvīpābdhiyugmaṃ pūrvasmād dvitīyābdhiyugmād dviguṇaṃ draṣṭavyam । plakṣādidvīpapañcake ca dvīpavistārapramāṇairubhavataḥ samudrasparśibhiḥ saptasaptabhirmaryādāgiribhirbhaktānyubhayataḥ samudrasparśāni sapta sapta varṣāṇi । puṣkaradvīpe tu madhyato balayākāreṇa mānasottareṇa vibhakte dve varṣe । sarvatra varṣādhipatisañjñābhireva varṣāṇāṃ sañjñā । śailanadyādayaśca sva-sva-sañjñābhireva sarvatra spaṣṭaṃ nirdiṣṭāḥ । tad evaṃ sthite tatra kiñcid vyākhyāyate plakṣadvīpeśvarāḥ pūrvādivarṣakrameṇa

Śrīdhara Sanskrit

अत्र च जम्बूद्वीपो लवणाब्धिश्च विस्तारतो लक्षयोजनप्रमाणौ । एतमुत्तरोत्तरं द्वीपाब्धियुग्मं पूर्वस्माद् द्वितीयाब्धियुग्माद् द्विगुणं द्रष्टव्यम् । प्लक्षादिद्वीपपञ्चके च द्वीपविस्तारप्रमाणैरुभवतः समुद्रस्पर्शिभिः सप्तसप्तभिर्मर्यादागिरिभिर्भक्तान्युभयतः समुद्रस्पर्शानि सप्त सप्त वर्षाणि । पुष्करद्वीपे तु मध्यतो बलयाकारेण मानसोत्तरेण विभक्ते द्वे वर्षे । सर्वत्र वर्षाधिपतिसञ्ज्ञाभिरेव वर्षाणां सञ्ज्ञा । शैलनद्यादयश्च स्व-स्व-सञ्ज्ञाभिरेव सर्वत्र स्पष्टं निर्दिष्टाः । तद् एवं स्थिते तत्र किञ्चिद् व्याख्यायते प्लक्षद्वीपेश्वराः पूर्वादिवर्षक्रमेण

Śrīdhara commentary.

Jambūdvīpa and the salt ocean are each one hundred thousand yojanas in extent. Each successive island-and-ocean pair is twice the preceding pair. In the five islands beginning with Plakṣa, seven regions on each side are divided by seven boundary mountains that reach the oceans; in Puṣkara, Manasottara divides the island into two regions in a circular ring. Regions take their names from their kings, and mountains and rivers are designated by their own names. The account now explains the rulers of Plakṣa in the order of its regions.
