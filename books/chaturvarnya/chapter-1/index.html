Birth-based varṇa

Varṇa and jāti

According to the lexicon, the word varṇa is established in the sense of the jāti of the twice-born and others, and also in the sense of colours such as white. The word jāti, however, is grounded in birth, as the species of a lion and other animals are grounded in birth.

The Puruṣa hymn

For this reason, the mantra in the Puruṣa hymn is said to explain the arrangement of the varṇas by birth:

ब्राह्मणोऽस्य मुखमासीत् बाहू राजन्यः कृतः ।
ऊरू तदस्य यद्वैश्यः पद्भ्यां शूद्रोऽजायत ॥
“The Brāhmaṇa was His mouth; His arms became the Rājanya. What was His thighs became the Vaiśya; from His feet the Śūdra was born.”

That is, the Brāhmaṇa was the mouth of the Lord in His vast cosmic form, meaning that he was born from His mouth. The Rājanya was made His arms because he was born from His arms. The Vaiśya was His thighs, and the Śūdra was born from His feet.

An objection may be raised. In the expression “the Brāhmaṇa was His mouth,” the identification might be only figurative, with the mouth merely imagined as the Brāhmaṇa. The same could then be said of the Rājanya and the others. The author rejects this reading. Since the mantra concludes, “From His feet the Śūdra was born,” it establishes birth from the feet; the Brāhmaṇa and the others, too, should therefore be understood as born from their corresponding limbs.

This grammatical apposition is not defective. Expressions such as “clay pot” and “golden earring” likewise place an effect alongside the material from which it is produced. The author also cites Śaunaka and the Taittirīya Śruti, reading their descriptions of being born and fashioned as supporting birth-origin for the varṇas.

Manu’s statement

Manu likewise describes the four varṇas as brought forth from the corresponding limbs:

“For the increase of the worlds, He brought forth the Brāhmaṇa, Kṣatriya, Vaiśya, and Śūdra from His mouth, arms, thighs, and feet.”

Kullūka Bhaṭṭa explains that they were created in that order from the mouth and the other limbs. The author says that this agrees with the familiar principle that jāti is grounded in birth, just as the jāti of a lion is grounded in birth.

Guṇa, karma, and birth

An objection is raised from the Bhagavad Gītā:

चातुर्वर्ण्यं मया सृष्टं गुणकर्मविभागशः ।
तस्य कर्तारमपि मां विद्ध्यकर्तारमव्ययम् ॥
“The fourfold varṇa order was created by Me according to the division of guṇa and karma. Know Me, though its creator, as the immutable non-agent.”

The author answers that guṇa and karma are named as causes in the creation of the fourfold varṇa order. He compares them to the implements used to make a pot. After the pot has been made, the potter’s stick does not maintain it; rather, the stick and other implements existed beforehand and contributed to its production. In the same way, he argues, guṇa and karma are causes of obtaining bodies associated with the different varṇas. The verse does not establish that qualities and actions acquired after birth continually determine a person’s varṇa.
